= RFD 4 Public PoC release charter

== Goal

Deliver a publicly accessible browser proof of concept that shows one reviewed
Japanese mission to Tang China through the complete first-chapter exploration
loop. The owner has set public deployment as the PoC goal: implementation is now
planned, while creating provider accounts, purchasing anything, and performing
the external deployment remain separately authorized actions. Meeting this
charter does not by itself promote the experiment stack to a final production
decision.

== Relationship to RFDs 1–3

link:../0001/README.adoc[RFD 1], link:../0002/README.adoc[RFD 2], and
link:../0003/README.adoc[RFD 3] remain the owners of product interaction,
historical meaning and evidence, and browser architecture. This charter changes
none of their decisions. It records that their implementation is now planned and
defines the release-level criteria the implementation must satisfy. Historical
contracts and behavior fixtures remain renderer-independent, as RFD 2 and RFD 3
require.

=== Decision: implementation is planned; external deployment stays gated

RFDs 1–3 said their decisions did not launch implementation or deployment. That
timing statement is superseded: with public deployment set as the PoC goal,
implementation of the approved Phoenix + Svelte/TypeScript + Vite experiment
with Cesium evaluated first is planned under this charter, and installing that
experiment stack is part of that implementation. What is not superseded:
PostGIS, the historical-content database migration, and any external account,
purchase, domain, or deployment still require explicit later authorization.
This charter alone authorizes no account creation, spend, or deploy action.

== Definition: publicly accessible PoC

A publicly accessible PoC is a production release build of the Lekishi browser
application, served from a stable public HTTPS URL, that a reviewer can open
without special instructions and use to explore one reviewed Japanese mission to
Tang China through the first-chapter loop: globe-to-local navigation, selection
and details, sources, search, timeline, and playback. It demonstrates the
product direction and evidence quality; it is not a claim of complete historical
coverage, production hardening, or a final stack decision.

It is not publicly accessible if it requires the Vite development server, a
private network, or per-reviewer setup such as a personal token. A private or
credential-gated preview may support internal review, but it does not satisfy
the public-route criterion.

== Acceptance criteria

Each criterion is verifiable on the deployed release build and on the agreed
devices. link:IMPLEMENTATION.adoc[RFD 4's implementation checklist] records the
verification evidence; the criteria are referenced, not restated, in the RFD 1–3
checklists, which own the work inside each stage.

=== AC1 Public HTTPS route

* The release is reachable at a stable public HTTPS URL; HTTP redirects to HTTPS
  with a valid certificate and no mixed-content or certificate warnings on a
  current desktop browser or the physical iPad.
* A reviewer needs only the documented URL, without authentication, personal
  tokens, or per-reviewer setup, to open the launch chapter.
* A documented health endpoint responds successfully over the public URL.

=== AC2 Reviewed launch content

* The launch chapter is one source-checked Japanese mission to Tang China with
  selected travel, encounter, and learning/adaptation claims, bounded by RFD 1
  and reviewed under RFD 2.
* Every published event, place, actor, and relationship carries citations, and
  the owner's editorial approval is recorded for the published revision
  (self-review permitted, not described as independent specialist review).
* Uncertainty, approximate locations, disputed accounts, and coverage limits
  are visible; attested claims and cited scholarly interpretations are
  distinguished; no unsupported or original-hypothesis claim is published.

=== AC3 Required exploration loop

* A reviewer can move from the globe to the chapter's local area, select an
  event, read its story, sources, participants, and separate nearby/connected
  lists, and follow connections.
* Search, layer controls, timeline/playback, and a non-map event list are
  present.
* Playback advances a playhead within a fixed exploration window and stops at
  its end; chronological stepping stays separate from explicit "Next in story"
  jumps.
* Bounded uncertain occurrences appear as visibly uncertain possible matches;
  wholly undated events stay discoverable outside time-filtered results; no
  exact date or duration is invented.
* A selection excluded by the time window keeps its details with an
  outside-selected-time notice and an explicit return action, without retaining
  its map marker.
* Manual pan or zoom stops camera following without pausing playback; following
  stays off until explicitly resumed at the current playback position.

=== AC4 Production-built assets

* The deployed application serves Vite-built JS/CSS and the selected renderer's
  production resources — Cesium workers, styles, runtime resources, and models
  if Cesium is selected — from the release artifact; the Vite development server
  is neither running nor required.
* Static assets are digested and served through the production cache manifest,
  and the existing Phoenix pages still work.
* A documented build command produces the deployed artifact from a clean
  checkout.

=== AC5 Device scope

* The loop passes on desktop/laptop browsers with mouse and keyboard and on a
  physical iPad-class tablet with touch.
* The phone layout is usable for navigation, selection, and details without
  claiming full exploration parity.
* The device/browser matrix and measurable performance thresholds are agreed
  and recorded before the comparative measurements; acceptance meets them and
  records remaining limitations. Generated images and desktop viewport or touch
  emulation are not accepted as tablet evidence.

=== AC6 Accessibility

* Keyboard-only exploration reaches search, timeline, selection, details, and
  story navigation.
* Touch controls work on the tablet; reduced-motion preference is respected;
  uncertainty and relationship cues do not depend on color alone; the event
  list is readable and usable without the map.

=== AC7 Attribution and rights

* Sources, providers, media, and datasets carry their required attribution in
  the release, with licenses and reuse terms recorded.
* No imagery or substantial text is reproduced without suitable rights;
  generated illustrations are labelled as design references, never as evidence.
* Map, imagery, terrain, and model provider terms are satisfied; offline use is
  not promised unless the rights review permits it.

=== AC8 CI, health, error visibility, and recovery

* CI passes on the commit being released: formatting, linting, compilation with
  warnings as errors, tests, and a production asset build.
* The deployed release exposes a health endpoint and records application errors
  where the owner can review them.
* Deployment steps are documented and repeatable, and a rollback or recovery
  path is documented and exercised: redeploy the previous release artifact or
  restore the last known good content revision.

== Non-goals

The PoC does not imply or require:

* PostGIS. Displaying points, routes, or GeoJSON does not by itself require it;
  adopt it only for a separately demonstrated spatial-query need.
* A historical-content database migration. Versioned, schema-validated content
  files remain the first chapter's authority; move to PostgreSQL only when a
  demonstrated editing or query need justifies it.
* Native applications. RFD 3's native evaluation remains a separate later
  checkpoint after the browser loop; no native shell, Flutter, or platform
  client is a PoC deliverable.
* Broad application- or database-backed contributor and editorial tooling.
  Owner-led review under RFD 2, with file-based revision metadata and
  publication rules, still applies from the first chapter; permissions,
  editorial transitions, and background jobs wait for demonstrated needs.
* Offline maps. Provider caching rights are reviewed before any offline
  promise, and offline behavior is not part of PoC acceptance.
* Multiple public launch chapters. Qin and other examples remain bounded
  comparison fixtures, not additional launch chapters.

== Owner decisions that remain later gates

These decisions are not resolved by this charter and do not block this
charter's acceptance:

* The exact mission, claims, and coverage of the launch chapter, following
  RFD 2's source-feasibility pass. The owner selected Candidate B and accepted
  its coverage model on 2026-09-24; the decision and its bounds are in
  link:GATE1_DECISION.adoc[the Gate 1 decision record]. The owner's editorial
  approval of the published fixture revision remains open under AC2.
* Any choice that materially affects historical meaning or the user experience,
  including the local-detail treatment selected at Gate 3.
* Paid providers, budget, hosting vendor, and domain.
* Representative physical test hardware and measurable performance thresholds.
* Explicit authorization to create accounts, purchase services, and perform the
  external deployment.

== Dependency order and gates

The checklist sequences the work; the RFD 1–3 checklists own the tasks inside
each stage.

[source,text]
----
Step 0  Release charter (this RFD) and RFD checklist alignment
Wave 1  Source feasibility (RFD 2) | Data/time contracts (RFD 2) |
        Technical foundation (RFD 3) | Delivery evaluation (RFD 3)
Gate 1  Foundations frozen: owner reviews findings, selects the mission,
        and accepts the delivery approach
Wave 2  Reviewed Tang fixture | Bounded Qin fixture |
        Deployment packaging (RFD 3) | Shared comparison harness and baseline
Gate 2  Common runnable baseline: comparable content, controls, time behavior
Compare Geographic/symbolic and illustrated-local treatments across Tang and Qin
Gate 3  Local treatment selected and the experiment renderer resolved: Cesium
        confirmed, or the bounded MapLibre comparison completed and reviewed
        because Cesium failed a named requirement
Then    Complete the browser exploration loop
        Hardening, integration, browser verification, and editorial approval
        Physical-iPad acceptance
        Public launch after explicit owner authorization
----

Wave 1 runs in parallel, and Wave 2 runs in parallel after Gate 1. The treatment
comparison must not start before Gate 2, or it would compare unequal features or
unsupported content instead of the treatments. The fixtures and the shared
baseline are prerequisites for both treatments, not deliverables of either.

  release?
