= RFD 2 several stories in one chapter

== Provenance

link:../0001/README.adoc[RFD 1] decided to widen the Tang chapter to its
network. Around the 750–754 embassy, the chapter adds three things: the dated
missions to Tang of 630–894 as a sparse network layer, 鑒眞's five failed
crossings of 743–748 as a second story, and 圓仁's 838–847 diary as a third.
A chapter manifest holds exactly one `story` today
(link:AUTHORING_FORMAT.adoc[the authoring format], "Decided: the chapter
manifest").

On 2026-10-21 the owner chose between two ways to add the crossings. One was a
new chapter manifest. The other was several stories in the Tang manifest. The
owner chose several stories, which is the reading RFD 1 gives. This record
proposes how that works.

On 2026-10-22 the owner decided to build it as proposed. The sections below
keep their "Proposed" headings as the record of what was decided; what the
build changed from them is under "Implemented on 2026-10-22".

== What stays as it is

* *Records.* The world-first tree is unchanged. The crossing records are
  added with this record as ordinary records that no chapter reaches yet: 33
  events, 28 places and 13 actors under `historical/*/tang/`.
  An event can stand in several stories. For example, the envoys' 753 visit
  to 延光寺 (`yangzhou-invitation-753`) is the embassy's turning point, and it
  is also the coda of the crossings, where the envoys say they know of his
  five attempts.
* *One approval per chapter.* RFD 2 decided on one owner approval per
  chapter, and that stands. The digest already covers everything a manifest
  reaches, so several stories only make the closure larger. The digest
  algorithm (`lekishi-chapter-digest/2`) does not change.
* *Journeys.* They are their own kind, decided by the owner on 2026-10-21
  (link:AUTHORING_FORMAT.adoc[the authoring format record], "Decided: journeys
  are their own kind"); the journey record's shape is still proposed there. A
  story steps through events and relationships, and a journey draws the legs
  between the events. The two are independent.

== Proposed: the manifest

`story:` stays valid as the form for a single story, so the Qin manifest and
its approval do not change. A chapter with more than one story writes
`stories:` instead, and the first story is the one the chapter opens on.

[source,yaml]
----
title: The embassy to Tang China
summary: …
window: 750..754                  # the first story's opening window
stories:
  - key: tang-mission-750-754     # the existing story; its id stays lks:story:tang-mission-750-754
    title: The embassy of 750–754
    summary: …
    span: 750..779
    coverage: …
    steps: [ … ]                  # unchanged
  - key: jianzhen-crossings-742-753
    title: 鑒眞's five attempts to cross, 742–753
    summary: …
    window: 743..748              # optional; defaults to the span
    span: 742..753
    coverage: …
    notes: …
    steps:
      - daming-invitation-742: 榮叡 and 普照 invite 鑒眞 at 大明寺, 742
      - langgou-wreck-743: The ship breaks at 狼溝浦, 743
      # …
      - yangzhou-invitation-753: "Coda — 753: the envoys know of his five attempts"
----

* *Keys.* A story's `key` is its id (`lks:story:<key>`). Keys are unique
  across the project, like every other id. The first story keeps its current
  id, so nothing published under it moves.
* *Titles.* A story has its own `title`, because the switcher needs a name
  shorter than the summary. The chapter's `title` and `summary` stay the card
  text.
* *Windows.* The chapter's `window` is the window the chapter opens on. Each
  story may give its own `window`, which defaults to its `span`, and switching
  stories moves to it. A story at 838–847 is then no stranger than the coda
  nodes of 779 are today. The projection's coda rule, which today fails a
  step wholly outside the chapter's `window` unless its caption starts
  "Coda — " (`src/lib/atlas/projection.ts`), is checked against each step's
  own story window. Without that change, every crossing step before 750
  would fail the build.
* *Captions.* Today every published event needs a caption, and only a story
  step supplies one. An event listed in two stories may carry a different
  caption in each. Event `title`, which the authoring format decided but has
  not built, would make the caption an override, as decided. This proposal
  does not need it.
* *Limits.* The schema allows 200 steps per story. It now also allows up to 8
  stories per chapter, which leaves room for RFD 1's three stories plus the
  embassy.

== Proposed: what the compiler emits

* One v0 story record per story, with the steps, span and coverage as today.
  v0 needs no change: a publication set can publish several story records.
* A list of the chapter's stories (key, title, window, span) on the compiled
  chapter entry, so the reader can build the switcher without reading v0.
* `pnpm approvals` shows the changes story by story. A diff that adds a story
  reads as one block, so it isn't a long list of `steps[n]` lines.

== Proposed: the reader

* *Address.* `?story=<key>` joins `?event=`, `?place=`, `?step=` and
  `?from=`/`?to=` (link:../0005/README.adoc[RFD 5]'s query parameters on the
  chapter URL). It is left out for the first story, so every address that
  exists today still means what it means today. `?step=` counts within the
  story named.
* *Switcher.* The chapter panel shows the stories as a short list (title and
  span) above the step list. It is one control built from the design system's
  primitives and works at 320 px. Picking a story sets the window, resets the
  step and writes the address with `pushState`.
* *Map.* The map draws the places and journeys of the active story. Places
  that other stories in the chapter reach stay hidden until an era view
  exists. This keeps the 743 coast and the 838 Shandong coast from crowding
  the 753 landfall.
* *Event links.* Search and an `?event=` link open the story that lists the
  event: the active story if it does, otherwise the first story that does.
  The search index records an event's stories, so a result can say where it
  will open.
* *Memory.* The remembered position (`$lib/atlas/memory.ts`) gains the story
  key, so returning to the chapter reopens the story the reader left. This
  stays the one thing the browser keeps.

== What it costs

* Every new story makes the approved Tang chapter stale until the owner
  approves again. Under the owner's decision of 2026-10-21 (link:AUTHORING_FORMAT.adoc[the
  authoring format record], "Decided: a missing approval never fails a
  build"), the build does not fail, but production leaves the whole chapter
  out, page, index and title, until then; it does not fall back to the last
  approved version. Previews and signed-in members of the `lekishi`
  organization (link:../0006/README.adoc[RFD 6]) can still open the changed
  draft. Adding one story can therefore take the Tang chapter off the public
  site until the owner approves the larger closure.
* Three threads are adding stories to one manifest. The manifest change should
  land once, carrying all the stories that are ready, so the owner approves
  one diff rather than three.
* The chapter bundle grows. At the 200+ events RFD 1 targets, it is still one
  file per chapter. Splitting a bundle per story can wait until a measurement
  calls for it.

== Alternatives

A chapter per story::
  Needs no code change and leaves the approved chapter alone. It departs from
  RFD 1 and puts three more cards on the home page. The owner did not choose it
  on 2026-10-21.

An approval per story::
  Would let a new story ship without re-approving the embassy. It breaks the
  decided rule of one approval per chapter, and one story's change could
  then reach a shared place another story uses without re-opening that
  story's approval. Not proposed.

A story by query (all events with a `part_of`)::
  Weighed and rejected for the single story on 2026-10-18. That reasoning
  holds for each story here.

== Implemented on 2026-10-22

Built on the Tang branch (PR #27) as proposed, in this order: the schema and
the compiler, the projection one story at a time, the switcher and
`?story=`, search across stories, and then the crossings as the Tang
chapter's second story. Where the build differs from the text above:

* *The crossings' window is `742..750`, not `743..748`.* The story opens on
  the 742 invitation, which is a step of the story rather than a coda, and
  it closes with the return north to 揚州 by 750. The return is a 750–753
  occurrence window, which overlaps the story's window, so it isn't a coda
  either. Only the 753 invitation is, as the example shows. The story's
  title follows the window: "鑒眞's attempts to cross, 742–750".
* *The chapter keeps its title.* "The embassy to Tang China" stays on the
  card, and the summary gains one sentence naming the second story.
  Retitling it is the owner's call (see "Open questions").
* *The embassy's tests read the first story.* The Tang chapter's coverage
  model (the 750–754 window, the coda years, the excluded outbound voyage
  of 752) is checked over the embassy's own events. The crossings' window
  and their one coda year are checked on their own
  (`src/lib/historical/contract/tang-chapter.test.ts`).
* *The remembered position needed no change.* It stores the chapter's
  address, and the address carries `?story=`.

The Tang approval is stale until the owner approves the larger closure.
Production leaves the chapter out until then. It was already stale on `main`
before this change, because other chapters changed places it shares.

== Open questions

* *The chapter's name and id.* `tang-mission-750-754` and "The embassy to Tang
  China" no longer describe a chapter that runs from 630 to 894. Proposed: keep
  the id for now, because addresses and approvals hang on it, and retitle the
  card when the second story lands. If the id changes later, it changes with
  a redirect in `static/_redirects`.
* *Which stories land first.* Proposed: the crossings, then the missions
  network, then 圓仁, each as soon as its records pass the contract. Any
  story that is ready at the same time goes in the same manifest change.
