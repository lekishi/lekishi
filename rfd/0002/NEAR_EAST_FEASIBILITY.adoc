This note covers four draft chapters written in one pass for the owner's
request of 2026-10-21: as many chapters and events before 100 CE as
possible, to test the data format and the engine. It records, for each
chapter, which sources were reachable and usable under the source register's
commercial-use rule, what the sources let the records say about dates and
places, and where the contract could not hold what the sources say.

Nothing here is published atlas content. The four manifests are drafts with
no approval block; none has been approved, and approval is the owner's step,
in each manifest (link:AUTHORING_FORMAT.adoc[the authoring format record]).
Until then production leaves the chapters out and names them in no public
file; previews show them under the draft banner, and signed-in members of
the `lekishi` organization may open them (link:../0006/README.adoc[RFD 6]).

== Dating

The chapters were picked to stress dating, and they do.

* *Regnal years with no Babylonian calendar.* The prism numbers campaigns,
  the Chronicle and the biblical books give regnal years, months and days,
  and Hammurabi's years are named, not numbered. The contract's calendars are
  julian, gregorian, islamic, chinese_lunisolar, regnal and unknown. Every
  date keeps its day or year-name in `as_written` and takes its BCE year from
  a cited conversion, at year precision.
* *No conversion from a regnal year with a cited epoch.* Only one source in
  the four chapters converts a single regnal year: the Livius note putting
  Sargon's eleventh year in 2323 BCE. Every other year-named or year-numbered
  event carries the window of its reign (1792–1750 for Hammurabi, 2334–2279
  for Sargon) or of the cited bounds (587–561 for the third deportation).
  The two reign events themselves are durations, activity across the whole
  span, while the events inside them are windows, one occurrence somewhere
  in it.
* *Competing chronologies.* Hammurabi's reign is dated 1792–1750 (Louvre),
  2130–2088, 2121–2066, 1990–1950 and from 2342, 2056 or 2041 (Catholic
  Encyclopedia, 1910). Sargon's is 2334–2279 (middle chronology), about 2650
  (King 1910), about 3800 (King and Hall, after Nabonidus) and about 1800
  (Smith 1880). The contract can hold one date value per record, so the
  other datings are alternative claims whose numbers live only in their
  `says` text, carried once on a reign event.
* *Disputed days and years inside one source tradition.* The temple burned on
  the 7th (2 Kings 25:8) or the 10th (Jeremiah 52:12) of the fifth month;
  Jehoiachin was released on the 27th or the 25th; Jerusalem was taken in
  Nebuchadnezzar's 7th year (Chronicle, Jeremiah 52:28) or his 8th (2 Kings
  24:12). These are conflicting claims on the date, with a dispute.
* *587 against 586.* The fall of Jerusalem is a window `587 BCE..586 BCE`
  with a dispute, and every event that follows it inherits the window.
* *"About".* The Louvre's "around 1750" and King's "about 2650" cannot be
  written in the date notation, although v0 has `endpoint_certainty`.

== Placement

* Of 54 place records, 40 take a Pleiades point. Three take a display
  anchor only, because the chapter has no cited identification: Akzib (at
  Ekdippa), Tahpanhes (at Daphnai) and Ebla (Sargon's Ibla, at Tell Mardikh).
* Eleven are `unknown`: Banai-barqa, Azuru, Eltekeh, Libnah, Azekah, Agade,
  Malgium, Emutbal, Iarmuti, Kazallu and Subartu. The plains of Jericho are
  carried by the town's point, with a note.
* The Cedar Forest has two candidate positions, the Amanus and the Lebanon,
  as King weighs them in 1918, with a dispute on the place. It is the first
  place in the tree with two positions. Checked on 2026-10-21: the atlas's
  projection keeps only the first position (the Amanus) and does not carry
  the place's dispute, so the first position's note names the Lebanon as the
  other candidate, which is all a reader sees of it.
* A start or end the sources do not name is a `context` place, never a
  `start` or `end`, because the atlas places an event at its first located
  non-context place: Babylon on Nebuchadnezzar's march west and on the third
  deportation, Agade on Sargon's march, and the city of Ashur for the
  King's Company, whose letter names the country.
* Hammurabi's prologue gives one event 21 context places. It is a test of a
  claim of dominion drawn as places, not as a territory.
* No route and no territory is drawn in any of the four chapters: the
  sources give stops (Sargon's march, Zedekiah's flight, the deportations)
  but no stages, and no extent.

== Disputes, legend and identity

* Miraculous and legendary accounts are claims about what a text says, never
  events: the angel of the LORD and Herodotus' mice (701), Marduk's
  punishment of Sargon, and the Legend of Sargon's basket of rushes.
* Sargon's identity is a dispute on the actor: King and Hall (1906) take the
  Shargani-shar-ali of the inscriptions for Sargon, King (1910) argues the
  traditional Sargon is Shar-Gani-sharri under an older king's name, and King
  (1918) quotes Sargon's own inscriptions as Sharru-kin's. The chapter
  follows 1918.
* Overtaken scholarship is cited as its author's, with the year: King and
  Hall's reading of a Hammurabi letter as proof that Assyria was a Babylonian
  province, and every pre-1920 chronology.

== Findings for the engine and the data format

Proposals only; none is built.

. *A `tradition` (or `legend`) evidence state.* Legendary and miraculous
  accounts are now `conflicting` claims, which overstates them: they do not
  contest the event, they explain it. A separate state would let the reader
  show them as tradition.
. *Competing chronologies as structured sibling dates.* An alternative claim
  could carry its own date value, so the viewer can switch a chapter between
  the middle chronology and others, or show the spread.
. *A regnal calendar with a cited epoch.* "Year 30 of Hammurabi" plus a cited
  accession year under a named chronology would give a one-year window
  instead of the whole reign.
. *A Babylonian lunisolar calendar*, so day dates like Addaru 2, year 7, can
  be stored as dates, not only as text.
. *"About" in the date notation*, mapping to v0's `endpoint_certainty`.
. *Hebrew script in the fonts*, so names can be given in Hebrew; for now they
  are Latin-script forms.
. *Alternative positions for one place*, so a place with two cited
  candidates (the Cedar Forest) shows both, with its dispute, rather than
  the first.
. *Media for the Lachish reliefs and the Hammurabi stele*, once media
  records exist; both are central images and both are held by museums whose
  terms need reading.
