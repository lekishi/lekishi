= RFD 3 campaign board: the globe drawn as a strategy map

== Provenance and scope

On 2026-11-12 the owner judged the `/dev/world` prototype (the B1 "campaign
map" direction) and asked that the atlas's globe be drawn the same way,
replacing the close-up diorama of 秋妻屋浦, which still appeared when a reader
opened a place from the prototype in its chapter: "I think we can just replace
the whole earth visualization in the civilization style."

This record covers the chapter atlas's globe only. The home page's globe of
chapters (`ChapterGlobe.svelte`) is unchanged. No historical content,
publication set, approval or contract version changed.

== Decision

* *The chapter globe is a campaign board.* The terrain is cut into hexes. A
  hex is lit where an event stands at the playhead or a journey's vessel
  stands, remembered where the story's events are over, and dark elsewhere.
  Explored land is raised by its relief. The scene derives the board
  (`buildScene` in `src/lib/atlas/scene.ts`); the renderer draws it
  (`src/lib/atlas/renderer/globe/board.ts`).
* *Towns, units and banners are symbols of the story's presence.* A town stands
  on each place the window's story has reached by the playhead, a ship or a
  travelling party at each journey's vessel, and each place is a city banner
  carrying its event count and evidence mark. None claims a settlement's size,
  a force's strength, or a territory; the legend says so. A banner takes a
  controller's tint where a drawn snapshot records the place held, so the
  legend's "held by" colours stay true, and the story's gold otherwise.
* *The camera is tilted* 40° below the horizon, opening at 6,000 km and
  following at 1,000 km, as a strategy map is seen. Home includes the chapter
  between the rails; at 3,000 km, 平城宮 fell behind the detail panel after
  returning from 益救島. The reader still turns and zooms freely.
* *The diorama is removed.* Its renderer layer, its definition and its file
  (`/dioramas/akimeya.json`) are gone, with the build invariant and browser
  check that required them. This supersedes RFD 1's diorama register for the
  region and local scales (link:../0001/README.adoc[RFD 1], "The Tang chapter's hero, approved 2026-10-10");
  that record stays as written.
* *RFD 4's RC1 cannot be met as written.* link:../0004/README.adoc[RFD 4]'s
  RC1 asks that the served Tang chapter match its approved hero, "the
  diorama's terrain, symbols, vessel, and chrome at region scale". The
  diorama no longer exists, so RC1 needs the owner's amendment to name the
  campaign board instead. This record does not edit RFD 4.

== Measurements (2026-11-12, orb, software WebGL)

Measured in the orb's headless Chromium, which renders WebGL in software
(SwiftShader), on the Tang chapter at 1440×900, device scale 2. These are
software-renderer measurements, not device performance acceptance; frame
times on real devices remain unmeasured for this change.

[cols="3,1"]
|===
|Forced redraw, the same tilted view without the board |493 ms
|With the board, hexes classified per pixel in the tile shader (first cut) |1,041 ms
|With the board, hexes classified once on the CPU into a per-hex texture |731 ms
|A settled board, nothing to draw |0 ms (no frame drawn)
|===

The forced-redraw figures above are from the initial implementation thread,
before the optimizations below. The recovered change reproduced the picking
timeout in this orb. Against the built artifact, with the existing 120-second
limits unchanged:

[cols="3,1,1",options="header"]
|===
|Browser scenario |Recovered change |Optimized board
|Click a place's mark, choose its events, return home and pick 平城宮
|Timed out at 120 s |Passed, 1.3 min
|Keep a selection readable outside the time window
|Passed, 1.7 min |Passed, 21.4 s
|Idle while a tile is pending, then redraw its arrival
|Not present |Passed, 25.0 s
|Restore an idle WebGL context
|Not remeasured |Passed, 18.2 s
|===

* The tile shader first classified every pixel's hex from fourteen texture
  reads; the board now classifies each hex once when its imagery arrives and
  the shader reads one texel. Only tiles to level 3 are painted into the world
  image, so zooming in never reclassifies a hex and moves its banner.
* Tile-grid longitude and latitude are interpolated to the fragment shader,
  removing two per-pixel inverse trigonometric operations. The board also
  skips the imagery sample it would immediately paint over. Tile refinement,
  resolution, lighting and the plain globe's imagery remain unchanged.
* Pending requests no longer force identical WebGL redraws. Tile arrivals and
  retries invalidate the view, as do camera, board and overlay changes. The
  browser regression holds refinement requests, observes an idle globe, then
  releases them and observes new draws; context restoration is checked too.
* The board's reach texture (4 MB) is rewritten in place on each change; it
  is rebuilt only when the derived board changes.
* The board's code ships with every chapter page: about 40 KB of source, a
  2048×1024 canvas for the world image, and two textures (reach and classes).
* In the initial investigation, a settled page composited at about 15 frames a second
  whether or not the board is on: the banners' and panels' shadows composite
  in software. Removing them in the diagnostic held 60; that visual change
  was not retained.

== Known limits

* The terrain is today's imagery and relief, not the land of the chapter's
  period.
* Banners can overlap where places cluster; hexes stretch near the poles and
  meet at a seam at 180°.
* On a phone the arrival plate can cover the arrived place's banner until it is
  closed.
