= RFD 3 comparison review route decision

== Provenance and scope

The bounded Qin comparison fixture
(`historical/chapters/qin-conquest-han-233-226/`) was prepared and self-reviewed,
and the internal review that followed found source-fidelity and wording
corrections. The preparing editor may not approve content, so the corrections were
first prepared as draft revisions, and the fixture stayed unapproved. The owner's
instruction of 2026-09-25 was *"actually wait. wire it up first, and let me review
again before approval"*: the corrected content had to become inspectable in the
product, still unapproved, before any decision about it.

This record is the dated decision that makes that possible. It supersedes the
artifact rule of link:COMPARISON_FIXTURE_BOUNDARY.adoc[the comparison fixture
boundary decision] — the fixture now ships as a prerendered review route instead
of being kept out of the artifact — and it leaves that record's other decisions
intact: the caller-supplied coverage window, the one projection, the comparison
catalog as the browser-safe loader for non-production chapters, the approval gate
over every chapter tree, and the fixture's `preparation snapshot` status. It does
not supersede link:CONTENT_BOUNDARY.adoc[the chapter content boundary decision]:
the atlas route still binds only the reviewed Tang chapter, and the projection's
refusals are unchanged.

== Decision

* **The corrected revisions are what the fixture's preparation set publishes.**
  The single set `lks:set:qin-conquest-han-233-226-v1` names the twelve corrected
  revisions the internal review prepared, and each corrected record also keeps the
  revision it replaced as unpublished history. Nothing is flattened and no
  approval is recorded: the ledger row stays `preparation snapshot` with no owner
  decision, and its digest is the corrected preparation.
* **`/compare/qin` is a prerendered review route.** It loads the fixture through
  `src/lib/atlas/chapter-catalog.ts` with the fixture's own coverage model and
  mounts the same workspace the atlas route mounts, with
  `detail="comparison-review"`. The route's load means a fixture that cannot be
  projected fails `pnpm build` instead of rendering an empty page.
* **The review surface says what the content is.** The route header carries the
  badge `Unapproved preparation`, the prerendered shell states the review note
  ("no release decision exists for this content, it is not a launch chapter, and
  the atlas shows the corrected revisions the internal review prepared"), and the
  workspace's notice reads `Unapproved preparation · Self-review only` instead of
  `Reviewed chapter`. The wording lives in `src/lib/atlas/comparison.ts`, so the
  page, its metadata, and its tests cannot describe the fixture differently.
* **The workspace takes its chapter and its detail surface from its caller.**
  `AtlasWorkspace` now receives `chapter` and an optional `detail`; the atlas
  route passes the reviewed Tang chapter with the production surface, and the
  comparison route passes the fixture with the review surface. The production
  surface's rendering is unchanged — including its wording, its counts, and the
  absence of the review-only dispute presentation — so approving the fixture
  later cannot silently change the launch chapter.
* **BCE years are printed as BCE years.** `yearLabel` and `windowLabel` in
  `src/lib/atlas/labels.ts` turn the astronomical ordinal the domain computes with
  into the historical year (`-232` is `233 BCE`), and a CE year prints exactly as
  it did before, so the Tang chapter's labels do not change. The occurrence, the
  window, the playhead, and the notice use it; the window inputs keep the ordinal
  the domain edits and state the BCE year beside it.
* **The published dispute is shown on the review surface.** The projection carries
  a disputed record's summary and its conflicting or alternative claims, with
  their cited locators, into the atlas domain, and refuses a record whose
  challenging claims are not presented as a dispute. The review surface marks the
  event in the list and shows the summary and the accounts in the details panel.
  The production surface keeps its current presentation: visible uncertainty for
  the launch chapter is an open RFD 4 AC2 part, and changing it is the owner's
  decision, not this record's.
* **The artifact rule becomes a review-page rule.**
  `scripts/check-static-build.mjs` requires `build/compare/qin.html` to exist,
  to carry exactly one mount point and the client entry, to state
  `Unapproved preparation`, and to name the fixture and its 233–226 BCE window,
  and it requires the built scripts to carry the fixture while `atlas.html` does
  not. A fixture that stopped shipping, or one that leaked onto the production
  page, fails the build.
* **The development server serves the chapter tree.** The comparison catalog
  loads chapter JSON with lazy `import.meta.glob`, so in development those files
  are fetched over HTTP; `vite.config.ts` allows the workspace root for that
  (`server.fs.allow`), and the production build bundles the same files into the
  artifact and never reads them from disk at runtime.

== Alternatives considered

* **Keep the fixture out of the artifact and review it in a local-only harness.**
  Rejected: the owner asked to review the corrected content in the product, and a
  separate harness would be a second content path with its own rendering, which
  is exactly what the chapter boundary exists to prevent.
* **Render the corrected drafts without publishing them.** Rejected: the atlas
  reads published revisions, and a route that projected unpublished drafts would
  bypass the publication gate that keeps a set from existing without a decision.
  Publishing them in the existing `preparation snapshot` set keeps the decision
  class honest and the digest current.
* **Show the dispute presentation on the production surface too.** Rejected for
  now: it would change the approved launch chapter's rendered surface without an
  owner decision. The shared component takes the detail surface as a prop so the
  launch chapter can adopt it later.
* **Bundle the fixture eagerly into the production boundary.** Rejected: the
  atlas route stays bound to the reviewed Tang chapter, and the comparison route
  keeps the catalog as its only path.
* **Inline the fixture's records into the prerendered HTML.** Not needed: the
  shell is prerendered from the load, so the page states the fixture's window and
  status before any script runs, and the records ship in the route's own chunks.

=== Update — 2026-09-26: the details panel's bound, after the owner's review comment

The owner's review comment on the served page asked why 南陽 has no sourced
position. The answer was already in the record — the entries state no extent or
seat, and the historical geography records the name for two regions — but at the
owner's 820x634 viewport the details panel rendered past the bottom of the
workspace, where nothing could scroll to it, so the explanation was the text that
fell off. Two shared-surface fixes, no content change:

* the details panel is now bounded by the overlay column (which already stops
  above the time controls) and scrolls inside that bound, instead of taking a
  fixed `100dvh` height and spilling past the viewport;
* `e2e/comparison.spec.ts` gains a check that the panel stays inside the viewport
  and clear of the time controls at 1440x900, 820x634, 390x844 and 320x640, and
  that scrolling it reaches the record's own explanation. The overlay-geometry
  check now covers those four sizes too, and the atlas phone-layout check's
  budget was raised to 240 s for the software renderer.

Re-measured after the change: `pnpm verify` passes (format, `svelte-check` 0
errors and 0 warnings, 462 tests: 461 passed and 1 skipped, static build 451
files); `e2e/comparison.spec.ts` 15 passed in 14.3 minutes across the desktop,
tablet-touch, and phone projects, and `e2e/atlas.spec.ts -g "phone layout"`
passes in 1.6 minutes.

== Consequences

* The owner can review the corrected fixture in the product at `/compare/qin`,
  with the atlas's own BCE labels and the published dispute, while the ledger
  still records no decision on the content.
* The fixture now ships in the artifact. That is a deliberate, reviewed change:
  it is unapproved content reachable at a URL, clearly labelled as such, and not
  linked from the production surfaces.
* The comparison harness has a first piece. Its remaining work — the shared
  baseline, the measured treatment comparison, and any additional comparison
  chapters — builds on this route rather than on a second rendering path.
* The workspace's overlay column now derives its bottom offset from the time
  controls' measured height, so a chapter whose labels are taller (a BCE window)
  cannot push the event list over the controls.

== Limitations recorded

* The content is **not approved**. The ledger row is a `preparation snapshot`,
  the set records the preparing editor's `self_review` only, the optional
  specialist reviews remain unperformed, and the locators still rest on
  unproofread Wikisource transcriptions that a critical edition must settle
  before any content freeze.
* The review route is not a launch chapter and not a release: RFD 4's AC2 does
  not apply to the fixture, and the treatment comparison does not start before
  Gate 2.
* The production surface does not show disputes yet, so the launch chapter's
  visible-uncertainty part of AC2 stays open.
* Every map position the fixture shows is a label anchor or display placement,
  not a historical coordinate: the anchors mark modern places that carry the
  names, and no site, extent, or seat is asserted. A gazetteer identification
  would replace an anchor with an asserted placement.
* The BCE labels are the atlas's own year text, not a calendar conversion: the
  contract's own `source_expression` remains the record's date as written, and
  the atlas does not translate between calendars.
* Shipping the fixture means the artifact carries unreleased preparation
  content. If the owner decides against the fixture, the route and the fixture's
  membership in the artifact are removed together, and the ledger row is retired
  with them.
