== Method and limits

* Every text quoted is a public-domain work read in a Project Gutenberg or
  Wikisource transcription, or on LacusCurtius. Following the
  link:../../docs/SOURCE_REGISTER.adoc[source register]'s rule, the records
  quote the work, cite the edition, and name the transcription.
* Coordinates come from Pleiades (CC BY 3.0, the dump of 2026-10-21) or, where
  Pleiades had no fitting record, from Wikidata (CC0). Each is published as
  the gazetteer's identification (`basis: interpretation`), never as a
  position the ancient text states.
* Places that no source read here locates are published with
  `interpretation: unknown` and no point, so nothing is drawn through them.
* Wikimedia hosts rate-limited this environment for most of the pass (HTTP
  429), and archive.org is blocked by the network policy. Breasted's volumes
  II and III could not be downloaded, so the Megiddo chapter reads the annals
  in the Wikisource transcription of Breasted's text, and Punt uses Maspero in
  their place.
* A source that reports an event is evidence of the report, not proof of the
  event. Royal inscriptions (the annals, the Kadesh poem, the Punt reliefs) are
  labelled as such in each event's notes.

[[chronology]]
== The chronology problem, common to the four Bronze Age chapters

The sources date by regnal year and the Egyptian civil calendar ("year 23,
first month of the third season, day 21"). Converting that to a BCE year
depends on which chronology of the New Kingdom is used, and the high and low
chronologies differ by about 25 years. None of the sources read gives a
chronology that is still accepted: Breasted (1906) and Garstang (1910) are
close to the middle chronology, and Sayce (1890) is a century off.

So every BCE date in the four chapters is an *editorial window* spanning the
high to the low placement of the regnal year in question, with the source's
own regnal date kept in `as_written`. Each event's notes say this, and that a
cited chronology must replace the window before approval. Two things would
fix it:

. a cited modern chronology (for example a handbook table) adopted as a source,
  so the windows can be narrowed and cited; and
. a decision on the calendar. The contract's calendar list has no Egyptian civil
  calendar, so these dates use `regnal`. An `egyptian_civil` value would let a
  date say which calendar its day and month are in. That is a proposed
  contract change for the owner to decide, not something the drafts assume.

The contract does not compare dates below the year, so the day-by-day order of
the Megiddo campaign is carried by the step order, not by the dates.

== Hatshepsut's expedition to Punt

What the sources support:: The episodes of the Deir el-Bahari reliefs as Maspero
retells them: Amun's command at Karnak, five galleys under a royal envoy, the
landing by a village on piles, the meeting with Parihu and Ati, the barter and
its goods list, the Ilim on the way home, the return festival, the incense
garden and the carving of the reliefs.
What they do not:: A regnal year (Maspero gives none, only that the queen
reigned at least eight years after), the port of departure, the route, or the
commander's name. Maspero says the port and the course are unrecorded; the
route in `punt-fleet-sails` is his reconstruction and is labelled so.
Places:: Deir el-Bahari and the Red Sea from Pleiades. Punt and the Ilim are
unlocated. Punt carries a `dispute` with the four views Maspero reports
(Somali coast, Arabia, both shores, Suakin to Massawa). Maspero's proposed
landing, the Elephant River near Ras el-Fil, is a separate place shown as a
display anchor at Pleiades' Elephas Promontorium.
Next sources:: Breasted, _Ancient Records_ II (the Punt reliefs) for the captions
and the commander's name; Naville's _Deir el Bahari_ (1894–1908) for the
plates. Both are public domain. The finds at Mersa Gawasis need a modern,
cited source before they can enter.

Journey stops, for when journeys exist (link:https://github.com/lekishi/lekishi/pull/26[PR #26]):
Karnak (the command), an unknown port, the Red Sea, the Ilim, Punt (the
landing), the Ilim, Thebes (the temple landing), Deir el-Bahari. Only the
first, the Red Sea and the last two are placed.

== Thutmose III at Megiddo

What the sources support:: The annals' day-by-day first campaign: Tharu in year
22, Gaza on the coronation feast, Yehem and the council of war, the Aruna
pass, the camp at the brook of Kina, the battle on the new-moon feast, the
siege wall, the submission, the booty lists and the goods of Yenoam, Nuges
and Herenkeru.
What they do not:: The length of the siege (other inscriptions give seven
months; none is read yet), the fate of the chief of Kadesh, or a BCE date
(<<chronology>>).
Places:: Tharu, Gaza, Kadesh, Karnak and Thebes from Pleiades; Sharuhen,
Taanach and Megiddo from Wikidata. Sharuhen's identification is disputed
(Tell el-Far'ah South or Tell el-Ajjul) and is kept at locality precision.
Yeraza, Yehem, Aruna, Zefti, the brook of Kina, Yenoam, Nuges and Herenkeru
are unlocated, so the pass, which is the point of the story, cannot yet be
drawn.
Next sources:: the Gebel Barkal and Armant stelae (Breasted II), and a public
domain topographical study of the Aruna road.

Journey stops: Tharu (year 22, IV Peret 25), Gaza (year 23, I Shemu 4–5),
Yehem (I Shemu 16), Aruna (I Shemu 19), the brook of Kina, Megiddo (I Shemu
21). Every stop is dated to the day by the annals; four of six are unplaced.

== The Amarna letters

What the sources support:: Niebuhr's summaries of the letters of
Kadashman-Enlil I, Burnaburiash II, Tushratta, the king of Alashiya and
Ashur-uballit I, and of the vassals: Aziru, Rib-Hadda of Byblos, Abdi-Heba of
Jerusalem, Milkilu, Tagi, Abi-Milku of Tyre, Itakama of Kadesh, Akizzi of
Qatna, Yabitiri. One docket gives a date (a restored year 32 of Amenhotep
III); the rest are undated. The records cite Niebuhr without quotation: the
translator Jane Hutchison's dates are unknown, so the English is not shown to
be public domain outside the US.
What they do not:: Tablet numbers (Niebuhr predates Knudtzon's EA numbering), a
date for any vassal letter, or which king most vassal letters went to. The
records put a letter under Akhenaten only where the text makes it so.
Places:: Akhetaten, Tyre, Simyra, Qatna and Joppa from Pleiades, Ugarit from
Wikidata. Byblos, Sidon, Jerusalem, Nineveh and Ashkelon are shared with the
Sennacherib chapter (copied byte for byte from that branch). Hinnatuni is
unlocated. Babylon, Assur and Washukanni are left to the Mesopotamian
chapters; Alashiya is an actor only, since its identification with Cyprus
postdates Niebuhr and no source read states it.
Identifications of their time:: Niebuhr equates the Habiri with the Hebrews and
puts Alashiya in Cilicia. Both records say these are his, not settled.
Next sources:: Conder's translations in _Egyptian Literature_ (1901, Wikisource,
blocked during this pass) and Knudtzon's _Die El-Amarna-Tafeln_ (1907–1915,
public domain) for tablet numbers and the letters Niebuhr only lists (Gezer,
Lachish, Hazor, Arzawa).

Journey stops: the statue of Ishtar, Nineveh to Thebes; Aziru summoned to
Egypt (unplaced); the Babylonian caravan robbed at Hinnatuni (unplaced).
The chapter is a network of letters, not a route; it is the test case for
showing correspondence between places rather than travel.

== Kadesh and the treaty with Hatti

What the sources support:: Sayce's retelling of the Egyptian account of Kadesh in
year 5 (the false spies, the ambush, the king alone, the second day),
Brugsch's translation of the treaty of year 21 (date, place, envoys, silver
tablet, clauses, the Storm-god seal), the year 8 campaigns and the marriage
of year 34; and Garstang's reading of the first Boghazköy tablets (the
Hittite losses, the Amurru revolt, the succession, Puduhepa's seal, the
queens' letters and the letters to Babylonia).
Disputes kept:: the outcome of the battle (the poem's victory against Sayce's
"check" and Garstang's "indecisive"), and whether the poem's Hittite overtures
belong to year 5 or year 21 (Müller against Garstang).
Licence:: Garstang died in 1956, so his book is public domain in the US but
protected in life-plus-70 countries until 2027-01-01. It is recorded with
`quotation_permitted: false`, and every Garstang cite is a citation without a
quote. It can be re-reviewed after that date.
What they do not:: A modern account of the succession (Urhi-Teshub), a modern
view of whether Hattusili travelled to Egypt, or the Hittite version of the
treaty in its own words. The notes say where an early reading is likely
superseded but assert no modern view without a source.
Places:: Kadesh, Aleppo, the Orontes, Pi-Ramesses, Hattusa, Abydos, the
Ramesseum and Abu Simbel from Pleiades. The Lake of Homs, Dapur and Tunip are
unlocated.
Next sources:: Breasted's _Battle of Kadesh_ (1903) and _Ancient Records_ III;
Langdon and Gardiner's 1920 translation of the Hittite treaty text.

Journey stops: the Hittite embassy, Hattusa to Pi-Ramesses, year 21; the
Egyptian embassy to Hattusa; the princess's journey to Egypt in year 34
(unplaced at the Egyptian end).

== Rome and Kush

What the sources support:: The Kushite attack on Syene, Elephantine and Philae;
Petronius's counter-campaign to Pselchis, Premnis and Napata; the garrison at
Premnis and the Candace's attack on it; the envoys at Samos and the terms;
Nero's exploration (Pliny's praetorians and their itinerary, Seneca's two
centurions at the marshes, Dio's spies). Strabo's and Dio's accounts of the
terms differ and are kept as a dispute; Pliny's, Seneca's and Dio's accounts of
Nero's mission are kept as separate accounts.
What they do not:: Exact years for the war (the sources give consular years and
the windows follow them), or the Meroitic side, which no public domain source
read gives.
Places:: Syene, Elephantine, Philae, Pselchis, Premnis, Napata, Meroë and Hiera
Sycaminos from Pleiades. Samos is shared with the Xerxes chapter. Pliny's
stages Tama, Acina, Pittara, Tergedus and Gagaudes, and the marshes, are
unlocated.
Next sources:: the Meroitic stelae (no public domain translation known) and the
Hamadab stela.

Journey stops: Petronius, Syene, Pselchis, Premnis, Napata (25–22 BCE, all
placed). Nero's praetorians, Syene, Hiera Sycaminos, Tama, Acina, Pittara,
Tergedus, Meroë (54–68, Nero's reign; three of seven placed). The centurions,
Meroë to the marshes (unplaced).
