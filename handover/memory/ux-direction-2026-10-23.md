On 2026-10-23, answering the UI/UX audit ([[ui-ux-audit-2026-10-23]]), Darwin named the problems in his own words:

1. "No exploration on the atlas."
2. Chapters are valid but belong on a separate, better organised page, filterable by region or culture. If the globe explores horizontally within an era, the site also needs vertical timelines, and chapters may be the place for them.
3. "IA is straight up bad. The point of this project is to aggregate historical information to show their correlation and relationships, I don't get any of those."
4. "Nothing makes it feel like civilization at all. No 3D animated figures, terrain don't zoom, and obviously no buildings and such when zoomed in most."
5. "Just sprinkling data points on the map is boring, and boring is not what I'm trying to achieve here."

**Why:** the atlas was built chapter-first and evidence-first; Darwin wants a world you explore, with relationships visible across chapters and a Civilization-register scene (diorama, figures, buildings, zooming terrain), as approved in the 2026-10-10 hero.

**How to apply:** judge any UI proposal against "does this show correlation and relationships" and "is this boring". Separate the chapters index from the atlas home; make the globe the exploration surface; treat the Civ look (diorama tier, units on journeys, settlements at attested sites, terrain LOD everywhere) as product requirements, not polish. The audit's recommendations were accepted as a diagnosis ("the recommendations look good").
