This note records what the draft chapter
link:../../historical/chapters/qin-unification-230-221.yaml[`qin-unification-230-221`]
is built from, how its places are positioned, where its sources disagree, and
how far the sources carry the territory the owner asked for. It deepens the
approved Han chapter
link:../../historical/chapters/qin-conquest-han-233-226.yaml[`qin-conquest-han-233-226`]
from Han alone to all six states, 230–221 BCE.

The chapter is a draft. Only the owner approves it, with `pnpm approvals`.

== Finding

The episode is well served by public-domain primary text in accessible
transcriptions. 史記 gives each event twice or more: in the annals of the first
emperor (卷六), in the six-states table (卷十五), and in each state's hereditary
house or a general's biography. 資治通鑑 卷六–七 gives a third, later
narrative. Every event, place and relationship cites at least one of them with
a locator and a short quotation, and every quotation was checked against the
transcription text on 2026-10-21.

The sources are enough for a dated, cited chapter whose story steps through 39 events and 11 relationships. They are not
enough for drawn borders or routes: they name cities, commanderies and passes,
not lines.

Separate source records keep the approved chapters fresh. `shiji`,
`zizhi-tongjian` and `dushi-fangyu-jiyao` belong to the approved Han chapter,
and editing them to add juan would make it stale. The chapter still cites
`shiji` (卷六 and 卷十五) and `zizhi-tongjian` (卷六) where the approved records
already cover the juan.

Before publication each locator should be rechecked against a critical edition
(the 中華書局 punctuated 史記 and 資治通鑑), because the transcriptions are
unproofread.

== Placement

[[placement]]
No place carries an asserted historical coordinate. Each of the 20 positioned
places has a `display_anchor` at the modern place Wikidata records (P625), cited
to the item revision, with its identification cited to 讀史方輿紀要 where that
work identifies it. Capitals and cities are anchored at the modern city
(邯鄲, 薊, 大梁, 臨淄, 陳, 咸陽 and others); commanderies and regions at their
modern namesake, labelled region-level (遼東, 代, 上谷, 太原, 會稽 and others).

Seven places stay without a position, because the sources name them and state no
site: 霸上, 衍水, 上郡, 鄢郢, 寢, the Zhao land of 東陽, and 淮南. 鄢郢 carries
胡三省's reading as an attributed interpretation, not as a position.

No route is drawn: the sources name a pass (井陘), a river crossing (易水) and
the ends of marches, not roads.

== Disputes

[[disputes]]
The chapter publishes one reading for each and keeps the others as
`conflicting` claims.

* *The end of Zhao.* The biographies end it with king 遷's capture in 228 BCE;
  the six-states table writes 秦滅趙 at the Dai king's capture in 222 BCE. The
  hereditary house puts the surrender in 229.
* *太子丹's death, 226 BCE.* The annals have Qin taking his head; the
  hereditary house of Yan and 刺客列傳 have his father send it.
* *The Wei king's fate, 225 BCE.* Captured, or surrendered and killed (資治通鑑).
* *李信's colleague, 225 BCE.* 蒙恬 in the biography, 蒙武 elsewhere.
* *王翦's recall*, dated 225 or 224 BCE.
* *項燕's end.* Killed in battle in 224 (the table, the hereditary house,
  資治通鑑) or a suicide in 223 (the annals).
* *The Chu king's capture.* 223 BCE in three sources, 224 in the annals.
* *The 36 commanderies.* 221 BCE in the annals, 220 in the table; and the
  weapons melted into bronze figures, likewise.

== Territory

The owner chose on 2026-10-21 to show territory as control by place, with no
borders; the proposed record is in link:TERRITORIES.adoc[Territories as control
by place]. The sources support exactly that: which named cities and
commanderies Qin took, and when. Read from the chapter's records:

|===

|228 BCE |Zhao |邯鄲 and the Zhao land of 東陽; the prince 嘉 holds 代, allied with Yan at 上谷
|226 BCE |Yan |薊; the Yan king withdraws to 遼東
|226 BCE |Chu |Ten cities, unnamed
|225 BCE |Wei |大梁 and all of Wei's land; 安陵 refuses an exchange and stays outside
|224 BCE |Chu |From 陳 south to 平輿
|223 BCE |Chu |The Chu king captured; the table writes 秦滅楚
|222 BCE |Yan, Dai |遼東; 代
|222 BCE |Chu |The lands south of the 江, made the 會稽 commandery
|221 BCE |Qi |臨淄 and Qi; the Qi king removed to 共
|===

Borders drawn as areas would need a source this pass did not find on usable
terms. OpenHistoricalMap has no Warring States polygons. 譚其驤's
中國歷史地圖集 is in copyright. The historical-basemaps dataset is GPL and too
coarse for a ten-year episode.
