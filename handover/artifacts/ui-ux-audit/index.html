Lekishi · 2026-10-23

What the atlas feels like with 39 chapters

An opinionated walk through the site as it stands on main, compared with the hero Darwin approved on 2026-10-10, with the bugs it turned up and the questions that decide what to build next.

Build local vite dev at 27f5ac3 (after #64 re-approved every chapter) Walked with Playwright, Chromium on a software GPU Frames 1440×900 · 820×1180 · 390×844 Content 39 chapters, 1 with two stories, about 1,100 story steps

The verdict

Lekishi today is a careful viewer for one chapter at a time. It is not yet an atlas. Two chapters hid that; thirty-nine make it plain.

Everything the reader meets is organised around the chapter: the home page is a catalogue of chapters, the switcher is the same catalogue in a dialog, search groups by chapter, and once inside a chapter the globe shows only that chapter's places. There is no screen on which the reader sees the world at a time, no way to stand at 長安 and see who passed through it across six centuries, and no path from Hannibal to Rome under the kings except knowing both exist. The product's own tagline, "follow embassies, voyages and conquests across a globe", promises the second thing and delivers the first.

Inside a chapter the bones are good: the frame holds, the URL contract works, the card reads narrative first, the story walks. But the map is not yet worth looking at, which RFD 4 made the exit for Track 1. At region zoom it is a blurred green and tan wash with dashed straight lines, and the words that qualify it (the legend, the "schematic, follows the dated stops" caption printed on the globe, the "label anchor, no site asserted" badges, the placement notes) take more of the screen than the history does. "Show, then qualify" has drifted back toward "qualify".

The playback is a time-lapse, not a story. At four years a second the whole Tang window passes in a second and nothing happens on screen while it does: no card opens, no camera moves, no caption changes. The hero's "circular next-event dial" is there, but what it drives is empty.

None of this is a polish problem. It is a shape problem: the chapter is the only unit of navigation, and the globe is a backdrop rather than the thing you navigate. The second half of this page is the questions that decide which shape you actually want.

1 · Against the hero

RFD 1 says the hero is the acceptance bar and the served page is judged beside it. Here is that comparison for the three scales, at the same place and date.

The chrome follows the hero's pattern faithfully (date bar, orbs, journal, pennants, dial, minimap). What is missing is everything that made the hero look like somewhere: the painted ground, the names on the water, the landscape symbols, the ship on its path. The frame is finished and the picture inside it is not.

2 · Finding a chapter

Blocking The time ribbon is unreadable at 39 chapters

The ribbon was designed for two chapters ("a reader sees how far apart the two stories are"). It is linear, labels every chapter, and takes its span from the widest window. One chapter with a 21,000-year window makes it a smear, and even without it, 38 chapters between 2334 BCE and 779 CE would need lanes, grouping or zoom to read.

Recommendation

Replace it, don't tune it. The ribbon should become the primary way in: a horizontal era strip with lanes by region, bars for windows, labels only at the zoom where they fit, and a non-linear scale (log before 1000 BCE, or a separate "deep time" band for the Americas and Lapita). Hovering a bar should light its mark on the globe and vice versa. At 39 chapters this is a time atlas, not a decoration.

Major The chapter list is a 6,800 px wall in alphabetical order

Every card is the same card: a window, a Cinzel title, four lines of summary, a gold button. They are sorted by file name, so "The campaigns of Alexander" is followed by "The Amarna letters" and 班超, and the Tang embassy (the chapter the whole project was built around) is 31st. Nothing says where a chapter is on Earth, what kind of story it is (a voyage, a war, a diary, a survey), how long it is (6 steps or 107), or which chapters belong together (the four Tang stories, the three Qin chapters, the three Egypt chapters, the Greek pair). The three "principles" at the bottom and the footer's "Members sign in" are the only things that are not a card.

Recommendation

Home should be the world and the time, not the list. One screen: the globe (or a flat painted map on small screens) with the chapters as marks, the era strip under it, and a search field. The list moves below the fold and gets structure: grouped by region or era, each card with a tiny locator, its length in steps, its kind, and a hero image when one exists. Order by time, not by file name.

Major Search ranks a sentence match over a name match

Results are grouped by chapter, the current chapter first, and within a chapter a name match beats a text match. Across chapters that order means the first hit on the screen is whichever chapter sorts first, not whichever record matches best. The second screenshot shows the deeper problem: the atlas has no idea that the 長安 of 張騫, 玄奘 and 杜環 is one city. Each chapter owns its own copy, so there is nothing to land on.

Recommendation

Rank globally: exact name and alias matches first across all chapters, then chapter titles, then text; group by chapter only after ranking. Longer term, give places and people an identity across chapters (same record id or a shared Wikidata item), so a search for 長安 lands on one place with its events from every chapter. That is also what makes section 3 possible.

3 · Moving between chapters

Major The only door between chapters is the catalogue

From inside a chapter there is no "around this time", no "also at this place", no "next chapter in this thread", no neighbouring chapters shown faintly on the globe. The 杜環 chapter starts the year the Tang embassy ends and both touch 長安; the reader can only know that by reading both summaries. Stephen II's journey and the Tang embassy happen in the same year on opposite ends of Eurasia; the site never says so. The Qin trilogy and the Egypt trilogy are three disconnected cards each.

Recommendation

Make the globe the switcher. Inside a chapter, draw the other chapters' home marks dimmed at the current playhead's century; a click opens them. Add to the card and to the About sheet a "Near this" list: chapters within a few hundred km and a century of the selection. Let a place card list its events from every chapter. Then the switcher dialog becomes a fallback, not the route.

4 · Inside a chapter

Major The window moves under the reader, and the plate tells them every time

Every step link recentres a five-year window on the step, so the window label changes on each step, the strip's bottom row rescales, and the arrival plate announces a widening that the reader did not ask for and cannot see. RFD 1 decided on "a fixed exploration window with a moving playhead" for exactly this reason. The plate also covers the middle of the scene at the moment the camera lands, on every frame size.

Recommendation

Keep the chapter window fixed while walking the story; widen only for a deep link that is genuinely outside it (the codas). Show the plate only then, and move it to the date bar as a one-line notice rather than a plate over the scene.

Major The legend lives in the scene

On desktop the legend is open by default, 288 px wide, with four badges, three sentences of honesty plate and the data attribution, and it sits in the scene's clear zone where the camera frames the selection. In every desktop screenshot on this page it occupies the bottom-left quarter of the map. The hero puts the same information in one 11 px line along the foot of the screen.

Recommendation

Fold the legend by default on desktop too, and make it a key, not an essay: the marks that are on screen, as glyphs with one word each, in one row at the foot of the scene. The attribution goes to the About sheet and the footer. The "what is real here" sentence belongs on the diorama, once, when it exists.

Minor The left rail is a list, three ways

Story, Events and Sources are three vertical lists in the same plate. The story list is the journal, which works. The events list is the story again in date order without the captions. The sources list is bibliographic metadata ("Wikisource digital transcription of the Taishō Tripiṭaka text (T.2089, 遊方記抄), unproofread; locators re-checked against it on 2026-09-24") that reads like a maintainer's note. The people pennants above the rail filter the events list, but the medallions are initials, so a reader of Vesuvius sees "C C P" and a reader of Hannibal sees "H Q M P M".

5 · The globe

Blocking At region scale there is nothing to look at

The high-resolution terrain exists only over Satsuma; everywhere else the globe serves the L5 global tiles, which look like a blurred satellite image at any region zoom. The painted relief from the hero (hypsometric tints, shaded relief, strong coastlines, rivers, muted sea with its name) was the thing that made the region screen read as a map. None of it shipped. Nothing is labelled except the chapter's own places, so a reader cannot orient: no seas, no modern coast names, no rivers, no mountain ranges.

Recommendation

This is Track 1's exit and it is still open. In order: (1) a painted relief basemap at the region level everywhere, built once from the same open data as the Satsuma tiles; (2) a base label layer of seas, rivers and ranges with historical names where the records have them; (3) label declutter by priority (selected, then count), which the atlas mock already specified; (4) journeys as curved or coast-following schematic lines with the caption in the legend, not on the water. The diorama tier can come after.

Major The opening view is often wrong for the chapter

The home view is the centre of the bounding box of the chapter's places and the camera height is whatever fits them, so chapters with far-flung places (Paul, Alexander, the Americas) open on a globe where the story is a cluster of discs, and chapters that cross the antimeridian open on the wrong side of the world. A chapter has no authored opening: no hero view, no first sentence, no place to start other than step 1 in the list.

6 · Story and playback

Blocking Play does nothing a reader can follow

The dial's play button advances the playhead at four years a second. For Vesuvius (79–80) that is a quarter of a second; for Tang (750–754) about a second; for the early Americas, an hour and a half. While it runs, events light up on the strip and markers pulse on the globe, but no card opens, no caption appears, the camera does not move unless "Follow" is pressed, and the journal does not advance. The result is that the one control that looks like "tell me the story" produces a flicker and stops.

The story, meanwhile, is the numbered list in the left rail and the "Next in story" button at the foot of the card. That works, but it is a slide deck: a list of 38 captions and a next button. The hero's journal showed the current step and the next, with the rest folded; the hero's dial was "next event", not a speed control.

Recommendation

Split the two ideas. Story mode is a guided tour: each step opens its card, flies the camera, moves the playhead to the step's date and holds until the reader presses next (or an optional auto-advance of a few seconds a step). Time mode is the scrubber: drag the playhead and watch the window's events and territories appear, with the speed scaled to the window's length so every chapter takes about the same time to play through. The dial should drive story mode; the strip should drive time mode.

7 · Cards and evidence

Major The qualification outweighs the claim

The card's order is right (narrative, quotation, place, date, people, connected, nearby, sources). What is wrong is the weight: every event with a label-anchor place carries a grey box of three or four lines about what the anchor is not; every uncertain span carries "One occurrence somewhere inside this span; the atlas does not treat the span as continuous activity"; every identified locality carries "identified, size is precision". A reader who opens six cards reads the same paragraph six times. RFD 1 asked for these to be states in the visual grammar with the text one click away.

Recommendation

One badge per state, the note behind a disclosure ("Why this placement"), and the dispute box collapsed to its first line with the two readings as a two-row table. Drop the digest and set id from About; keep the decision line and the counts. Give the modern name a visible value and a label ("today: 坊津").

8 · Phone and tablet

The phone frame is mechanically sound and the best-behaved of the three; the sheet's three detents do what RFD 5 said. What it lacks is the same as desktop: a map worth looking at under the sheet, and a story that moves. The handheld tier from the hero has not started, and on a 390 px screen the 3D globe at region zoom is a green blur behind a sheet. The tablet frame hides the journal entirely until a reader finds the list orb, which is the hero's weakest screen in practice.

10 · What I would change

In the order I would do it, assuming the answers in section 11 point roughly where I think they do.

Within a week

  1. Fix the antimeridian centre, the pennant initials, the "Modern name" label, the step-widening plate, and search ranking.
  2. Fold the legend on desktop and shrink it to a key; move the attribution out of the scene.
  3. Scale playback speed to the window, and open the card and fly the camera when the playhead crosses an event.
  4. Replace the ribbon with an era strip that can hold 39 bars: lanes, a deep-time band, labels on hover.

The next step that changes the product

  1. Home as the world. The globe (flat painted map below 1024 px) with chapter marks, the era strip, search. The list below, grouped and ordered by time, with a locator and a kind per card.
  2. Story mode. A guided tour per chapter: step, card, camera, date, hold. The journal shows the current step and the next, not the whole list. This is what "play" should mean.
  3. The map worth looking at. Painted relief everywhere, base labels (seas, rivers, ranges), label declutter, journeys drawn as paths with the caption in the key. The Track 1 exit.
  4. Qualify quietly. One badge per state; notes behind a disclosure; the dispute as two rows; About without the digest.

After that

  1. Chapters that know each other. Dimmed neighbour marks on the globe at the playhead's century; "near this" on the card; one place record across chapters so 長安 is one place.
  2. The diorama and the handheld tier, once the region map is right, because both inherit its ground.

11 · Questions for Darwin

You said the experience is "not actually what I want in various ways". These are the forks where I cannot guess, and each one changes what gets built. Short answers are fine; "all of the above" is also an answer.

  • What is the unit the reader moves through?Today it is the chapter. Is it the world at a moment (scrub time, watch the globe change, dive into whatever is there), a thread (follow 鑒眞 across chapters), a place (stand at 長安 and see six centuries), or the chapter as now? The home page and the globe are built differently for each.
  • Is "play" a story or a clock?A narrated tour that opens cards and moves the camera step by step, or a time-lapse of the window where events light up? They need different controls and different speeds, and the dial can only mean one of them.
  • Does the hero still set the bar?The diorama, the painted region map and the handheld tier were approved on 2026-10-10 and none have shipped. If they still define done, Track 1 is the next work and the chapters pause. If the bar has moved, I would rather know what it is now than keep comparing against the old one.
  • How loud should the qualification be?The site currently puts every caveat in the reader's face: badges, grey boxes, legend sentences, captions on the water. Is one badge and a disclosure enough, or do you want the evidence state visible without a click for a scholarly reader?
  • Who is this for first?A curious reader who wants to be shown something, or a scholar who wants to check something? The first wants a tour and a beautiful map; the second wants search, entities and sources. The site is currently built for the second and sold to the first.
  • Should chapters stay the authoring unit but stop being the reading unit?Shared place and person records across chapters would let the site show one 長安, one 鑒眞 and a world view. That is a data decision (ids and approvals) before it is a UI one.
  • What does five minutes on the site look like when it is right?If you can describe three things you would do in those five minutes, in order, I can build the home and the chapter around them instead of around the records.
