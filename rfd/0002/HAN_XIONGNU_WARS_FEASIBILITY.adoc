== Finding

The wars are well served by public-domain primary text. 史記 tells them twice,
in the 匈奴列傳 (卷110) as a year-by-year sequence anchored on 元朔二年 and
元鼎三年, and in the 衛將軍驃騎列傳 (卷111) as the two generals' campaigns
with the court's citations. 漢書 卷六 武帝紀 dates the same campaigns in its
annals. Every claim cites one of these with a locator and a short quotation,
and every quotation was checked by script against the Wikisource
transcriptions on 2026-10-21.

The sources carry a dated, cited story of 17 events from the 馬邑 ambush to
the 漠北 campaign and the sacrifice at 狼居胥山. They do not carry routes or
borders: they name commanderies, mountains and the 單于's court, not lines.

== Placement

Display anchors sit at modern places of the same name or at the modern
county that holds the Han commandery's name, pinned to a Wikidata revision:
馬邑 (朔州), 河南地 (the Ordos plateau), 定襄 (和林格爾), 祁連山, 居延, 右北平 (寧城), 河西 (the corridor) and 隴西 (臨洮), plus `shanggu` and `dai` from the six-states chapter. None
asserts a historical site.

No position is asserted for 龍城, 朔方, 高闕, the 單于庭, 趙信城, 狼居胥山,
北地 and 焉支山. Wikidata has no coordinate for 焉支山, and the others are
named without a site. The map therefore cannot draw the 漠北 campaign: its
end points are unknown, which is the honest state.

== Disputes

`hexi-commanderies`:: 漢書's annals put the founding of 武威 and 酒泉 in
元狩二年 (121 BCE); the 匈奴列傳 places the commanderies after 渾邪王's
surrender in the reign of 烏維單于. Both are cited, the second as the
conflicting claim, and the record does not choose.

== Dates

Han reign years began in the tenth month until 104 BCE. A reign year is
given as the Julian year of its main part, and the source wording is kept in
`as_written`. Where the 匈奴列傳 gives only "the next year", the date is
derived from its anchor years and the record says so in its notes.

== What the chapter is good for

The chapter is a test case for a war told through many unplaced places. Over
half its stops cannot be drawn, so the map, the story list and the cards have
to cope with a chapter whose most important places are unknown.
