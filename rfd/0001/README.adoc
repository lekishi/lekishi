= RFD 1 Historical exploration and visual interactions

== Goal

Lekishi is a passion project for exploring what happened across the world and
how people, societies, and institutions interacted over time. It should be
engaging to explore, not merely a database of pins. Begin in the browser while
keeping native devices and native applications open as future delivery options.

== Established direction

Start with a globe and narrow continuously into regional and local exploration.
Use a maps-application layout with Civilization-inspired readable terrain,
symbolic settlements, and visible interactions. Globe and local views are scales
of one experience, not competing designs or unrelated modes.

War is one interaction among many. Trade, diplomacy, missions, learning,
religious transmission and division, institutional development, and local
adaptation matter equally. States, cultures, religions, and companies are not
interchangeable entities or mutually exclusive territorial categories.

The Civilization reference describes clarity, discovery, and the pleasure of
following interactions. It does not request a playable strategy simulation,
resource economy, alternate-history engine, or invented historical certainty.

== Proposed experience

=== Globe to region to event

At globe scale, display a sparse selection of events or clusters in a selected
time window. Reveal places, relief, routes, and individual events with zoom.
At closer scales, introduce symbolic landmarks without implying that the
landscape is an archaeological reconstruction. Preserve real geographic
positions and distances rather than arranging cities like board-game pieces.

Keep the time window and selection stable across zoom changes. A selected event
opens its story, participants, sources, nearby events, and connected events.
Nearby does not mean related; connected events can be geographically distant.
Following a link can move the camera, but should offer an explicit time change
when the destination falls outside the current window.

Prefer a full-screen map, floating search and layer controls, persistent time
controls, and a selection drawer. On smaller screens the drawer can become a
bottom sheet. A flat or low-tilt view remains an option for geographic reading.

Place labels lead with historical names and include their source-supported local
script alongside romanization or translation, following RFD 2's naming policy.
Use fonts with appropriate script coverage and keep paired labels readable on
desktop and tablet. Modern names and alternate spellings belong in details and
search rather than replacing the historical label.

=== Decision: retain details when time filtering excludes the selection

Changing the selected time window does not close the selected event's detail
panel. If the event no longer matches, keep its story and sources available,
visibly mark it as "outside selected time", and offer "Return to event's time".
The return action explicitly changes the time window; retaining the panel does
not silently undo the user's time change.

Exclude the event from ordinary map results and do not keep an out-of-window
marker merely because it is selected. The open panel preserves reading context,
not an exception to temporal filtering. For example, a selected event from
762 CE remains readable after moving the window to 700 CE, but its event marker
does not remain visible on that basis.

Apply RFD 2's possible-match policy before classifying an event as outside the
window: a bounded uncertain occurrence that overlaps still qualifies as a
visibly uncertain possible match. The return action must respect date precision
and uncertainty rather than invent an exact date for an uncertain event.

=== A visual language for different relationships

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Subject |Visual form |Meaning to preserve
|Campaigns and war |Directional campaign segments, battle markers, control snapshots |A schematic campaign is not an exact marching itinerary.
|Trade |Networks of ports, cities, and route segments |The Silk Road is not one timeless road; a network does not prove a particular shipment.
|Missions and learning |Selected journeys within wider networks, outbound and return stages |Travel, encounters, and subsequent adaptation are separate claims.
|Religion |Community markers and journeys linked to a relationship timeline |Spread is not territorial ownership; divisions develop over time.
|Companies and institutions |Selectable organizations, changing roles, places and relationships |The British East India Company's commerce, diplomacy, and coercion need distinct treatment.
|Diplomacy |Selected alliances, agreements, and dated links |Connections can change, overlap, or be disputed.
|===

Use symbols, line patterns, and labels as well as color. Reveal relevant
connections on selection or within a focused story layer rather than drawing
every relationship at once. Relationship timelines supplement the map when
geography alone cannot explain institutional or religious change.

=== Decision: compare stories on a shared map and timeline

Allow multiple stories to be selected as layers on one map, sharing the selected
time window and playhead. Identify each story's events and connections through
labels or other non-color-only cues. Showing events together establishes neither
contact nor causation; cross-story relationships require their own cited evidence.

Stories reference the same underlying records. An event belonging to multiple
selected stories remains one event with visible story memberships, not duplicate
historical occurrences. Keep relationship-type symbols meaningful while identifying
story membership. Contested interpretations still follow RFD 2's main-account and
cited-alternatives policy, not interchangeable story layers.

Side-by-side maps with independent cameras are deferred. This comparison behavior
does not expand the first public chapter into multiple full launch chapters;
small contrasting examples can verify it during implementation.

=== Playback and evidence

Offer scrubbing, play/pause, event stepping, speed control, and optional camera
following. Time resolution must fit the story: centuries for a broad network,
years for a campaign overview, and finer dates only where evidence supports
them. Exact playback and uncertainty contracts belong to link:../0002/README.adoc[RFD 2].

==== Decision: fixed exploration window with a moving playhead

Playback moves a playhead within the selected time window without moving or
expanding that window. The window filters the exploration context; the playhead
highlights the current moment within it. For example, playing a 700–800 CE
window retains that context while the playhead advances and stops at its end,
without automatically looping or continuing into a new window. A rolling-window
playback mode is deferred.

Distinguish contextual events from those supported as occurring at the playhead;
remaining visible in the window does not mean an event is currently active.
Keep uncertain occurrences visibly uncertain, following RFD 2, rather than
highlighting them as definite activity throughout their possible date range.
Playhead movement alone does not exclude the selected event from the window or
trigger the outside-selected-time notice.

==== Decision: chronological controls and separate story navigation

Time controls step through dated events chronologically by default. Curated
chapters provide a separate "Next in story" action for editorial sequences that
may revisit earlier context or jump ahead to consequences. Do not silently
replace chronological stepping with narrative order when entering a chapter.

Make story-related time jumps explicit, retaining the existing confirmation
behavior when the destination is outside the selected window. A position in a
story sequence describes presentation order, not evidence that one event preceded
or caused another. Where uncertain dates do not establish a chronological order,
preserve and communicate that ambiguity rather than fabricating an exact date
or presenting an arbitrary tie-break as known historical order.

==== Decision: manual camera control stops following, not playback

When the user manually pans or zooms during camera-follow playback, stop camera
following while time continues playing at the selected speed. Leave the camera
under the user's control and expose an explicit "Resume following" action.
Do not automatically return to following after a delay or at the next event.
Resuming follows the current playback position rather than rewinding time to
where the user left follow mode. Programmatic camera movement is not manual
input and must not itself cancel following.

==== Evidence and accessibility

Show supported boundary snapshots rather than smoothly inventing intermediate
front lines. Label illustrative motion and schematic routes. Selecting a link
must expose what is asserted and its sources, not leave an arrow to imply
causation. Do not assign numerical confidence percentages without a meaningful
methodology.

Keyboard navigation, touch controls, reduced-motion behavior, readable labels,
and a non-map event list should let users explore without relying on animation,
precise pointer input, or color discrimination.

== Candidate stories, not a committed launch dataset

* Qin's conquests of the rival Warring States: campaigns, battles, chronology,
  and political change. Do not equate the resulting domain with modern China.
* Alexander's campaigns: movement, selected milestones, and consequences.
* World War II: a later stress case for simultaneous theaters and dense dates,
  rather than the default first dataset.
* Silk Road networks and the British East India Company: changing commercial
  connections and organizations, not only military states.
* Japanese missions to Tang China: people, learning, return journeys, and local
  adaptation, situated within wider East Asian networks.
* Christianity's spread and divisions: communities, councils, contested
  reception, and changing relationships rather than a single clean split.

=== Decision: first public chapter

Start with Japanese missions to Tang China, bounded to one mission and selected
learning/adaptation links rather than the entire history of the missions. Choose
the specific mission, people, dates, and links after checking source availability
and reuse rights under RFD 2. This selects the chapter direction, not a researched
itinerary or an assertion that a journey caused a particular cultural change.

The first public chapter should make travel, encounters, and subsequent adaptation
explorable as distinct, sourced relationships. A small set of contrasting test
examples can exercise campaigns and institutional change without becoming
additional full launch chapters. The other stories above remain future candidates;
the precise content inventory and research budget remain open.

=== Decision: source feasibility before selecting the mission

Before selecting the exact Tang mission, run a bounded source-feasibility pass
over up to three missions in one initial desk-research pass. Use accessible
sources and references supplied by the owner. Return findings and gaps for review
before deeper research, purchases, or outreach; this pass authorizes none of those
follow-on activities. This bounds the initial scope without setting a numerical
time or spending budget. Compare reliable source availability, reuse rights, geographic
detail, and evidence for travel, encounters, and subsequent learning/adaptation
as separate claims. Reference books can inform the shortlist without being its
only evidence base.

The pass should recommend one mission with a concrete proposed inventory of
events, places, actors, and relationships, a source bibliography, coverage limits,
and remaining evidence or rights gaps. Identify where optional specialist review
would help. If no candidate is sufficiently supported within this initial pass, report
that outcome and propose a narrower scope rather than filling gaps speculatively
or continuing research without a bound.

This selects the research approach, not the mission or its historical claims.
The feasibility pass and implementation are now planned under
link:../0004/README.adoc[RFD 4]; the mission remains an owner decision after the
pass returns.

=== Decision: explicit coverage guidance and distinct empty states

Keep globe exploration available while clearly identifying the chapters, regions,
and periods included in the published content. Describe this as curated coverage,
not a claim that every event within those bounds has been documented.

Distinguish "not yet covered" from "no results with these filters" using the
declared content scope, not an empty query result alone. Offer available chapters
for uncovered areas and relevant filter adjustments for filtered results. Where
coverage is not established, say so rather than imply completeness. An empty map
never establishes that nothing happened there; missing historical knowledge and
missing atlas content are different limitations.

Provide this guidance without requiring a geographic coverage overlay for the
first release. A dedicated coverage overlay is deferred, not a prerequisite for
unrestricted globe exploration.

=== Decision: compare local-detail approaches through later implementation

Keep the local-detail choice open until runnable implementations can be compared.
Use separate implementation threads later, not additional image-generation
threads now. Evaluate two scenarios: the bounded Japanese mission to Tang China
and a bounded episode from Qin's conquest. Qin is a comparative prototype scenario,
not a replacement for the first public chapter or a second full launch chapter.
The specific Qin episode remains to be selected and source-checked.

For each scenario, compare both approaches:

* Geographic detail with symbolic landmarks: terrain, rivers, historical labels,
  selected sites, and route/event markers without city reconstruction.
* An illustrated local historical scene: restrained buildings and scenery that
  provide context, explicitly distinguished from researched reconstruction.

Keep scenario content, controls, selection, and time behavior comparable between
the two treatments so differences in features do not obscure the visual trade-off.
Compare globe-to-local continuity, readability, interaction, performance, asset
and research effort, and the risk of implying unsupported historical detail.
Generated images can inform styling but cannot settle implementation feasibility.
This comparative work is now planned under link:../0004/README.adoc[RFD 4] and
begins only after RFD 4's Gate 2; it still does not select either treatment as
the production default.

== Alternatives and trade-offs

A pin-only atlas is simpler but misses the central interaction experience.
A game simulation would be engaging but invent outcomes beyond the evidence.
A purely photorealistic globe risks modern-looking scenery and weak historical
readability; a heavily illustrated local world risks inaccurate scale and costly
reconstruction. Prefer geographic continuity with restrained symbolic detail.
