Lekishi · Identity · Round 2

The Meridian dial, refined

You picked the Meridian dial and asked for a wordmark. This round has three versions of the dial, a favicon for each, three wordmarks, and a bench where you can pair any of them. Every mark and wordmark here is outlined paths, so none of it waits on a font.

It leans 23.4°The globe and the dial tilt by the Earth's axial tilt, so the figure is the planet rather than a generic globe icon.
The playhead is on the axisGold sits where the tilted axis meets the dial: the year you are reading, straight above the globe's pole.
No longer a sunRound 1's loose ticks read as rays. The ticks are lighter now and hang from a ring or a band.
Small cuts drawn separatelyThe 24 and 32 px cut keeps one meridian and the near equator; the 16 px cut keeps one meridian on whole-pixel strokes.
The mark

Three dials

A1

Dial

The whole dial, leaning with the globe. Twelve hours, the playhead on the one where the axis points.

  • Closest to round 1.
  • Thin ring and short ticks; still the busiest at small sizes.
A2

Window

Only the span you are reading is drawn; the rest of the dial is a dotted path ahead. The playhead closes the window, the way the atlas's time window ends at the playhead.

  • Most specific to Lekishi: window plus playhead is the atlas's own time control.
  • Its small cut is a distinct C-shape that cannot be mistaken for a sun or a gear.
A3

Bezel

A solid dial band with the hours cut out of it, like a medallion or an astrolabe's rim.

  • The heaviest silhouette; strong on a social card.
  • Reads as a coin or a badge, and heavier than the rest of the hairline chrome.
16 and 32 px

Favicons

The favicon sits on a lacquer tile, because a cream mark disappears on a light browser's tab strip. Left: the 16 and 32 px files enlarged pixel for pixel. Right: real size in light and dark tabs.

The name

Three wordmarks

W1

Cinzel caps

The display face the interface already uses for chapter titles, tracked at 0.16 em like the section labels. Quiet, carved, and it sits on the same baseline grid as the header.

  • Matches the system with no new face.
  • The most generic of the three on its own; the mark does the identifying.
OverviewAtlas
W2

Cinzel and 歴史

The same caps followed by 歴史 after a hairline. The Latin name and the word it echoes, with both scripts centred on one optical middle.

  • Says what the name means, in the reader's second script.
  • Too wide for a 320 px header; it is a lockup for the overview page, the about sheet and the social card.
OverviewAtlas
W3

Lowercase, gold present

Noto Serif in lowercase. The dots of the two i's are drawn separately, and the last one is gold: the present moment at the end of the word.

  • Warmer and more readable than caps at small sizes.
  • Next to the mark it would put two gold dots on screen; paired with the dial, its dot goes ink.

My recommendation

A2 Window with W1 caps

The window dial is the only one of the three that could belong to no other product: its arc and playhead are the atlas's own time control, and its small cut is a shape of its own at 16 px. Cinzel caps keep the wordmark inside the system, and W2 with 歴史 is the larger lockup for the overview page, the about sheet and the social card.
