= RFD 3 chapter reader-route decision

== Provenance and scope

The owner's instruction of 2026-09-27 was *"approve 1, then proceed with 2"*:
approve the current Qin set for release, then build the shared reader-facing
chapter experience. The approval is recorded in
link:../0002/PUBLICATION_APPROVAL.adoc[the publication approval gate]; this record
is the dated decision for the second half.

Two earlier records fixed the surfaces this one replaces. The bounded Qin chapter
shipped as a review route (`/compare/qin`) that stated `Unapproved preparation`
and rendered through a separate "comparison catalog", because the owner asked to
inspect corrected content before deciding on it
(link:COMPARISON_REVIEW_ROUTE.adoc[the comparison review route decision]). The
root page then presented both chapters as peers, but kept the Qin chapter's path
and its unapproved status line, and left the details surface's review-independence
prose as an open owner decision
(link:CHAPTER_PRESENTATION.adoc[the chapter presentation decision]). With the Qin
chapter approved, "unapproved preparation" is no longer true of it, and a review
route is no longer what a reader should meet.

This record supersedes the user-facing parts of both: the Qin chapter's review
route and its unapproved framing, and the open decision about `/compare/qin`'s
path. What still holds from them: the caller-supplied coverage window, the one
projection, the browser-safe catalog as the only loader, the publication approval
gate over every chapter tree, the peer cards, the display names, and the artifact
invariant's bans on the framings the owner rejected. No historical content,
publication set, or ledger digest changed here — only which route serves the
chapters and what a reader can do on them.

== Decision

* **One chapter route serves every chapter.** A chapter lives at
  `/chapters/<chapter-id>`, where the id is the chapter tree's own id. One
  dynamic route (`src/routes/chapters/[chapter]/`) loads the chapter through the
  browser-safe catalog with the coverage model
  `src/lib/atlas/chapter-registry.ts` gives it, prerenders one page per served
  chapter from `entries()`, and 404s an id the site does not serve. There is no
  per-chapter route, no review route, and no second shell: both chapters render
  through the same page component, the same workspace, and the same controls.
* **The served list is the site's chapter list, and the gate reads it.** The
  registry names the chapters the site serves; the chapter cards are keyed by the
  same ids, and the publication approval gate fails the build when a served
  chapter's current revisions come from a set without an `owner approved` row.
  Adding a chapter to the list, or moving a served chapter to a later revision,
  therefore requires the owner's decision rather than inheriting an older one. A
  chapter prepared for review but not served stays out of the list, keeps its
  `preparation snapshot` row, and does not ship.
* **The legacy routes redirect.** `/atlas` (the launch chapter's route) and
  `/compare/qin` (the second chapter's review route) are prerendered redirect
  pages that point at the chapter each one served, so the URLs a reader or a
  deployment already has keep working. The artifact invariant checks each
  redirect page names its target and carries no workspace mount point, so a stub
  cannot be served as if it were a chapter.
* **Both chapters expose the same exploration loop.** The workspace panel has
  three surfaces — the window's events, the chapter's story, and its sources — and
  a search field on the events surface; the details panel shows the selected
  event's place and placement note, its occurrence with precision and mode, the
  record's summary, its participants, its published dispute, and its citations
  with locators and quoted passages, plus explicit previous/next story jumps. The
  workspace takes no chapter branch and no per-chapter detail mode: the review
  surface's dispute presentation is now part of the one chapter surface, which is
  what RFD 4's AC2 visible-uncertainty part asked for and what the owner's
  instruction to build the shared experience authorized.
* **Search covers the chapter, not the window.** A query is matched against the
  chapter's own published text: event titles and summaries, the date as written
  and as the atlas states it, place and participant name forms in their own script
  and in the romanizations and translations the records publish, the published
  dispute, and citation locators and quotations. Comparison folds case, diacritics
  (`Ōtomo` matches `Otomo`), and whitespace. A modern equivalent is deliberately
  not searchable: the contract keeps it out of the historical name list and forbids
  reading it as an identification. Results include events outside the selected
  window, and each such row says so; selecting one keeps its details with the
  outside-selected-time notice and the explicit return action, without a map
  marker. No alias, coordinate, or claim is invented for search.
* **Story navigation follows the chapter's own order.** The navigator lists the
  published story's steps in the story's order with the story's own captions. A
  step that is a published event is an explicit jump (it selects the event and
  moves the window and playhead to its time, stopping playback); a step whose
  record has no event surface — a relationship, for example — stays in place as a
  summary step rather than being dropped, and is never treated as an event.
  Chronological stepping stays separate and still never changes the window.
* **Sources are shown as the records state them, and only when they are cited.**
  The details panel lists the selected event's citations with the record's own
  locator and the published quotation where the source's recorded terms permit
  quoting; the sources surface lists the chapter's *cited* sources with kind,
  edition, the record's attribution and licence id, and the identifier the record
  carries. A source document that no published revision cites is preparation
  content: it stays in the tree and out of the chapter, so a draft cannot change a
  served chapter's Sources surface without a decision covering it. The atlas
  states the record's terms and never restates or upgrades them.
* **The smallest viewports keep the drawer and the details apart.** At phone
  widths the details panel and the open drawer do not share the screen: the
  smallest reviewed viewport cannot hold the notice, the drawer's list, and the
  details at once, and a reader who opens the list is choosing to look at the
  list. Selecting an event closes the drawer, so the details are what a reader
  returns to, and from `md` up both stay visible side by side. The overlay column
  is bounded above the time controls and scrolls rather than overflowing them, and
  the panel's tabs carry their counts so no surface repeats a heading of its own
  and the list keeps the panel's height.
* **The comparison vocabulary leaves the product.** `src/lib/atlas/comparison.ts`
  is deleted, the chapter registry replaces it, and the reader-facing pages and
  cards carry none of the project's internal framing; the artifact invariant bans
  the rejected phrases (`comparison fixture`, `comparison harness`, `map
  treatments`, `treatment comparison`, `lekishi-historical-contract`, `Reviewed
  chapter`, `Comparison chapter`) on the root page and on every chapter page. The
  treatment-comparison rationale stays in the records, where it belongs.

== Alternatives considered

* **Keep `/compare/qin` as a second chapter route.** Rejected: the owner asked for
  ordinary chapter routing, the chapter is approved, and a path that says
  "compare" tells a reader about the project's internal comparison rather than
  about the chapter.
* **Keep `/atlas` as the Tang chapter's own route and add `/chapters/...` for the
  rest.** Rejected: two routes for two peer chapters is exactly the asymmetry this
  decision removes; the legacy URL redirects instead, so nothing that points at it
  breaks.
* **Make `/atlas` a chapter index.** Rejected: the root page is the index, and a
  second list page would duplicate it. `/atlas` therefore redirects to the chapter
  it always served.
* **Keep the review-only dispute presentation and add a second, reader
  presentation.** Rejected: the owner asked for visible uncertainty and dispute
  cues on both chapters, and two presentations of the same published record would
  drift. The review surface's presentation became the shared one.
* **Search the current window's events only.** Rejected: a reader who types a name
  must find it, and the atlas already has a rule for a selection the window
  excludes — keep the details, state the exclusion, offer the explicit return.
* **Make search a client-side filter of the rendered list.** Rejected: it would
  search only what the window shows, and it would put search logic in the
  component instead of in a pure, testable module.
* **Drop the story steps that are relationships.** Rejected: the story's order is
  the chapter's published presentation order, and silently dropping a published
  step would present a different order. They stay, marked as summaries.
* **Show modern equivalents as search aliases.** Rejected: the contract keeps them
  out of the historical name list and forbids reading them as identifications, so
  making them the string that finds a historical place would undo that rule in the
  UI.

== Consequences

* A reader meets two chapters as peers, each at an ordinary chapter URL, each with
  the same story navigation, search, sources, uncertainty cues, and dispute
  explanations. The project's comparison work is no longer part of what a reader
  sees or reads.
* Approving a chapter and serving it are now the same decision point: the gate
  fails a served chapter whose content is not owner-approved, so the site cannot
  ship a chapter behind a stale decision.
* The details panel states the owner's decision and the recorded review
  independence together, so the approval cannot read as independent or specialist
  review, and the panel is longer than before — it scrolls inside its bound, which
  the browser checks pin at every reviewed size.
* The workspace's panel is now a three-surface panel with a search field, so the
  overlay column has one more thing to hold at phone widths; the browser checks
  cover the tabs and the search at 1440x900, 820x634, 390x844, and 320x640.
* The chapter route's `load` runs during prerendering, so an unrepresentable
  chapter fails `pnpm build`; the served chapter's records still ship as the
  route's own chunks and never as a runtime fetch of the content tree.

== Limitations recorded

* **No layer controls, no nearby/connected lists, no relationship surface.** The
  loop now has chapter/story navigation, search, sources and locators, and
  uncertainty and dispute cues; RFD 1's layer controls and the separate
  nearby/connected lists, and any surface for relationships and actors, remain
  open. Relationship steps appear in the story as captions, and participants
  appear on their events; a reader cannot yet open a relationship or an actor.
* **Search is a simple substring match.** It has no ranking beyond name matches
  first, no fuzzy matching, and no per-field scoring; it also searches citation
  locators, so a query can match an event through a locator rather than through
  its story.
* **Undated events are still rejected.** The atlas window is closed at both ends,
  so a wholly undated event or an open-ended interval fails the projection rather
  than being discoverable outside time-filtered results. RFD 4 AC3's
  undated-event behavior stays unimplemented.
* **Areas and routes are still rejected.** The domain draws points; a
  `source_supported_area` or an itinerary fails the projection.
* **The specialist reviews and the critical-edition re-check remain open** for
  both chapters, as their ledger rows state; this record changes no content.
* **`/compare/qin` keeps the word in its path** as a redirect stub. The path
  resolves, but the URL a reader may still have in a bookmark says "compare" until
  it is dropped; removing a redirect is a later decision, and the redirect exists
  so no existing link breaks today.
* **The map labels still crowd at the whole-globe view** for the Tang chapter, the
  same rendering earlier records measured; nothing here changed the renderer.
* **An out-of-window selection can still be a camera-follow target.** The marker
  is filtered out with the window, and the details say so, but the follow control
  stays available for a selection the window excludes and would move the camera to
  a place whose marker is not drawn. Whether following should be disabled for an
  excluded selection is an open question, not something this record decides.
* **The panel's surfaces are reached through the drawer at phone widths.** Search,
  the story, and the sources are all inside the exploration panel, so on a phone
  the drawer has to be opened before they are visible; the drawer's label states
  the event count but not that more surfaces are behind it.
