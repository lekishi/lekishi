= RFD 3 globe renderer decision

== Provenance and scope

On 2026-10-12 the owner chose the custom Three.js globe as the atlas's
renderer (link:RENDERER_SPIKE.adoc[the renderer spike record], decision). That
record left the shipped atlas on Cesium and named the next work: bring the
custom globe behind the `AtlasRenderer` boundary, bundled through SvelteKit.
The owner approved a plan for that slice the same day, with one addition from
the owner's question about the file count: four sibling tiles share one file.

This record covers the browser renderer and its tiles only. The domain, the
state, the scene, the workspace, and the `AtlasRenderer` boundary are
unchanged; no historical content, publication set, or ledger row changed.
link:STATIC_ARCHITECTURE.adoc[The static architecture decision] stands, and
its Cesium-specific rules (the runtime copied into `static/cesium`, the base
URL, the ion token cleared) are replaced by the rules below; that record stays
readable as written.

== Decision

* *The custom globe replaces Cesium in the atlas.*
  `src/lib/atlas/renderer/globe-renderer.ts` implements `AtlasRenderer` on a
  Three.js globe ported from the spike (`spikes/renderer/globe/followup/`) into
  typed modules under `renderer/globe/`: tile geometry, the tile manager, the
  tile worker, geodesy and flights, label placement, and the view. Cesium, its
  runtime copy, and its build checks are removed from the package and the
  artifact.
* *What the renderer draws now.* Event markers in the scene's two tones
  (definite and possible) and three emphases (base, active, selected); labels
  placed without collision, the selected and active ones first; picking by
  screen distance (8 px for a mouse, 14 px for touch) or on a label; the home
  view; flights to a focused event (1.2 s, eased, over an arc for long hops, a
  jump under reduced motion); and camera following that stops on manual input,
  detected from pointer and wheel events so a flight never cancels it. The
  Firefox/Linux Ctrl-wheel pinch keeps its 5x amplification.
* *Tiles built at deploy time, not committed.* `scripts/globe/build-tiles.py`
  builds them into `static/globe` from the Terrain Tiles on AWS Open Data and
  the Natural Earth II hue, vendored at level 2 under
  `scripts/globe/natural-earth-ii/`. `pnpm dev` and `pnpm build` run it
  through `scripts/sync-globe-tiles.mjs`, which skips the build while
  `scripts/globe/` is unchanged; CI caches the tiles on the same key.
  Committing ~20 MB of binary tiles would grow the history on every repaint.
* *Coverage.* Levels 0–5 worldwide and 6–7 over the Tang chapter's extent
  (107°E–140°E, 22°N–40°N). Beyond the built levels the globe subdivides the
  parent's heights and imagery instead of requesting tiles that do not exist.
* *Four sibling tiles per file.* Each refinement is one request: `b/root.bin`
  holds the two level-0 tiles and `b/{z}/{x}/{y}.bin` holds the four children
  of tile z/x/y. The quadtree always loads siblings together, so a bundle adds
  no bytes and no wasted requests. The format is versioned (`LKGB`, version 1)
  and read and written by `renderer/globe/bundle.ts`.
* *Attribution.* The visible line names Three.js, 3d-tiles-renderer, the
  elevation, and the imagery, and links the full required attribution, which
  ships at `/globe/ATTRIBUTION.txt`.
* *The build invariant.* `scripts/check-static-build.mjs` now requires the tile
  worker, the `/globe/` URL in the built scripts, a well-formed tileset whose
  bundle count matches the files, a valid root bundle, the attribution, one
  stylesheet, and at most 20,000 files (the static host's limit).

* Without bundles the same tiles would be 6,324 files (a height and an
  imagery file per tile); the bundles bring the artifact to 944.
* The tile build from a cold cache, elevation fetched, took about 2 min 45 s;
  an unchanged builder skips it.
* The artifact is larger because Cesium drew a flat ellipsoid with 42 Natural
  Earth II tiles and the globe now carries relief and painted imagery to
  level 7. The reader downloads only the tiles the view needs: the home view
  loads the root bundle and a few child bundles, and the atlas JavaScript a
  reader downloads before the globe appears is about a seventh of Cesium's.
* Checks on this change: `pnpm check` with no errors, 524 unit and contract
  tests passing, the build invariant passing, and the browser suite on the
  built artifact, including a new check that a click on a globe marker selects
  its event and a check that every globe request stays on the artifact's own
  origin.

== Open

* *Frame times on devices.* The owner benchmarks the pull request's preview on
  the desktop, the iPad, the iPhone, and the mid-range phone before merge. The
  spike's lighter phone tier (half the trees, no shadows) concerns the diorama,
  which the atlas does not draw yet, so no tier is chosen in this change.
* *The deploy build.* The static host's build now needs Python 3 and network
  access to the elevation tiles and the package index on a cold cache. The
  first preview build of this change is the check that its build image
  provides them.
* *Not in this change:* draped polygons, relationship arcs, and journey lines,
  which wait on the domain and scene types for them
  (link:IMPLEMENTATION.adoc[the implementation plan], rendering).

== Review hardening — 2026-10-12

Oracle's review of PR 18 identified five renderer and migration defects. This
supplement records the corrections without changing the original measurements
above; the renderer choice and release scope stand.

* A failed refinement now keeps its loaded parent until every child is drawable.
  Failed roots and children no longer keep demand rendering busy. Two retries,
  after one and two seconds, recover transient failures without an unbounded
  request loop; disposal cancels the timers. `tiles.test.ts` covers transient and
  permanent root failure, failed refinement, and a partial sibling bundle.
* Co-located picks break equal-distance ties by the same selected, active, base
  priority used to draw the dots. The browser regression checks the exact
  arrival and departure identities rather than just their shared place name.
* Context restoration invalidates an idle view so Three.js redraws its restored
  resources without camera input. The browser regression loses and restores a
  settled context and observes new WebGL draw calls.
* Flights read the initial camera against WGS84, the same surface used to place
  it, rather than against the mean sphere. `geo.test.ts` now checks reconstructed
  eye and target positions within one centimetre, including 1,000 m nadir and
  500 m tilted views instead of tolerating a 15 km range discrepancy.
* Sync removes the retired generated `static/cesium` before either its cache-hit
  exit or a cold build; `make clean` also removes it, and it stays ignored during
  migration. Existing checkouts need no manual cleanup.

The pinned Nix shell also now includes Python, numpy and Pillow, and orb setup
calls `sync:globe` instead of the removed `sync:cesium`. This was necessary after
the first verification run failed to load `libstdc++.so.6` in the fallback
NumPy virtual environment. With the pinned packages, isolated Python imports
work, `pnpm verify` passes (528 tests; no type errors or warnings), and the cold
tile build produced 3,162 tiles in 809 bundles in 143 seconds. The static
invariant still verifies 944 files. Orb setup passed twice (8.8 s and 4.4 s),
using the cached tiles on both runs.

The built-artifact browser matrix exercised 117 cases across desktop, tablet
touch and phone emulation: 108 initially passed, five were deliberately skipped,
and four new-regression cases failed because of test setup. After making picking
stationary and allowing the loss event to finish before requesting restoration,
`playwright test --last-failed` passed all four, including restoration in each
project. The picker also checks that an initially unselected event becomes
selected from the canvas, so ignoring clicks cannot pass the regression.

Evidence: link:https://github.com/lekishi/lekishi/pull/18[PR 18] and
link:https://ampcode.com/threads/T-01a1315d-3a80-7728-a14b-3f462e746445[the review
and verification thread]. Physical-device frame-time acceptance remains the
owner's separate gate.

== Finer levels around the chapters' places — 2026-10-14

On 2026-10-14 the owner reported, from an iPhone on the PR 23 preview, that the
ground stayed blurred when zoomed in over 阿兒奈波島, and chose finer tiles over
a zoom floor. The renderer had loaded every tile it could: level 7, about
0.6 km per imagery pixel, was the finest built, so at the reported distance
(about 40 km) each pixel was drawn more than 15 screen pixels wide, and the tile
edges showed as straight seams. This supplement records the change; the
measurements above stay as measured.

* *Levels 8 to 10 around each drawn place.* `scripts/globe/build-tiles.py`
  builds level 8 within 0.8°, level 9 within 0.6° and level 10 within 0.4° of
  each of the 17 points the two chapters draw (`PLACES`), down to about 76 m per
  imagery pixel. A new place needs its point there. The renderer needed no
  change: it reads several ranges per level from `tileset.json` and subdivides
  its parent wherever a level is not built.
* *z10 elevation for those levels only.* Terrarium z10 is fetched over six
  boxes around the places (744 tiles, once, into `.cache/globe`) and serves
  levels 8 to 10 only. Levels 0 to 7 paint from the same sources as before, so
  they still match their neighbours; the fine levels take their colouring
  (slope, high ground, fields) from z7 as every level does, so only the
  coastline and the relief sharpen.
* *Needles removed.* The sharper close-up showed a dark wedge on the sea at
  秋妻屋浦: a 728 m needle in Terrarium's z10 data beside sea-level samples.
  The z7 mosaic carries them too (5,310 m beside samples at sea level). The
  builder now replaces any sample more than one pixel's width (at least
  150 m) above the fourth highest of the 24 around it, or below the fourth
  lowest, with their median: 24 in the z7 mosaic and 1,280 across the z10
  boxes. Of the 3,162 tiles built before, 37 changed (2 at level 4, 9 at
  level 5, 12 at level 6, 14 at level 7), all where a needle was; the others
  are byte-identical.
* *Measured.* 3,839 tiles in 1,052 bundles, 28.7 MB (was 3,162 in 809,
  20.7 MB). The artifact is 1,209 files (was 966), far under the static host's
  20,000. The cold tile build, z10 included, took about 3 min 40 s in the cloud
  development container; an unchanged builder still skips it.
* *Still soft below about 20 km.* Level 10 starts loading at about 39 km on a
  phone, where it is drawn about 2 screen pixels per imagery pixel; at 20 km it
  is about 4, and the camera can still come down to the ground. A zoom floor stays
  open if the owner wants one.

Evidence: link:https://github.com/lekishi/lekishi/pull/23[PR 23]; before and
after close-ups over 秋妻屋浦 at 57, 29 and 14 km and over 阿兒奈波島 at 38 km,
taken in the cloud container's software renderer.

=== Level 8 across the chapters, and inland detail — 2026-10-14

On the preview the owner found the sharper ground confined to small areas.
Two things caused it: the place boxes are small, and inland the land's colour
comes from Natural Earth's 20 km pixels, so only coasts and relief sharpened.
Offered both remedies, the owner chose both.

* *Level 8 across the chapters' extent.* Level 8 (about 0.3 km per pixel) now
  covers the same rectangle as levels 6 and 7, painted from Terrarium z9
  (1,600 tiles over the extent, needles removed: 1,254), which serves levels 8
  to 10 only. Levels 9 and 10 keep their boxes around the places; z10 is
  fetched over the level 9 boxes now, in seven mosaics.
* *Inland detail.* From level 8 down, vegetated ground above its surroundings
  darkens towards forest and valley floors lighten towards fields (the fine
  elevation less its 1.6 km blur), with a mottle at two finer scales. Each
  term is zero on average and fades in with the pixel size, so level 8 keeps
  level 7's colours where a view shows both. The elevation is read over a
  margin wider than the blur, so neighbouring tiles agree at their edges.
* *Measured.* 4,985 tiles in 1,347 bundles, 46.4 MB (level 8 alone is 1,248
  tiles and 17.3 MB); the artifact is 1,504 files. Levels 0 to 7 are
  byte-identical to the build above. The tile build, with the elevation
  already cached, took 8 min 36 s in the cloud development container, and its
  peak memory was about 2.3 GB (the z9 mosaic); a cold cache adds the z9 and
  z10 downloads.

=== Painting in parallel, and one build in CI — 2026-10-14

Painting the tiles dominated the build, and continuous integration repeated
it in all eight jobs on two-CPU runners whenever the builder changed.

* *Builder.* Each level's tiles are painted across one forked process per
  CPU, the needle filter ranks its neighbours with a partition instead of a
  full sort, and the elevation tiles are decoded on threads. The output is
  byte-identical to the build above.
* *Measured.* With the elevation cached, the build takes 373 to 394 s in the
  four-CPU cloud development container (was 8 min 36 s), with a peak of
  2.34 GB summed proportional set size across the workers, because the
  forked workers share the sources. In CI, one build job on a four-CPU runner
  repainted every tile and built the artifact in 4 min 6 s. The browser jobs
  download that artifact rather than building their own.
