== The audit's verdict, 2026-10-23

The chapter is the only unit of navigation. Home is a catalogue of 39 plates;
the switcher is a list on a ribbon too crowded to read; search groups by
chapter before match quality; a chapter's globe shows only its own places, so
nothing on the site says that Alexander's Babylon is Hammurabi's Babylon or
that 杜環 and the embassy of 750 were in 廣州 within a year of each other.
Play is a four-years-per-second clock that opens no card and moves no camera.
The region-scale map is the low-resolution imagery wash with no painted
relief, no sea or river names and no label declutter, so the "map worth
looking at" that RFD 4 Track 1 exits on is not yet on the screen.
Qualification (the legend essay, placement notes, the "schematic" caption
drawn on the water, the digest in About) outweighs the history.

Ten defects were reproduced and are listed in <<defects>>.

== The owner's diagnosis

The owner accepted the audit's recommendations as a diagnosis on 2026-10-23
and named the problems in their own words:

. "No exploration on the atlas."
. Chapters are valid but belong on a separate, better organised page, filterable
  by region or culture. "If the globe is a way to explore horizontally in an
  era, we need a way to showcase vertical timelines as well, and chapters
  might be a good area to do so."
. "IA is straight up bad. The point of this project is to aggregate historical
  information to show their correlation and relationships, I don't get any of
  those."
. "Nothing makes it feel like civilization at all. No 3D animated figures,
  terrain don't zoom, and obviously no buildings and such when zoomed in
  most."
. "Just sprinkling data points on the map is boring, and boring is not what
  I'm trying to achieve here."

These are the acceptance test for this note. A screen that does not show a
correlation or a relationship, or that is boring, fails it.

== Proposed structure: two axes

The site has two axes and one world.

Horizontal, the world at a moment::
  The globe is the exploration surface. The reader holds a year; every chapter
  alive at that year lights up where it happened, and the threads between
  them are drawn from the places and peoples they share. Dragging the globe
  explores space; dragging the playhead explores an era. Diving into a chapter
  from here keeps the year, so the reader lands in the scene at the moment
  they were looking at.
Vertical, chapters as time::
  The chapters page is one column per culture, time running down the page,
  each chapter a bar at its window, filterable by region and by kind. This is
  the vertical timeline the owner asked for. Hovering a bar lights the
  chapters it shares places with across the other columns, so the correlation
  is visible before anything is opened. The deep-time chapters (the early
  Americas from 21051 BCE) keep their windows but the axis folds before 2400
  BCE rather than stretching to them.
Relationships connect the two::
  A place has one page: its vertical timeline across every chapter that
  touches it. A people has the same. From a chapter's card, "threads through
  this chapter" lists the chapters it shares records with and what they share.
  None of this needs new data (<<data>>).
A chapter is a guided tour::
  Inside a chapter, play is a story, not a clock. Each step flies the camera,
  moves the date, opens the card, reads the source's own words, and holds. A
  scrubber remains for the reader who wants a clock, with its speed scaled to
  the window rather than fixed at four years a second.

The mock shows each of these as a screen: the world at 753 with three chapters
alive and the 杜環–embassy thread drawn through 廣州; the ten culture columns
with their links; the Tang embassy as a 38-step tour on painted relief with
the party moving between attested stops; and Roma across six chapters as one
timeline.

|===
|Page |Route |Carries

|===

Search stays one dialog reachable with `/`. It ranks by match quality first,
then by chapter, and a place result opens the place page when the place is
shared.

=== What the data already carries

The audit's draft text said each chapter owned its own copy of every place.
That was true of the search index and the chapter projection, not of the
records. The world-first layout (RFD 2) has one record per place and per
actor with a global id, and the chapter manifests point at them. Counting on
`main` at 27f5ac3:

[cols="3,1",options="header"]
|===
|Across the 39 served chapters |Count

|Events |930
|Places, one record each |683
|Places referenced by two or more chapters |88
|Actors referenced by two or more chapters |41
|Chapter pairs linked by a shared place or actor |91
|Story steps |1,065
|===

The relationships the owner wants are therefore already in the records. What
is missing is a projection that keeps the ids across chapters instead of
re-keying them per chapter, and the three pages above that read it. The
compiler's one-file-per-chapter output stays; it gains one small index of
shared records (ids, names, the chapters and years that touch each), which is
what the mock's world and place screens are built from.

Two things the data does not yet carry, and the pages should degrade without:
a region or culture per chapter (the mock assigns the ten columns by hand from
the manifests; a `region` field on the manifest, or on the sources' culture,
settles it), and typed cross-chapter relationships beyond "shares a place or
a people" (RFD 2's relationship records can express "succeeds", "cites",
"is the same episode as" when an author wants to assert them).

=== The basemap is already paid for

The mock's ground is painted relief (hypsometric tint, hillshade, darkened
coasts, an ice mask) generated with NumPy and Pillow from the Terrarium
elevation tiles the build already caches for the globe: level 4 for the
world, level 7 for the Tang region. Painting the world at 4096 × 2048 took
seconds. This is RFD 1's "painted relief map" and RFD 3's basemap pipeline,
and it shows that the "map worth looking at" is a build step away rather than
a data purchase. The proposal is to ship it as the globe's base imagery and
the 2D fallback at every scale, with sea and river names and label declutter
on top, before any further chrome.

=== The scene must feel like a civilization

The owner's fourth and fifth points are RFD 1's approved hero, restated as
requirements rather than polish. Listed here so that the IA work does not
land on a scene that fails them:

* Terrain that zooms: the globe's level-of-detail everywhere a chapter has
  places, not only around 薩摩. Relief at region scale, the diorama mesh at
  local scale.
* Units on journeys: a party or vessel at the playhead's stop, moving between
  attested stops with the tour, in the hero's register.
* Settlements at attested sites: glyph buildings of a kind at local scale,
  never a reconstruction, as RFD 1's visual grammar states.
* Figures: the pennants' people present in the scene at the step's place,
  animated in the diorama tier.

These are the diorama, symbolic settlements, relief basemap and
collision-free label items of link:../0001/IMPLEMENTATION.adoc[RFD 1's
checklist]. This note asks that they be scheduled with the IA pages rather
than after them, because the
owner's judgement of the IA will be made on the scene it sits in.

== Alternatives weighed

A single page with everything on the globe::
  Chapters as the only vertical structure, with filters on the globe. Keeps
  one surface but gives the owner no vertical timeline, and the ribbon already
  fails at 39 chapters.
Chapters page as a filtered catalogue::
  The current plates with region chips. Organised, but still a list; it shows
  no overlap between chapters and does not answer "what else was happening".
Build the relationships into the records first::
  Typed cross-chapter relationships before any page. The shared ids already
  give the first 91 links for free; typed links can come as authors assert
  them without blocking the pages.

== What this note asks of the owner

Judge the mock the way the hero was judged: against "does this show
correlation and relationships" and "is this boring". Three choices are open:

. Whether the world page replaces home, or home keeps a short list beside it.
. Whether the scene requirements are scheduled with the IA pages (this note's
  recommendation) or after them.
. The region or culture assignment per chapter: the ten columns in the mock,
  or a different grouping.
