= RFD 2 Historical data, time, and evidence

== Goal

Represent historical events and relationships with enough temporal, geographic,
and evidentiary context to support link:../0001/README.adoc[RFD 1] without turning
visual convenience into historical certainty. Support small curated chapters
first and eventual contributions from researchers, professors, and other editors.

== Context

The owner's illustrated history books are useful high-level references, but
will not supply every date, route, interaction, or competing interpretation.
Public datasets, papers, and specialist contributors will be necessary. No
single dataset is expected to provide a reliable global history of influence.

== Proposed domain model

This is a conceptual model, not an approved database schema.

Places:: Stable identities with historical names, source-backed locations,
  geometry validity periods, and spatial precision or uncertainty. A changing
  name must not silently create a new place or imply that identities never change.
Actors:: Distinguish people, states, communities, companies, religious bodies,
  and other institutions. Membership and roles can vary over time. Cultures
  are not assumed to occupy exclusive, sharply bounded polygons.
Events:: Occurrences or processes with temporal bounds, participants and roles,
  locations, and links to a broader campaign, mission, or sequence.
Relationships:: Typed connections among actors, places, and events: trade,
  travel, conflict, diplomacy, transmission, institutional change, or proposed
  influence. Direction and temporal validity are explicit where meaningful;
  not every relationship is directional or causal.
Claims and sources:: Attach assertions to citations, locators such as page or
  section, provenance, review status, uncertainty, and supporting or conflicting
  interpretations. One source need not support every field of an event.
Stories:: Curated selections and navigation sequences over the historical
  records, not separate copies of those records or the only possible interpretation.

Use portable identifiers and retain external dataset identifiers at import.
Keep presentation colors, viewport coordinates, and Cesium objects out of the
historical source of truth. Geometries can use GeoJSON where suitable, accompanied
by explicit temporal and evidentiary metadata.

== Decision: historical names with local-script forms

Use the period-appropriate historical name as the primary place label, paired
with its local-language/script form where supported by sources. Include Japanese
kanji, Chinese hanzi, and equivalent writing systems for other places rather than
displaying only romanized or English names. A label can pair a readable
romanization with local script, such as "Chang'an · 長安"; the example does not
establish a universal transliteration convention or assert historical pronunciation.

Retain the name's language, script, historical validity, and source, including
the relationship between a local-script form and a romanization or translation.
Do not infer language or pronunciation from shared characters alone. Prefer
source-supported period-appropriate forms; do not silently substitute modern
orthography or fabricate a historical spelling when evidence is unavailable.
Document missing or uncertain forms and distinguish editorial transliteration
from original-script evidence. Multiple attested local names may coexist.

Modern equivalents and alternate spellings remain available in details and
search, including local-script and romanized queries. Preserve original display
forms when normalizing search text. Explain relationships to modern places:
renaming, relocation, successor settlements, and changed boundaries are not
automatically aliases of an identical place.

== Time and space contracts

Historical dates are not ordinary application timestamps. Preserve the source's
date expression, calendar where known, precision, and uncertainty separately
from normalized values used to sort and filter. Distinguish an event that lasted
several years from an event whose exact date is only known to fall in that range.

Before durable storage, specify BCE/CE conversion and the treatment of year zero,
calendar conversion, open-ended intervals, overlapping events, and inclusion at
window boundaries. Civil BCE/CE display has no year zero; internal astronomical
numbering is a possible representation, not yet a decision. JavaScript Date and
the renderer's clock must not be the authoritative historical model.

Playback can use a derived numeric coordinate. Derivation must not convert a
year-only claim into an asserted January 1 date. Preserve partial ordering when
the evidence does not establish which event happened first.

Locations likewise need precision and interpretation. A point may represent an
approximate site or a label anchor, not a surveyed location. A route may be an
attested itinerary, a reconstructed route, or a schematic link. Record the
difference. Political control, religious presence, and cultural influence are
different relationships and must not share one generic territorial meaning.

Modern terrain is geographic context, not historical reconstruction. Rivers,
coastlines, settlements, and borders can change; historical geometry overrides
need their own sources and validity ranges.

== Decision: readable, explicitly approximate locations

Include approximate locations on the map using clearly marked approximate points
or source-supported areas. Use labels or symbols as well as color to distinguish
them from precise locations, and explain the location claim, its uncertainty,
and supporting sources in details.

Do not invent an uncertainty radius or precise boundary when the evidence supplies
none. If a point anchors the label for a broadly located event, identify it as a
display anchor rather than an asserted event coordinate. A source-supported area
does not imply uniform probability or political control. Keep the default map
readable rather than requiring prominent uncertainty areas for every event.

When there is no usable geographic placement, retain the record in lists and
details without manufacturing a map position. Approximate placement is distinct
from wholly unknown location and is not grounds for excluding a supported event
from the map.

== Decision: possible temporal matches are included by default

Time filtering includes events whose supported possible occurrence range
overlaps the selected window, even when occurrence within that window is not
certain. Visibly distinguish these possible matches from definite matches and
expose the source date expression and uncertainty in the event details. The cue
must not depend on color alone.

For example, an event known only to have happened sometime between 730 and 760 CE
appears as a possible match in a 740–750 CE window. This does not assert that it
happened in those years, lasted thirty years, or occurred at the range midpoint.
An occurrence range entirely outside the window does not match on this basis.
Playback must preserve the distinction between uncertain occurrence and duration;
it must not invent an exact event date or animate continuous activity throughout
the uncertainty range.

This decides the inclusion default for bounded uncertain occurrences, not all
temporal mechanics. Exact endpoint conventions, calendar normalization, uncertain
duration endpoints remain to be specified.

== Decision: undated events remain discoverable outside temporal results

Events with no usable date remain accessible through search, connected events,
and a clearly labelled "Date unknown" list. Exclude them from time-filtered map
results, chronological stepping, and playback; do not assign a placeholder date
or treat a missing date as matching every time window. This differs from a
bounded uncertain occurrence, which follows the possible-match policy above.

Opening an undated event exposes its details and sources without changing the
time window or inventing a playhead position. Mark its date as unknown, not
"outside selected time": there is no evidence that it falls outside the window
either. A connection to a dated event does not by itself establish its date.

== Claims drive visualization

Documented contact does not establish influence; influence does not imply
unchanged adoption. Trade networks do not prove particular voyages. A religious
council does not cause an instantaneous, geographically uniform division.

Store competing accounts without averaging them into false precision. Prefer
descriptive uncertainty and review states over invented confidence percentages.
Publication and historical certainty are separate: a reviewed account can
explicitly describe a disputed claim.

Only interpolate movement when the chosen presentation is justified or clearly
labelled illustrative. Use dated political-control snapshots rather than
invented continuously moving boundaries. Where location or date is unknown,
retain the record in the narrative/list rather than assigning arbitrary map
coordinates or dates merely to make it visible.

== Decision: first-release interpretation policy

The first public release includes directly attested claims and interpretations
already advanced in cited scholarship. Clearly distinguish the two and attribute
interpretations to their sources. Attestation records what a source reports;
it does not by itself establish that the report is undisputed or accurate.

Original contributor hypotheses remain unpublished research notes, not public
atlas content or a separate experimental public layer. This limits the initial
review burden without excluding scholarly disagreement. Whether to publish
original hypotheses in a later release requires a separate policy decision.

Every published relationship requires a cited evidentiary basis. An interpretive
label never substitutes for evidence. Label schematic route geometry separately
from the historical claim: a sourced connection does not establish an exact
itinerary. Generated illustrations and AI suggestions do not supply evidence.

=== Decision: present an attributed main account with cited alternatives

For the first release, present one clearly attributed main account with a visible
indication when it is disputed. Explain the disagreement and make alternative
scholarly accounts available in the detail panel, citing each account. Choosing
the main account is an editorial presentation choice, not a declaration that
the disagreement is settled or that alternatives have equal evidentiary support.

Preserve distinct claims and their sources rather than merging conflicting dates,
locations, or interpretations into a supposedly definitive record. A map depiction
based on the main account must not conceal material disagreement behind an
unqualified visual assertion. The detail panel should explain what differs and
which account informs the depiction.

Selectable interpretations that change the map and simultaneous competing map
overlays are deferred beyond the first release. Disputed relationships need not
be excluded solely because scholars disagree; they still must satisfy the cited
evidence and publication policies above.

== Acquisition and publication

Begin with versioned, schema-validated JSON/GeoJSON and citations for one chapter.
Imports are candidates for review, not automatically published facts. Once
editing and queries require a database, migrate the curated records into the
storage proposed in link:../0003/README.adoc[RFD 3] with stable identities and
provenance. Do not maintain files and database as competing writable authorities.

Candidate sources include:

* https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing[Wikidata]: identifiers and
  structured candidate facts; structured data is CC0, not a guarantee of accuracy.
* https://pleiades.stoa.org/[Pleiades]: ancient-place information useful for
  selected ancient-world chapters, not complete world coverage.
* https://whgazetteer.org/[World Historical Gazetteer]: historical place names,
  temporal context, and linked datasets with uneven coverage and dataset-specific
  reuse terms.
* Scholarly books, papers, primary-source editions, and specialist review for
  relationships and interpretations that gazetteers do not supply.

Record licenses and attribution requirements for each dataset and media asset.
Open access is not the same as unrestricted redistribution. Owning a book does
not grant permission to reproduce its maps or illustrations. Cite and summarize
appropriately; do not copy imagery or substantial text without suitable rights.

Initially, contributors can propose content-file changes for review. A later
editorial UI can support draft, source review, and publication, with revisions,
attribution, corrections, and disagreement retained. Apply the initial editorial
authority below; credentials alone do not substitute for sources.

AI may assist extraction, normalization suggestions, and missing-field checks.
Its output remains a candidate with a traceable source, never an automatic
historical authority or a reason to invent missing evidence.

=== Decision: owner-led initial editorial review

Darwin, the project owner, is the initial editor and may approve his own sourced
work for the first public release. A second reviewer or specialist is not a
mandatory publication gate. Invite specialist review when available and useful,
without describing self-reviewed material as independently or expertly reviewed.

Require citations and a documented review checklist before publication, including
checks for source support, attribution, date/location uncertainty, the distinction
between attestation and scholarly interpretation, and reuse rights. Record the
reviewer and the content revision approved, including when author and reviewer
are the same person. This applies to the initial content-file workflow, not only
to a future editorial application.

Self-review does not permit unsupported claims or original contributor hypotheses
in public content. External contributors can propose changes, but publication
requires the initial editor's approval; broader delegation remains to be defined.
AI assistance does not constitute editorial approval.

=== Decision: stable records with reviewed revisions and public correction history

An event, relationship, or other historical record has a stable identity across
multiple revisions. Its normal URL shows the latest published revision, not an
unreviewed draft. Preserve published revisions and expose a reader-facing history
of what changed, why, and the supporting sources; readers need not understand Git
commits to follow corrections.

Each revision preserves its content, citations, author, and change explanation.
Publication records the approving editor, the exact revision approved, and when
approval and publication occurred. A draft correction leaves the previous
published revision visible until the correction is approved and published.
Publish related changes as a consistent set of record revisions so an event and
its relationships do not expose incompatible intermediate updates.

For the initial file-based workflow, use Git history with explicit editorial
metadata alongside the content. A commit alone is not editorial approval or
publication. Retain the history needed to serve prior published revisions and
their correction explanations. This does not require readers to select numbered
chapter editions or require a custom branching-and-merging system.

Corrections are distinct from scholarly disagreement: if competing claims remain
supported, preserve them in the current revision under the main-account and
cited-alternatives policy rather than hiding one in an older revision.

If editing later moves into PostgreSQL, preserve these semantics with immutable
revision records and a reference to the current published revision, retaining
consistent publication across related records. The migration must preserve
stable identities, published history, and approval metadata without introducing
two writable authorities.

== Alternatives and trade-offs

Live queries against public knowledge services avoid an import pipeline but
introduce availability, consistency, and review problems; prefer curated releases.
A universal event table with one year and coordinate is simple but cannot
represent uncertainty or relationships. A full scholarly ontology before the
first chapter is costly; begin with the smallest explicit model that preserves
the distinctions above and version its interchange format.
