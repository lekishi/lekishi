This note goes with three draft chapters written on 2026-10-21 in the world-first
layout of link:AUTHORING_FORMAT.adoc[the authoring format]: Xerxes' invasion
(480–479 BCE), the march of the Ten Thousand (401–399 BCE), and Alexander's
campaigns (335–323 BCE). They are candidates #15–#17 of the chapter-selection
survey. None carries an approval. The aim was volume: enough records, of enough
different shapes, to test the data format and the engine, not to publish.

Each chapter rests on one ancient narrative read in a public-domain English
translation, with places identified through Pleiades. Every quotation in the
records was checked against the text it cites.

== Sources

* *Herodotus*, *Xenophon*, *Arrian*: the works and these translations are public
  domain in the United States. Chinnock's death date is not yet confirmed, so
  the Arrian translation's worldwide status is open; it must be settled before
  release.
* *Grote*, _History of Greece_: cited only as `interpretation`, for turning
  Herodotus's seasons and Xenophon's halts into years BCE.
* *Chinnock's headings and notes*: cited only as `interpretation`, for turning
  Arrian's Athenian archons, months and Olympiads into years BCE.
* *Pleiades*: one dataset source whose citations pin a place id and the daily
  dump of 2026-10-21. The register asks for numbered releases; before release
  the citations should be re-pinned to the current numbered release. The
  maintainers ask for an email notice of reuse, which is an outward step for
  the owner to take.

The source register now has one row for the Project Gutenberg translations.

== Dating

None of the three narratives gives years. Herodotus names one archon (Calliades,
VIII.51); Xenophon gives distances in stages and parasangs and halts in days;
Arrian dates some events by archon and month. Every year BCE in these chapters
is therefore a modern conversion, cited as the interpretation of a named scholar
(Grote or Chinnock), with the ancient passage as a supporting claim. The one
exception is the start of the Athos canal, which Grote does not date: about 483
BCE is an editorial count of Herodotus's "about three years" back from Grote's
date for the march, cited as such.

Most stages fall *between* two anchors rather than at one. For those, the date is
a `window` from the earlier anchor's year to the later one's, and its claim cites
both anchors in one claim with two sources. That keeps one main claim per field,
which the contract requires, but it reads as "somewhere in this span", which is
weaker than the text supports: the sources give a sequence and day counts that
an engine could use to order and interpolate. See <<proposals>>.

Recorded discrepancies:

* The Gutenberg text of Macaulay reads "five-and-thirty days" at VIII.115, where
  the Greek reads forty-five. The figure is not published until the printed
  edition is checked; the event's notes say so.
* Chinnock dates Gaugamela to September 331 BCE in one note and the eclipse
  eleven days before it to 20 September in another. The event keeps the year
  only and records both.

== Placement

Every located place carries Pleiades' representative point as an
`approximate_point`, with precision mapped from Pleiades' location precision.
Eleven places are unlocated, either because Pleiades has no position (Critalla,
Ceramon-agora, Caystru-pedion, Thymbrium, Corsote, Charmande, the Sogdian rock,
Pura) or no
entry that could be matched with confidence (Peltae, Kunaxa), or because its
point is a placeholder (Thapsacus). No route geometry is drawn anywhere: each
campaign is an event whose span covers its stages, and the stages are separate
events at their places. In the Ten Thousand every stage points to the campaign
with `part_of`; in Alexander every stage but the destruction of Thebes does; in
Xerxes only the twelve stages of the march and the voyage do, and the battles
and the preparations of 483–481 BCE stand beside the campaign event in the
window.

== Findings for the engine and the data format

. *Journeys.* The owner decided on 2026-10-21 (#26) that journeys are their own
  record kind under contract v1; the record's shape is still proposed and the
  compiler does not accept it yet. All three chapters are journeys in that
  sense, and the Anabasis is the clearest attested itinerary the period has:
  every stage has a distance and most have a halt. Until the kind is built, the
  chapters use campaign events with `part_of`. A journey should add a cited
  sequence of stops over these events, not replace them: the stage events
  already carry start, waypoint and end places in order.
. *Parallel legs.* Xerxes' army and fleet moved separately and met at Therma and
  Phaleron. The events say so in prose; a journey with two legs would show it.
. *Disputes and alternatives.* The Gordian knot carries a `disputed` status with
  Aristobulus's account as an `alternative` claim; the attack on Delphi carries a
  `conflicting` claim from Grote. Both are good fixtures for the dispute UI.
. *Shared records.* Copying another thread's file byte for byte works, but it
  cascades: a copied place brings its sources with it, and one copied source
  failed `prettier --check` on its own branch.

[[proposals]]
== Proposals for the owner

Nothing below is implemented. The second was decided in principle on
2026-10-22; the others need a decision.

. *Vocabulary.* Five records use `other` because the vocabulary lacks a type:
  a canal (the Athos canal), a battlefield (Kunaxa), an army as an actor (the
  Ten Thousand), and two relationship kinds, mercenary service (with Seuthes)
  and marriage (Alexander and Roxana). Proposed additions: place types `canal`
  and `battlefield`; actor type `army`; relationship types `service` and
  `marriage`.
. *An evidence state for conventional chronology.* Converting an archon year or a
  season into a year BCE is standard, but `interpretation` attributed to one
  scholar makes it look contested. A distinct basis such as `conventional`
  would say "a standard conversion" and let the reader see where real disputes
  are. The owner chose to add one on 2026-10-22; its shape and the rule for
  when to use it are proposed in link:CONVENTIONAL_DATES.adoc[a conventional
  basis for standard date conversions].
. *Dates between anchors.* A date mode that names its two anchoring claims and
  the source's sequence or day count would let the engine order and interpolate
  stages instead of showing a multi-year window.

== Shared ids

shared memory: `samos`, `ephesus`, `miletus`, `athens` and `corinth` are this
