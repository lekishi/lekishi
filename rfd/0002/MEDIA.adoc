= RFD 2 media v1: images as records

== Provenance

link:../0001/README.adoc[RFD 1] sets a target of 20 or more cleared images for
the Tang chapter. link:AUTHORING_FORMAT.adoc[The authoring format record] has
proposed a media kind since 2026-10-14. A media file names the file, the
source that carries its licence, the records it `depicts`, and a depiction
label. That proposal says images arrive with contract v1, together with the
first chapter that shows one. The owner chose on 2026-10-21 to make journeys
their own v1 kind (#26; link:AUTHORING_FORMAT.adoc[the authoring format
record], "Decided: journeys are their own kind"), and v1 now arrives with the
first published journey, with territories, media and per-claim credit joining
it as further rules once they are agreed. This record proposes the media
rules.

Everything below is *proposed*, except where files live, which the owner
decided on 2026-10-22: masters in the repository. No image is added and no
code is changed until the owner decides the rest.

== What the evidence rules require

AGENTS.md and RFD 2 already settle the hard part:

* An image is never evidence on its own account. A 1298 handscroll of the 748
  storm shows how the story was told five centuries later. It does not show
  the storm.
* Generated visual concepts are design references, never historical content.
  They cannot be media records.
* Reuse rights are recorded per source, with the reviewer and the date. Text
  only reaches readers when `redistribution` permits it.
* No provider, account or token, and the site stays static.

So a media record has to say three things, and the reader has to show them:
what the image is, what it depicts and on whose word, and who may reuse it.

== Proposed: the media record

[source,yaml]
----
# historical/media/toseiden-emaki-storm.yaml
title: The storm of 748 in the 東征伝絵巻
file: toseiden-emaki-storm.jpg          # in historical/media/files/, see "Where files live"
sha256: …                                # of the committed file, checked by the build
alt: >-
  A ship in high waves under a dark sky; monks crouch on deck while sailors
  work the mast.
depiction: later_depiction
made:
  value: "1298"
  calendar: julian
  cite: { source: commons-toseiden-emaki-storm, at: "Date" }
depicts:
  - record: south-drift-748
    cite:
      id: toseiden-emaki-storm-depicts
      says: The holding institution's description identifies the scene as the 748 storm.
      source: …                          # the catalogue or a cited study, never the editor
      at: …
creator: 蓮行 (painter)
holder: 唐招提寺
source: commons-toseiden-emaki-storm     # a source record of kind media, with the licence
caption: >-
  A later depiction, painted in 1298, of the storm in the biography. It is not
  evidence of the event.
----

* *Depiction vocabulary.* `depiction` takes one of five values. The card
  states it in words next to every image:
  ** `contemporary_object`: an object from the period itself, such as the 8th-century
     seated figure of 鑒眞. It is evidence of itself, not of the events
     around it.
  ** `later_depiction`: a later work that shows a historical subject, such as the
     emaki. It is never evidence of the subject.
  ** `site_photograph`: a modern photograph of a place as it is now.
  ** `modern_reconstruction`: a replica or reconstruction, such as the
     遣唐使船 replica at 平城宮跡.
  ** `map_or_diagram`: a historical or modern map, drawn or reproduced.
* *What it depicts is a claim.* `depicts` links to records, and each link
  carries a cite like any other claim. The identification comes from the
  holder's catalogue or from scholarship. A scene the editor recognises
  without a source gets `basis: interpretation` and an `attributed_to`, or it
  isn't linked.
* *Two layers of rights.* The media source records the object's status (a 1298
  scroll is public domain) and the reproduction's status. A faithful
  photograph of a flat work is treated as public domain, following Wikimedia
  Commons' PD-Art practice. A photograph of a statue or a building has its
  own copyright, and its licence decides. Both layers must permit
  redistribution, or the image isn't served.
* *Licences accepted.* Public domain, CC0, CC BY and CC BY-SA. Not accepted:
  non-commercial and no-derivatives licences, or "used with permission". The
  same terms must hold for a future native app, and the site resizes every
  image.
* *Alt text is required.* It describes what is visible, not what the image is
  claimed to depict.

== Proposed: where files live

Decided on 2026-10-22: the masters live in the repository, as the first point
below proposes. The derivatives and the rule that only published chapters'
images are public are still proposed.

* *Masters in the repository.* One master per image goes in
  `historical/media/files/`, at most 3000 px on the long side and normally
  well under 1 MB. Its `sha256` is in the record, so the approval digest
  covers the exact pixels. At the 20-image target this adds roughly 10–15 MB
  of history. Masters are inputs, like the record files; nothing under
  `historical/media/` is copied into the artifact.
* *Derivatives at build time.* `pnpm build` derives the sizes the site serves
  (for example 480, 960 and 1600 px, in AVIF and JPEG) into a generated,
  ignored directory, the way the globe tiles and fonts are built today. 20
  images come to about 120 files, against the 20,000 limit. The build
  invariant fails on a record whose file or hash doesn't match.
* *Only published chapters' images are public.* A production build derives
  and credits only the media reached by a chapter it serves. Media that only
  a draft or stale chapter reaches gets no derivative, no credit line and no
  public URL, because a file name, an alt text or a credit can name what the
  chapter withholds (link:../0006/README.adoc[RFD 6]). An image shared with a
  served chapter stays public. Signed-in members opening a production draft
  see its text without its draft-only images at first; serving them would
  need authenticated asset delivery, which is a separate decision and must not
  widen the worker's routes on its own. Previews built with drafts derive
  every image. The build invariant gains a check that no draft-only media
  file, derivative or credit is in a production artifact.
* *Not chosen: fetching from Commons at build time.* It would keep the
  repository small, but it makes every build depend on a third-party service,
  and the images could change under an approval. RFD 2 and AGENTS.md rule out
  both.

== Proposed: the reader

* The event card shows the images that depict the event, as thumbnails that
  open full size. Under each image are its depiction label, creator, date
  and licence line, and a link to the source.
* The manifest's `hero:` names a media record for the chapter card. A
  `later_depiction` hero carries its label on the card as well.
* Images never go on the globe as markers, and they never stand in for a
  place's position.
* `/globe/ATTRIBUTION.txt` gains a sibling, `/media/ATTRIBUTION.txt`, with
  the full credit of every image the artifact serves, and only those. The
  build fails if a served image is missing from it.

== Contract v1

Media adds a record kind, a depiction vocabulary, and depiction claims that link
an image to the records it depicts. AGENTS.md requires new vectors for new
rules, so v1 gains vectors such as these:

* a media record without a source licence is rejected
* a `non-commercial` licence is rejected
* a `depicts` link without a cite is rejected
* a `later_depiction` cannot support a claim about its subject
* a file whose hash differs from the record fails

These rules join v1, which arrives with the first published journey; media
does not gate journey publication, and journeys do not wait for media.
Neither kind changes v0, and the 187 vectors stay as they are.
