This report answers link:../0001/README.adoc[RFD 1]'s "source feasibility before
selecting the mission" decision and link:../0002/README.adoc[RFD 2]'s Phase 0
checklist item: compare up to three Japanese missions to Tang China in one initial
desk-research pass using accessible sources and owner-supplied references, and
return findings before deeper research, purchases, or outreach. It is a
feasibility report, not a selection: the mission choice is
link:../0004/README.adoc[RFD 4]'s Gate 1 owner decision.

Nothing in this report is published atlas content. Where it states historical
facts it cites a source and a locator; where it describes identifications,
reconstructions, or scholarly readings it says so. It asserts no route, no
causation, and no adaptation beyond what its cited sources state.

For the recommended mission the coverage model is: *primary mission window
750–754; included coda nodes outside that window at 758, 759, 763, and 779; the
outbound voyage of 752 is excluded* (<<coverage-model>>). This one model is used
without variation in the recommendation, the comparison, the coverage model, the
coverage exclusions, and the Gate 1 decision.

== Purpose, scope, and method

Scope performed in this pass:

* Read `AGENTS.md`, RFD 1, RFD 2, RFD 4, their implementation checklists, and the
  founding discussion linked from RFD 1.
* Inspected the two owner-supplied photographs attached in the founding
  discussion (see <<owner-inputs>>).
* Shortlisted three missions and read their principal primary sources in
  accessible digital editions, recording locators (volume and entry).
* Checked access status and reuse terms for each candidate's sources, for
  candidate image material, and for candidate structured-data sources.
* Checked every URL cited below with an HTTP request on 2026-09-23 (see
  <<verification>>).

Scope explicitly not performed, per the pass's bounds: no purchases, no account
creation, no specialist contact, no archival or library visit, no
interlibrary-loan request, no route reconstruction, no new research beyond the
shortlist, and no editorial approval of any content.

Method notes that matter for reading the findings:

* "Accessible" means reachable in this orb without payment, login, or
  institutional affiliation. Paywalled or print-only works are recorded as
  bibliography candidates with their access limitation stated.
* Digital transcriptions of classical texts (Wikisource, university sites) are
  convenient surrogates, not critical editions. Locators below were verified in
  the transcription named; before publication, each locator must be re-checked
  against a critical edition, because these transcriptions are unproofread and
  use placeholders (`■`) for damaged or unavailable characters.
* A source that reports an event is evidence that the report exists; it is not
  by itself proof that the event occurred as reported. Reports written long
  after the event, self-reports, and hagiographic biographies are labelled as
  such below (RFD 2's attestation rule).

== Candidate A — the 717–718 embassy (押使 多治比縣守)

=== Sources, availability, and access

Two independent court traditions plus one archaeological object:

* *續日本紀* — appointment (霊亀二年八月癸亥), substitution of the ambassador
  (同年九月丙子), 節刀 (養老元年三月己酉), tax relief for the mission's sailors
  (同年十一月甲辰), report of return (養老二年十月庚辰) and formal return
  (同年十二月壬申–甲戌).
  link:https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E7%BA%8C%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E7%B4%80/%E5%8D%B7%E7%AC%AC%E4%B8%83[卷第七] and
  link:https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E7%BA%8C%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E7%B4%80/%E5%8D%B7%E7%AC%AC%E5%85%AB[卷第八].
* *續日本紀* — the return of the students' transmission: 天平七年四月辛亥 lists
  what 下道朝臣眞備 (吉備真備) presented
  (link:https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E7%BA%8C%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E7%B4%80/%E5%8D%B7%E7%AC%AC%E5%8D%81%E4%BA%8C[卷第十二]).
* *舊唐書* / *新唐書* — the Tang-side notice, including the request for teachers
  of the classics and the purchase of books.
* *井真成墓誌* (734) — an excavated dated epitaph of a Japanese student who died
  in Tang.

Availability is good: all texts are public domain and readable free of charge;
the chronicle entries are day-dated and the object is dated by its own text.

=== Reuse rights

Texts: public domain (see <<source-classes>>). The epitaph's *text* is public
domain and published; its *photographs* are held with the object by 西北大学博物館
and no reuse licence was found. Two further rights questions arise only if the
chapter illustrates this candidate: the museum object and the illustrated
portraits in 菊池容斎『前賢故実』 (1868; public domain; verify per file before use)
— the latter are 19th-century imaginings and must be labelled as such.

=== Supported dates and geographic detail

Day-level dates are available for court events in Japan (appointment, 節刀,
return) and for the student's death and burial in Tang (開元廿二年正月 death;
二月四日 burial at 萬年縣滻水原). The embassy's own sea route and landing place
are *not* documented in the accessible sources: the chronicle records departure
preparations and the return, not the voyage. Modern summaries that place the
landfall near the Yangzi mouth or Hangzhou present it explicitly as an inference
(for example the reference-work article 多治比県守,
link:https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%A4%9A%E6%B2%BB%E6%AF%94%E7%9C%8C%E5%AE%88[ja.wikipedia]).
The
chapter could therefore show dated events in Japan and in the Tang capital, but
must not draw an outbound route.

=== Identifiable people and places

People (documented): 多治比縣守 (押使), 阿倍安麻呂 (appointed 大使, replaced),
大伴山守 (大使), 藤原馬養 (副使), 吉備真備, 阿倍仲麻呂, 玄昉 (monk), 井真成
(student), 坂合部大分 (earlier ambassador who returned with this mission).
Places (documented): the Japanese court and Nara-area sites, 蓋山 (rite before
departure), the Tang court and capital (the 鴻臚寺 teaching and 仲滿's stay in
京師), 萬年縣滻水原 (burial place of 井真成).

=== Evidence: travel, encounters, learning/adaptation (separately)

*Travel* — attested at the level of departure and return only; no voyage
itinerary. The chronicle adds a striking negative fact: on the return it records
"this time the envoys suffered no losses" (此度使人略無闕亡), together with the
return of 坂合部大分.

*Encounters* — attested, but briefly, and with a source conflict to resolve.
舊唐書 places a request for scholars to lecture on the classics in the early
Kaiyuan period (開元初), with 四門助教趙玄默 ordered to teach at 鴻臚寺, and adds that
the envoys spent their gifts on books to carry home and that 朝臣仲滿 (阿倍仲麻呂)
stayed. 新唐書 repeats the request and the book purchase but attributes that
embassy to 粟田真人, whose embassy belongs to 701–704 — which conflicts with the
Japanese record naming 多治比縣守 for 717. The association of the 趙玄默 episode with
the 717 mission is therefore a scholarly reading, not a settled attribution. No
dated audience protocol survives.

*Learning/transmission* — the strongest single item in this candidate. 續日本紀
天平七年四月辛亥 lists what 吉備真備 presented on return: 唐禮 130 volumes,
大衍暦經 1 volume and 大衍暦立成 12 volumes, a shadow-measuring iron rule, a
bronze pitch-pipe, 樂書要録 10 volumes, and bows and arrows. 續日本紀 also records
that in the following month "the returning envoys and Tang men performed Tang and
Silla music and spear-play" at court, and that an envoy presented a six-volume
record of questions and answers (請益秦大麻呂問答).

*Adaptation* — a separately dated later event, not an inference from the mission:
續日本紀 天平寶字七年八月戊子 records "the Gihō calendar was abolished and the
Dayan calendar first used" (廢儀鳳暦始用大衍暦) — 28 years after the calendar text
arrived. This is exactly the kind of separately sourced adaptation claim RFD 1
asks for, but it is one node, not a chain.

=== Disputes and uncertainty

* 井真成's status is disputed: whether he held office in Tang (the 贈尚衣奉御
  title), whether the epitaph's drafting involved a Japanese hand, and whether
  阿倍仲麻呂's circle arranged the honours. These are scholarly arguments
  reported in the literature (the 専修大学・西北大学 joint-project volume, 2005, print;
  summary in the reference-work article
  link:https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%BA%95%E7%9C%9F%E6%88%90[井真成]), not
  settled facts.
* Widely repeated figures for the mission's size (for example 557 people in four
  ships) could *not* be verified in the accessible primary text in this pass;
  續日本紀 records the tax relief but not a headcount. Treat such numbers as
  unverified until a scholarly edition supplies them.
* 吉備真備 attracts legendary attributions (the game of go, military texts,
  divination) that the sources do not support and that must be excluded.

=== Coverage model, gaps, and specialist review

A workable coverage model would be the embassy as a *transmission* story: 716–719
departure and return, the students' long stay, the 735 return with an itemized
list, and the 763 calendar change as a labelled coda node. Chief
gaps: no voyage documentation, no dated encounter protocol, and dependence on a
single chronicle for the item list. Optional specialist review: a Japanese
ancient-historian for the 735 list's interpretation (what the items imply and
what they do not), and a scholar of Tang ritual and calendar history for the 763
change.

== Candidate B — the 752–754 embassy (大使 藤原清河)

=== Sources, availability, and access

The best-corroborated of the three:

* *續日本紀* — appointment (天平勝寶二年九月己酉), audience and 節刀 with
  promotions including the student 藤原刷雄 (天平勝寶四年三月庚辰, 閏三月丙辰),
  the return reports (天平勝寶六年正月: 大伴古麻呂's return with 鑒眞・法進 and
  eight others; the report on 吉備真備's ship; 古麻呂's own report of the Tang
  New Year audience), the rewards and the fourth ship's arrival (天平勝寶六年四月
  壬申・癸未), and the later fates (寶龜十年二月乙亥; 五月丙寅).
  link:https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E7%BA%8C%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E7%B4%80/%E5%8D%B7%E7%AC%AC%E5%8D%81%E5%85%AB[卷第十八],
  link:https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E7%BA%8C%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E7%B4%80/%E5%8D%B7%E7%AC%AC%E5%8D%81%E4%B9%9D[卷第十九],
  link:https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/%E7%BA%8C%E6%97%A5%E6%9C%AC%E7%B4%80/%E5%8D%B7%E7%AC%AC%E5%8D%85%E4%BA%94[卷第三十五].
* *唐大和上東征傳* — the continuous voyage narrative and the transmission list
  (<<bib-toseiden>>).
* *續日本紀* 卷第廿一 and 卷第廿四 — the 758 title and precept-study order, and
  the 763 death notice with a biographical summary of 鑒眞's crossing.
* *舊唐書* / *新唐書* — the Tang-side notices: 舊唐書 records that "in the twelfth
  year of Tianbao they again sent tribute," while 新唐書 records 朝衡's return to
  court that year; both give 朝衡's career in outline.

=== Reuse rights

Texts are public domain, and — unusually for this period — so are relevant
images: the 13th-century illustrated scroll *東征伝絵巻* is available on Wikimedia
Commons as public-domain files
(link:https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:T%C5%8Dseiden_Emaki[category];
for example link:https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tosei_Eden_(Toshodaiji).jpg[Tosei Eden (Toshodaiji)]),
and a 1949 photograph of the 鑒眞 portrait statue is also marked public domain
(link:https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ganjin_wajyo_portrait.JPG[Commons file]).
Two cautions: the scroll is a *later illustrated copy* and is a design reference,
not evidence of the 8th-century events; and any modern reconstruction map, such
as link:https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Travels_of_Jianzhen.png[Travels of Jianzhen]
(CC BY-SA 4.0), is a Wikimedian's reconstruction that must not be presented as a
sourced route. The 唐招提寺 statue itself is a National Treasure whose own
photography may be restricted; museum photographs need per-file clearance.

=== Supported dates and geographic detail

This candidate has by far the richest dated geography. From the 東征傳's voyage
passage: the envoys visit 鑒眞 at 延光寺 in 揚州 on 天寶十二載十月十五日; 鑒眞 leaves
龍興寺 on the 19th; the four ships sail on 十一月十五日 from 蘇州黃泗浦; ships 1 and 2
reach 阿兒奈波島 on the 21st; on 十二月六日 ship 1 is aground and ship 2 heads for
多禰島; on the 7th ship 2 reaches 益救島; it leaves on the 18th and lands at 薩摩國
阿多郡秋妻屋浦 on the 20th, entering 大宰府 on the 26th. The chronicle adds
吉備真備's ship reaching 益久嶋 on 天平勝寶五年十二月七日 and drifting to 紀伊國牟漏埼,
the fourth ship's arrival at 薩摩國石籬浦 (判官 布勢人主), and 清河's ship drifting to
驩州 on Tang's southern frontier. On the Tang side, 古麻呂's report places the
New Year audience at 蓬萊宮含元殿 on the first day of the first month of 天寶十二載.

The modern identifications of 阿兒奈波島 (Okinawa Island), 多禰島 (Tanegashima),
益救島 (Yakushima) and 秋妻屋浦 (Ata district, Satsuma; proposed as 坊津) come from
reference works and research articles, not from the sources, and must be labelled
as identifications (<<bib-places>>).

=== Identifiable people and places

People: 藤原清河 (ambassador), 大伴古麻呂 and 吉備真備 (vice-envoys), 藤原刷雄
(student), 阿倍仲麻呂 (in Tang; joined the return), 布勢人主 (fourth ship's
判官), 川部酒麻呂 (fourth ship's helmsman, in the chronicle's account of a
shipboard fire), 鑒眞 with 法進 and 思託, 榮叡 and 普照 (monks who had sought a
precept master), the Tang emperor and general 呉懷實, and 王維, whose parting poem
for 晁衡 (阿倍仲麻呂) is part of the literary record (poem text not verified in this
pass). Places: 難波, 大宰府,
益救島/益久嶋, 多禰島, 阿兒奈波島, 秋妻屋浦, 石籬浦, 紀伊國牟漏埼, 揚州, 蘇州黃泗浦,
驩州, 長安/蓬萊宮含元殿, 東大寺, 唐招提寺.

=== Evidence: travel, encounters, learning/adaptation (separately)

*Travel* — attested with dated legs and named landfalls (above). The voyage
narrative rests on the 東征傳, a single near-contemporary biography; the court
chronicle independently corroborates the return of the ships and the fact of
divergent landfalls, not the day-by-day sequence.

*Encounters* — two kinds, both dated. (1) The envoys' invitation to 鑒眞 at
延光寺 in 揚州 (東征傳). (2) The Tang New Year audience at 含元殿, reported to the
Japanese court by 古麻呂 himself, including the seating-order dispute with the
Silla envoy and its resolution by 呉懷實. The second is a self-report and should
be labelled as such; it is also unusually concrete (date, hall, seating,
intervention).

*Learning/transmission* — the 東征傳 lists the items 鑒眞 brought: "forty-eight
items" (合四十八部), including the *Tiantai zhiguan famen* in forty scrolls,
Xuanzang's *Xiyu ji* in twelve scrolls, and calligraphy by 王羲之 and 王獻之, as
well as images and ritual objects. This is an itemized transmission list of the
same kind as Candidate A's, but attached to the same voyage as the travel and
encounter evidence.

*Adaptation* — separately dated, later, and partly independently corroborated:
the 東征傳 describes a precepts platform (戒壇) at the 盧遮那殿 in the fourth month
of 754, imperial ordinations, 440-odd ordinations of novices, and 80-odd senior
monks re-ordaining; 續日本紀 (卷第廿一, 天平寶字二年) independently records that
鑒眞 was given the title 大和上 and that monks wishing to study the precepts were
ordered to study under him; the 東征傳 dates the founding of 唐招提寺 to 寶字三年
八月一日 and 鑒眞's death to 寶字七年五月六日. Note the chronicle's death notice
says he was 77, while the biography says 76 — a documented discrepancy to publish
as such.

=== Disputes and uncertainty

* The ordination events of 754 are recorded in the biography but *not* in the
  court chronicle's accessible text, which this pass checked for the relevant
  terms. They are attested, but single-source.
* The voyage sequence is single-source; the number of ships and personnel is
  partly reconstructed. The chronicle's own figure of "222 persons below the
  envoys" receiving rewards is a rare documented headcount for this mission.
* Textual variants across editions should be expected: 益救島/益久嶋 and
  胡麿/古麻呂; and the day of 古麻呂's report appears as 正月十一日 in the Wikisource
  transcription and as 正月十二日 in the searchable preview of the 専修大学 PDF, so
  the date must be fixed against a critical edition.
* 清河's fate rests on a report made 25 years later (779); 阿倍仲麻呂's death is
  reported to the Japanese court in 779, and the accessible Chinese chronicles do
  not state a death year in the passages checked.

=== Coverage model, gaps, and specialist review

Coverage model for this candidate if selected: 750–754 primary mission window
(appointment, dispatch, audience, return voyage, arrival, ordination); included
coda nodes outside that window at 758, 759, 763, and 779; the outbound voyage of
752 excluded. Chief gaps: the *outbound* voyage of 752 is undocumented in these
sources; the 754 ordinations lack an independent court record; the place
identifications are scholarly proposals. Optional specialist review: a maritime
or ancient historian for the return-voyage landfalls and for what can and cannot
be drawn as a route; a Buddhist-studies specialist for the ordination and lineage
claims and the biography's hagiographic layers; a Tang historian for the 含元殿
audience and court protocol.

== Candidate C — the 804–805 embassy (大使 藤原葛野麻呂)

=== Sources, availability, and access

* *日本後紀* 卷第十二–第十三 — the mission's appointment, audience, 節刀, the
  report that the four ships put to sea in the seventh month of the previous
  year (延暦23年), with two ships driven back and two unaccounted for, prompting an
  inquiry sent to Silla, and the 805 return of 葛野麻呂 and 最澄's court
  appearances. Crucially, these volumes are labelled *逸文* (reconstructed
  fragments), not the original chronicle.
* *御請来目録* (806) — Kūkai's own catalogue, available as a free Taishō text
  (T55n2161) and as a Kyoto University printed copy whose images may be reused
  with attribution.
* *叡山大師伝* (919) — Saichō's biography, public domain on Wikisource.
* *舊唐書* / *新唐書* — both name 橘逸勢 (舊唐書: 橘免勢) and 空海 as students who
  stayed, and record the later request that they return.

Accessibility is good — arguably better than expected — but the Japanese court
record is a reconstruction.

=== Reuse rights

Texts are public domain; the Kyoto University copy explicitly permits image reuse
with a credit line. Later hagiographic portraits and temple photographs need
per-file checking; the standard English monographs on Saichō and Kūkai are
copyright-protected and usable only as citations.

=== Supported dates and geographic detail

Documented: the 804 dispatch and the ships' divergent landfalls (the chronicle
reports two ships driven back and two unaccounted for, with an inquiry sent to
Silla); Kūkai's own catalogue is dated 大同元年十月廿二日; the Tang chronicle names
the students. Detailed
geography — which ship reached 福州長渓県, which reached 明州, and the overland
movements — comes from the travellers' own records and later biographies rather
than from the court chronicle, and this pass did not verify those itineraries in
their primary texts.

=== Identifiable people and places

People: 藤原葛野麻呂 (ambassador), 石川道益 (vice-envoy, died at 明州 per the
chronicle), 最澄, 空海, 橘逸勢, 恵果 (Kūkai's Tang master), and the emperor 桓武.
Places: 肥前国松浦郡 (departure area), 福州, 明州, 長安, 天台山, and on return
大宰府 and the capital.

=== Evidence: travel, encounters, learning/adaptation (separately)

*Travel* — attested at the level of ship departures and divergent landfalls, with
the chronicle's own note that two ships were driven back; detailed itineraries
rest on the travellers' records and later biographies, which this pass did not
verify.

*Encounters* — the transmission accounts are largely self-reported: Kūkai's
catalogue and later biography describe his study under 恵果; Saichō's biography
describes his study and his return. No independent dated encounter record
comparable to Candidate B's audience report was located in this pass.

*Learning* — the catalogues are strong itemized evidence of what was brought
back, and Kūkai's is dated by its own colophon.

*Adaptation* — the strongest long-run institutional record of the three
(Tendai and Shingon foundations, ordination platforms, temple foundations), but
the relevant records are later, spread over decades, and require their own
sources; they are adaptation nodes far downstream of the voyage.

=== Disputes and uncertainty

* The Japanese court record survives only as 逸文; its entry placement and
  wording must be checked against a critical edition before citation.
* The learning accounts are self-reported and later hagiography accreted around
  both figures.
* Mission ordinals are inconsistent across references (this pass saw the same
  mission called the 16th, 17th, and 18th), so identifiers should use years, not
  numbers.

=== Coverage model, gaps, and specialist review

A chapter here would need a coverage model far wider than the voyage (for example
804–823) to reach the adaptation claims, and would rest its most vivid material
on self-reports. Chief gaps: fragmentary court record, unverified itineraries in
primary texts, and heavy reliance on later biographies. Optional specialist
review: a historian of Japanese Buddhism for the transmission and lineage claims,
and a scholar of Heian court records for the 逸文 reconstruction.

== Also considered, not advanced

The 838–847 embassy (大使 藤原常嗣) with the monk 圓仁 was considered and not
advanced as one of the three. 圓仁's *入唐求法巡礼行記* is the single richest
travel and encounter source of the whole period and is freely readable (text via
link:https://cbetaonline.dila.edu.tw/zh/B0095_001[CBETA, B18n0095]; the standard
English translation by Reischauer is copyright-protected), but it documents nine
years and hundreds of places — far beyond one bounded mission — and the mission's
own learning/adaptation links to Japan are diffuse and later. It is a strong
candidate for a *later* chapter, and its diary is a candidate source for any
chapter's travel methodology.

== Recommendation

**Recommend Candidate B, the 752–754 embassy of 藤原清河, with the coverage model:
primary mission window 750–754; included coda nodes outside that window at 758,
759, 763, and 779; the outbound voyage of 752 is excluded (see
<<coverage-model>>).**

Rationale against RFD 1's requirement that travel, encounters, and subsequent
adaptation each be explorable as separate, sourced relationships:

* It is the only candidate whose *travel* record carries dated legs and named
  landfalls, and whose landfall evidence is corroborated at the fact level by the
  court chronicle (the ships returned separately; the fourth ship reached
  Satsuma; 清河's ship was lost to the south).
* It is the only candidate with a *dated, placed encounter*: the Tang New Year
  audience at 含元殿, reported by a named envoy, including a protocol dispute.
  Candidate A's encounter evidence is a brief notice; Candidate C's is
  self-reported study.
* Its *transmission* evidence is itemized (the 48 items), and its *adaptation*
  evidence is separately dated and partly independent of the biography (the 758
  court order, an included coda node outside the primary window).
* It has the best reuse position: the texts are public domain, and the
  illustrated scroll images are public domain, so the chapter can be illustrated
  without a rights purchase — subject to labelling the scroll as a later copy.
* Its risks are nameable and bounded: single-source voyage detail, one
  single-source ordination episode, and scholarly (not source-stated) place
  identifications.

Candidate A remains a legitimate choice if the owner decides the launch chapter
should foreground *learning and adaptation* over travel; its transmission and
calendar evidence is precise, and its excavated epitaph gives the chapter a human
anchor. Its weakness is that the voyage and encounter layers would be thin, and
the chapter would lean on one chronicle entry.

Candidate C is not recommended for the launch chapter: its court record is a
reconstruction, its most vivid material is self-reported, and its adaptation
claims lie decades downstream.

=== Optional specialist review

Not mandatory under RFD 2's owner-led review, and not a gate. Where it would add
most value, in order: (1) a maritime or ancient historian for the return-voyage
landfalls, what may be drawn as a route, and the reconstruction of the four
ships' courses; (2) a Buddhist-studies specialist for the ordination, lineage,
and transmission claims and for separating hagiography from record; (3) a Tang
court historian for the 含元殿 audience and protocol; (4) a philologist to fix
the 東征傳 and 續日本紀 editions and the textual variants before content freeze.
