= RFD 2 authoring format and content layout

== Provenance and supersession

link:README.adoc[RFD 2] approved on 2026-10-10 a compact authoring format: "a
folder per chapter with one small file per record", compiled at build time
into link:CONTRACTS.adoc[the v0 contract]. That decision left the concrete
shape of the files open, and the compiler and the migration are still open
items in link:IMPLEMENTATION.adoc[the RFD 2 checklist]. This record specifies
that shape. It was opened on 2026-10-14 from a discussion with the owner.

It contains two kinds of statement, and says which is which:

* *Decided* statements carry the owner's decision and its date.
* *Proposed* statements are recommendations awaiting the owner's word in this
  record's review. They are not implemented and not final until marked
  decided.

For the content layout only, this record supersedes "a folder per chapter" in
RFD 2's *Authoring format* bullet (<<Decision: world first, chapters as views>>).
Everything else in that decision stands, and RFD 2's text is not rewritten to
match: one small file per record, flat fields of simple types, compilation into
the v0 contract, Git history as the revision history, one owner approval per
chapter, and the standardized content model, roles, and contribution surface.

== Where the content stands (2026-10-14, `main` at dd572bd)

* *Two chapters* live under `historical/chapters/<id>/` in three folders:
  `records/`, `sources/`, and `publication-sets/`.
  ** `tang-mission-750-754` has 29 events, 15 places, 12 actors, 9
     relationships, and 1 story.
  ** `qin-conquest-han-233-226` has 5 events, 4 places, 6 actors, 4
     relationships, and 1 story.
* *Every record is a full v0 document:* a revision envelope (author, change
  explanation, `revision_hash`), the content, claims pointing into the content
  by JSON pointer, and a publication block naming its set.
  ** The 86 records come to 12,658 lines of JSON, about 147 lines per record.
  ** `event-akimeya-landfall-753.json` states one cited fact in 103 lines.
* *Sources are per chapter,* so both chapters carry their own `wikidata.json`.
  A place or actor that two chapters share would be two records with two ids.
* *Some chapter facts have no field and are inferred instead:*
  ** The chapter title is the leading clause of the story summary
     (`chapterTitle` in `src/lib/atlas/projection.ts`).
  ** An event's title is its story caption.
  ** The home view is the centre of the places' bounding box.
  ** The default window and the served list are TypeScript constants in
     `src/lib/atlas/chapter-registry.ts`.
* *The loader emits one script per content file.*
  `src/lib/atlas/chapter-catalog.ts` lazily globs every JSON file, so Vite emits
  one JavaScript chunk per file. A client build of `main` on 2026-10-14 emitted
  121 scripts, and 103 of them are content: 86 records, 15 sources, and 2
  publication sets. The deployed file count therefore grows with every record,
  against the static host's 20,000-file limit
  (link:../0003/GLOBE_RENDERER.adoc[the globe renderer record] measured the
  whole artifact at 944 files, almost all globe tile bundles).
* *RFD 1 and RFD 5 ask for things the current authoring and rendering path does
  not supply.* v0 geometry already permits routes with dated waypoints and
  polygons with validity dates; the gaps are in selecting and interpreting
  that geometry, not all in contract expressivity.
  ** Journeys drawn through dated stops. The parts exist (`part_of`, place
     roles, dated waypoints, and the `schematic_link` interpretation), but
     membership does not say which places are actual stops or establish their
     historical order.
  ** Dated political-control snapshots: v0 can hold a dated polygon, but no
     authoring kind or renderer gives it the actor-control semantics RFD 1
     distinguishes from religious presence and cultural influence.
  ** Images that depict a record: only `source.kind: media` exists, with no
     link from an image to what it depicts and no depiction label.

== Decision: world first, chapters as views

Decided by the owner on 2026-10-14.

Every record exists once, in a project-wide tree organized by kind. A chapter
is a manifest that points at records; it does not contain them.

Record ids are unique project-wide. An event can appear in more than one
chapter's story: 鑒眞's 753 crossing belongs to the Tang embassy and to any
chapter on the transmission of the precepts.

That is the extent of the decision. The concrete directory inventory and
contract-fixture placement, filename-to-id rule, YAML format,
chapter-manifest fields, compiler output, and migration below were proposals on
2026-10-14; <<Decisions of 2026-10-18>> records which of them the owner has
since decided. In particular, this decision neither relocates the existing v0
schemas and vectors nor changes the current publication gate.

Why: the owner's stated aim is exploring an era across the globe and zooming
freely (recorded in link:../0003/README.adoc[RFD 3]). An era view shows several
chapters' records at once, and search and the chapter ribbon of RFD 5 are
already cross-chapter. A chapter-first tree would have to duplicate every
shared place, actor, source, and event, or reach into another chapter's folder
for it.

What it costs:

* An edit to a shared record re-opens the approval of every chapter that
  reaches it. <<Decided: per-chapter approval pins a content digest>> makes
  that automatic rather than a matter of memory.
* Kind folders grow large, which the proposed subfolders absorb.
* The migration effort is the same as the migration already planned, and at
  86 records it is the cheapest it will be.

== Decisions of 2026-10-18

The owner decided six of the proposals below on 2026-10-18, one at a time, in
the project thread that followed PR #23. Each section keeps its text and is now
headed *Decided*; this section records what was chosen, the alternatives the
owner weighed, and what the owner added.

. *Directory and identifiers:* as proposed.
. *The record file:* YAML as proposed, after weighing Markdown with YAML front
  matter, JSON with the same flat fields, and AsciiDoc. AsciiDoc remains a
  candidate for an optional companion file if a chapter gains a long essay with
  footnoted sources; records stay YAML.
. *Date notation:* as proposed, after weighing EDTF and separate structured
  fields. The compiler may convert EDTF on import; authors write the short
  strings.
. *The chapter manifest:* as proposed, with the story listed by event id. A
  story by query was weighed and not chosen, because it orders same-year events
  that the contract deliberately leaves unordered and it changes an approved
  chapter without anyone touching it.
. *Digest approval:* as proposed, with two additions by the owner:
  ** a `CODEOWNERS` rule that requires the owner's review on every chapter
     manifest, because a digest proves the content has not changed since
     approval but not who wrote the approval; and
  ** an approvals command, `pnpm approvals`, that lists every chapter as
     approved, draft, or awaiting approval and, for one awaiting approval, shows
     the records added, removed, or changed since the approved content, field by
     field, with the new digest. It finds the approved content by walking Git
     history to the revision whose closure matches the manifest's digest.
. *The compiler and migration:* as proposed, and the existing content is made
  consistent with the new format, including its sources
  (<<Decided: one Wikidata source, pinned per citation>>).

Still proposed: journey curation, territories, media, per-claim credit, and
contract v1 (<<Proposed: journeys, territories, and media>>). The existing
publication gate stays authoritative until the digest check is built, enforces
the same invariant, and the owner approves the switch; no step of this work
records an approval for the owner.

== Decided: directory and identifier realization

One realization of the decided world-first layout is:

----
historical/
  v0/                            existing schemas, vectors, and synthetic examples
  events/
    tang-embassy/akimeya-landfall-753.yaml
  places/
  actors/
  relationships/
  sources/
  territories/                   with contract v1, see below
  media/                         with contract v1, see below
  chapters/
    tang-mission-750-754.yaml    the chapter manifest
----

* *Ids.* The id is the file name. The folder supplies the kind, so
  `events/…/akimeya-landfall-753.yaml` is `lks:event:akimeya-landfall-753`, and
  no id is written inside the file, which keeps the two from drifting apart.
* *Uniqueness.* The decided ids are unique across the whole project, because
  link:../0005/README.adoc[RFD 5]'s search and deep links (`?event=`,
  `?place=`) span chapters. The proposed compiler rejects duplicate full ids.
* *Subfolders.* Subfolders under a kind folder are free for authors' tidiness.
  They never change an id, and the build fails on two files with the same
  name under one kind.

== Decided: the chapter manifest

One YAML file per chapter owns every fact the projection now infers or
hard-codes. It replaces the story record as an authoring input (the compiler
still emits the v0 story), and it replaces the coverage constants and served
list in `chapter-registry.ts`.

[source,yaml]
----
# historical/chapters/tang-mission-750-754.yaml
title: The Tang embassy of 750–754
summary: >-
  The 750–754 embassy of 藤原朝臣清河, from appointment through the return
  voyage and the 754 ordinations.
window: 750..754          # the window the chapter opens on
span: 750..779            # the story, including its coda nodes
coverage: …               # today's coverage_note
home: { center: [125.0, 31.5], scale: region }   # optional; else the bounding box
hero: hero-landfall-753   # a media record, once media exists
story:
  - appointment-750                                    # caption defaults to the event's title
  - japan-tang-embassy: The diplomatic mission from Japan to Tang
  - akimeya-landfall-753
approval:
  digest: sha256:…
  by: Darwin D Wu
  on: 2026-10-20
  reviewer: …             # a specialist reviewer, when there is one
----

* *Approval states.* The compiler validates every manifest carrying an
  approval before it derives the served list. A matching approval is served;
  no approval is a draft and is omitted; a stale or malformed approval fails
  the build and is never silently filtered out. Removing the approval from a
  chapter already served is a withdrawal, not a draft edit, and requires a
  separately recorded owner decision.
* *Titles and captions.* Events gain a `title` field, and a story caption
  becomes an optional override of it.

== Decided: per-chapter approval pins a content digest

The approval records a digest of the chapter's content closure: the manifest
and every record and source it reaches, transitively. It does not record a
commit, because a commit cannot name itself and a squash merge rewrites it.

The proposed digest payload is canonical JSON containing the manifest without
its entire `approval` block, followed by each reachable file's kind, logical id,
and normalized content in a deterministic order. It includes source metadata.
Generated revision, publication-set, and mirror fields are not inputs. Revision
metadata is a deterministic product of the content payload; publication
metadata may copy the separately validated approver, date, and reviewer from
the approval block, but cannot feed back into the content digest. The compiler's
tests must pin the canonicalization before this proposal can replace the
current gate, so writing an approval can never change the digest it approves.

The build recomputes the digest. A mismatch fails the build for a chapter
that carries an approval, before any served-list filtering, so any change to an
approved chapter, including an edit to a shared place it uses, reaches readers
only after a new approval.
This is the check RFD 2 lists as "no change to an approved chapter reaching
the build without a new approval". It replaces the per-set digest ledger in
link:PUBLICATION_APPROVAL.adoc[the publication approval gate] only after the
owner approves this replacement and its implementation enforces the same
invariant. Until then, the existing gate and approved content remain
authoritative. The ledger stays in the tree as the record of how the first two
chapters were approved.

== Decided: the record file

YAML, flat fields, and each citation attached to the field it supports. The
compiler generates the claim ids and JSON pointers the contract needs, so an
author never writes `asserts: /places/0`.

The event that is 103 lines today:

[source,yaml]
----
# historical/events/tang-embassy/akimeya-landfall-753.yaml
title: The landfall at 秋妻屋浦
summary: The second ship lands at 薩摩國阿多郡秋妻屋浦 at noon on 天寶十二載十二月廿日.
types: [journey]
part_of: return-voyage-753
date:
  value: 753-12-20
  calendar: chinese_lunisolar
  as_written: 廿日乙酉午時
  cite: { source: toseiden, at: 廿日乙酉午時，第二舟著薩摩國阿多郡秋妻屋浦 }
places:
  - place: akimeya
    role: site
    cite: { source: toseiden, at: 廿日乙酉午時，第二舟著薩摩國阿多郡秋妻屋浦 }
notes: >-
  The landing place is attested; its modern identification is a cited
  interpretation on the place.
----

An abridged place syntax example, not an evidence-complete migration fixture:

[source,yaml]
----
# historical/places/yangzhou.yaml
name:
  form: 揚州
  lang: lzh
  script: Hani
  roman: Yangzhou
  scheme: pinyin
  cite: { source: toseiden, at: 天寶十二載 十月十五日壬午; 十月［十］九日戌時 }
types: [settlement]
at:
  point: [119.4363, 32.3912]
  basis: display_anchor
  precision: unspecified
  cite: { source: wikidata, at: Q57906 (P625) }
modern: [{ form: 揚州市, lang: zh-Hans }]
----

Conventions:

* *Basis.* `basis` defaults to `attested`. An interpretation says
  `basis: interpretation` and cites the scholarship, and compiles to the
  contract's `cited_interpretation`.
* *Locators.* A source declares its locator kind once (`entry`, `page`,
  `juan`, `dataset_row`), so a citation gives only `at:`.
* *Disputes.* A disputed value carries `alternatives:` beside the main value,
  each with its own `cite`. The main value stays the editorial choice, as RFD 2
  requires.
* *Credit.* `contributed_by` and `reviewed_by` are set at the file level and
  can be overridden per field. They are new to the contract (v0 has
  `reviewed_by` only on licences), so they arrive with v1, below.
* *Vocabularies.* The geometry interpretations, place and actor types, roles,
  and relationship types keep the v0 vocabulary. The format is shorter, not
  looser.
* *Editor support.* A JSON Schema for each kind of record file gives editors
  completion and inline errors, and it is the first CI check.
* *Existing source identities.* Migration first inventories logical-id
  collisions. The two current `wikidata` sources, for example, record different
  read dates, hashes, review dates, and usage notes and therefore cannot be
  merged automatically. They either become distinct, versioned authoring
  sources whose approved v0 metadata compiles unchanged, or an owner-reviewed
  consolidation explicitly supersedes them. No collision resolution may erase
  approved provenance merely to satisfy global uniqueness.

== Decided: date notation

Dates are short strings, compiled into the contract's existing `interval`,
`precision`, and `mode` objects, so the 187 vectors keep holding.

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Written |Means
|`753-12-20` / `753-12` / `753` |Day, month, or year precision
|`750..754` |A two-sided range; `mode: window` (one occurrence somewhere inside) or `mode: duration` (activity across it), with `instant` the default for a single date
|`233 BCE`, `233-10 BCE`, `233 BCE..226 BCE` |Dates before the common era, with the era spelled out
|`calendar:` and `as_written:` |The source calendar and the source's own expression, as v0 already keeps them
|===

BCE is spelled out rather than written as astronomical years. EDTF writes
233 BCE as `-0232`, which is the off-by-one the contract's `era` field exists
to prevent. The atlas's internal year ordinals do not change.

== Proposed: journeys, territories, and media

* *Journey membership is not a route.* `part_of` can suggest candidate events,
  but the compiler must not connect all of their places or sort them into a
  historical route automatically. A context place may be only an intended
  destination; two events may be separate, unresolved accounts; disputed dates
  must remain disputed; and v0 deliberately does not order same-year events at
  sub-year precision. An explicitly curated schematic sequence remains the
  proposal, with rules still needed for actual-stop eligibility, branches,
  account identity, and unsupported order. A route reconstructed in scholarship
  is an explicit `route:` on the event, with its own GeoJSON file and citation,
  compiled to `reconstructed_route`.
* *Territories are a proposed new authoring kind.* A territory file holds a
  dated control snapshot: whose control (an actor), the snapshot's date, a
  GeoJSON file, its basis, and its citation. v0 already admits a polygon with a
  validity date; the proposed kind adds the control semantics and authoring and
  rendering rules. RFD 1 forbids interpolating between snapshots.
* *Media is a new kind.* A media file holds the file, the source that carries
  its licence, the record ids it `depicts`, and a depiction label (for example,
  "a later depiction, not evidence").

Dedicated territory records with actor-control semantics, media depiction
links, and per-claim credit are new rules. Under AGENTS.md they mean a new
contract version, *v1*, with new vectors, not an edit to v0. The proposal is to
specify them in this record now and implement v1 with the first chapter that
draws a territory or shows an image, so that v1 lands with real content to test
against. Until then the compiler targets v0.

== Decided: the compiler and what ships

* *Compiler.* A Node script, run by `pnpm build` and by the contract tests,
  reads the tree and emits the complete v0 representation: one synthetic
  revision per record, a generated story record for each manifest, generated
  publication sets and record mirrors, and computed hashes. Those generated
  artifacts remain necessary while `Publication.checkAll`, the vectors, and
  `projection.ts` consume v0, even though authors no longer edit them.
* *What ships.* The compiler writes one compiled chapter bundle containing
  those v0 documents, and `chapter-catalog.ts` imports that bundle instead of
  globbing record files. Content then costs one deployed file per chapter
  however many records the chapter has, instead of one per record (103 files
  today, and thousands once chapters reach the 200+ events the RFD 2 checklist
  targets for Tang). Authoring record files never ship.
* *Migration.* A script converts the two chapters from JSON to the record
  files. The first preservation target is compiled v0 output identical to
  today's documents apart from synthetic revision metadata. Any unavoidable
  source-id collision resolution is listed separately and reviewed before it
  changes an id or approved metadata; passing validation alone is not evidence
  of a lossless migration.
* *Retirement.* Hand-authored publication sets, the ledger, and
  `publication-approval.test.ts` retire only after an owner-approved digest gate
  enforces the equivalent publication invariant. The compiler continues to
  generate the v0 story, publication sets, and mirrors until their consumers
  migrate to a later contract.

== Decided: one Wikidata source, pinned per citation

Decided by the owner on 2026-10-18. This supersedes, for Wikidata, the
*Existing source identities* convention above, which would have kept the two
chapters' Wikidata files as two dated sources.

Both chapters carry a `wikidata` source today. They are different reads: the
Tang file records a read on 2026-09-24 for external identifiers and label
anchors, and the Qin file a read on 2026-09-25 for one label anchor. Neither a
read date nor a content date identifies what was read. A read date says when
we looked and collides when two reads fall on one day; a dataset has no single
content date, because each item has its own edit history.

* *One source.* There is one `wikidata` source record. It states the dataset,
  its licence (CC0 1.0), and its terms once.
* *The revision is in the citation.* Every Wikidata item edit has a permanent
  revision id, so each citation pins the exact data it used:
  `at: Q57906 (P625), revision 2234567890`. Any number of reads on any day
  resolve to exact content.
* *Existing citations are re-pinned, not guessed.* Migration resolves each
  cited item's revision through the Wikidata API and checks that the cited
  value (the coordinate or identifier the record uses) still equals the value
  the approved record holds. A citation whose value has changed is listed for
  the owner rather than re-pinned silently.
* *This is new content for the approved chapters.* The consolidated source and
  the re-pinned citations change what the Tang and Qin approvals cover. They
  are prepared as drafts; the approved content stays served until the owner
  approves the new content, under whichever gate is authoritative then.

== Implemented on 2026-10-18

The decisions of 2026-10-18 are implemented in PR #25. This section records
what was built, the evidence that the migration lost nothing, and the details
the decisions left open, which the implementation chose and the owner reviews
with the change.

*What was built.*

* The tree: 84 record files under `historical/{places,actors,events,relationships}/`
  (subfolders `tang/` and `qin/`), 14 source files under `historical/sources/`,
  and two manifests in `historical/chapters/`, each with the chapter's former
  README beside it as `<id>.md`. The two stories are the manifests' `story`
  blocks, so the chapters still publish 86 records. Every file is checked
  against a JSON Schema in `historical/schema/`.
* The compiler, `src/lib/historical/authoring/`, emits the complete v0
  representation. The site gets it from a Vite plugin
  (`scripts/historical-chapters.mjs`) as one lazily loaded chunk per approved
  chapter; nothing compiled is written to disk. The contract and chapter tests
  compile the same bundles through `src/lib/historical/chapter.ts`.
* The gate: `publication-approval.test.ts` now fails the build for a stale
  approval or a misdated one, and requires the served chapters to be exactly the
  approved ones. `pnpm approvals` lists and compares chapters as decided, and
  `.github/CODEOWNERS` requires the owner's review on every manifest.
* The hand-written JSON trees under `historical/chapters/<id>/` are removed.

*Migration evidence.* Before the site switched to the compiled chapters, a
check compiled each of the 86 records and compared it with the revision its
approved set published. Content, dispute, and every claim matched, with claims
compared by the field they assert; four records list claims for different
fields in another order, which carries no meaning. Every source's
`source_hash` was unchanged apart from the consolidated `wikidata` source. One
id changed: the Tang story `lks:story:tang-mission-750-779`, named after its old
file, is now `lks:story:tang-mission-750-754`, after its chapter. `pnpm
approvals` shows exactly these differences against the approved JSON sets: the
cards' text (code until now), the Wikidata source and its 17 pinned citations,
and the story id.

*How the 17 Wikidata citations were pinned.* The Wikidata API (`api.php`)
refused the requests with HTTP 429, so each item's history was read from its
`index.php?action=history` page and the data from `Special:EntityData` at the
chosen revision. Each citation names the revision that was current on the day
its chapter recorded the read (2026-09-24 for Tang, 2026-09-25 for Qin). For all
17, the item had no edit on that day, so the revision is unambiguous, and the
coordinate at that revision equals the record's to the record's precision. No
citation needed to be listed for the owner.

*Details the decisions left open.*

* A generated revision's id is `lks:rev:<file name>-<first 12 hex digits of
  the sha256 of its content, claims, and dispute>`, so it changes exactly when
  the record does. Its `created_at` is 2026-10-18, the day the record files
  were adopted, and its author is "Lekishi contributors", because who wrote a
  line is in Git history.
* An approved chapter's set is `lks:set:<chapter>-<first 12 hex digits of the
  digest>`, published at the approval's date by the approver, whose role is
  `owner`. Its review stays the contributors' `self_review`, and its notes say
  that the approval is an owner decision, not independent or specialist review.
* An approval's date is a UTC calendar day, no earlier than 2026-10-18 and no
  later than the day the build runs.
* A chapter without an approval is a draft. The site does not serve it. The
  contract and chapter tests check it as a preview set that names no owner, so
  its content is held to every rule before anyone approves it.
* `pnpm approvals approve <chapter>` needs an interactive terminal and the
  chapter id typed back. It names the approver from `git config user.name`
  unless `--by` names someone, and it writes only the manifest's `approval`
  block.

*Retirement.* The ledger's test is replaced by the digest gate in the same
change, and the ledger takes no further rows. The criterion above holds when
the owner approves this change and approves both chapters with `pnpm
approvals`. Until then the branch serves no chapter, and its chapter tests fail,
so it cannot be merged by mistake.

== Alternatives

Chapter folders with a shared catalog for places, actors, and sources only::
  Keeps a chapter's events in one folder, which is easy to browse, but still
  duplicates or cross-references events that belong to two stories, and it
  does not fit an era view across chapters. Not chosen by the owner on
  2026-10-14.

Keeping one JSON document per record in the v0 envelope::
  Changes nothing, and keeps a fact at about a hundred lines and a correction
  at a new revision, set, and ledger row. RFD 2 already rejected it on
  2026-10-10.

Markdown files with YAML front matter::
  Better for long prose, and GitHub renders them. The cards' narrative is a
  sentence or two today, so plain YAML is proposed; this is worth revisiting
  if card narratives grow beyond a paragraph.

A database::
  The data is relational, but a relational shape does not by itself call for a
  database server. Integrity (every id resolving) is a build and CI check. No
  reader queries anything live, because every join happens once, when the
  compiler assembles a chapter. Files keep every change a reviewable diff with
  history and credit, and keep an approval pinned to exact content. If authors
  need ad-hoc queries, the compiler can also write the tree into a local,
  ignored SQLite file on the author's machine and in CI. It is never deployed,
  and it needs no infrastructure. If a cross-chapter era view ever outgrows
  loading per-chapter bundles, SQLite compiled to WebAssembly can read a
  database shipped as a static file with HTTP range requests, which keeps the
  site static; that would be measured and decided then. A hosted database (for
  example Cloudflare D1, which needs a Worker) is a runtime and a provider, and
  stays reserved by RFD 2's stage 4 for many concurrent editors or for
  reader-facing data the build cannot precompute, with Git kept as the
  publication record.
