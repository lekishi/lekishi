This note goes with the draft chapter

candidate 4 of the 2026-10-21 chapter selection. It says what the sources
support, where the chapter had to make editorial choices, and what would raise
its quality before the owner approves it. The chapter is a draft: it has no
approval block, and dev and pull-request previews serve it.

== Verdict

Feasible, and a useful test chapter because its evidence is mostly physical.
The inscriptions are dated, located and still standing, so the chapter has 36
events across 33 new places from the Peshawar valley to Mysore and Lanka, with
the five Hellenistic kings of Rock Edict XIII as actors. What it lacks is an
absolute calendar: the inscriptions count years from Aśoka's consecration, and
every BCE year is a modern conversion. That makes it a good test of regnal
dating, of monuments that were moved after the event, and of legend kept apart
from inscription.

Smith died in 1920 and the book was published that year, so both sources are
public domain. They are quoted from the English Wikisource transcription,
which is CC BY-SA, so the records cite the work and its chapter, never the
page, as the register's Wikisource row requires. Every quotation in the records
was checked mechanically against the downloaded transcription (revisions are
listed in each source's notes).

Hultzsch's _Corpus Inscriptionum Indicarum_ I (1925) is the standard edition
and is likely public domain (published 1925, Hultzsch died 1927), but archive.org was unreachable from
the research machine on 2026-10-21. The quotations need to be re-checked
against it before release, and the register lists it as unread.

== Chronology

* *Regnal years are the source dates.* Nineteen events carry
  `calendar: regnal` with the inscription's own words in `as_written` ("when I
  had been consecrated twelve years"). The BCE window beside them is Smith's
  conversion and is cited as his interpretation.
* *The epoch is uncertain.* Smith puts the consecration in 269 BCE and calls
  the Indian dates "merely approximate". Other scholars put it a few years
  either side; no second chronology is cited here, so none is published as an
  alternative. A second edition (Hultzsch, or a modern synthesis with clear
  terms) is what would let the chapter record that spread.
* *All 36 events are windows, widened by Smith's own error.* Smith writes that
  the error in his dates for the reign "cannot exceed three years", so every
  BCE year or range converted from his chronology runs three years either side
  (a dated edict of 249 BCE is published as 252–246 BCE), with that sentence
  cited on the date. The window also covers a regnal year straddling two
  Julian years. Only Dionysios' embassy, dated by Ptolemy II's reign, keeps its
  Greek dates. Aśoka's rule, a duration, keeps Smith's 273–232 BCE, since
  widening a duration would assert rule across years the evidence does not
  support.
* *Five disputes are recorded, not merged:* the chronicles' massacre of
  ninety-nine brothers at the succession, whether _sambodhi_ means a journey
  to Bodh Gayā or a turn towards enlightenment, the chronicles' date for the
  third council against Smith's, the length of the reign (thirty-seven or
  fifty-four years), and which Alexander Rock Edict XIII means (Epirus, or
  Beloch's Corinth).

== Places

* *Thirty-three new places*, most of them inscribed rocks and pillars. Seven
  have an `approximate_point` at the monument itself; 24 have a
  `display_anchor` at the nearest settlement or site; two (Suvarṇagiri and
  Jatinga-Rāmeshwara) have an `unknown` position with Smith's description.
  Siddapura uses Smith's own degrees and minutes.
* *The monuments are typed `other`.* The v0 place types have no inscription
  or monument type, so each such record says so in its notes.
* *Moved pillars.* Fīrōz Tughlak moved the Topra and Meerut pillars to Delhi
  in the fourteenth century, and the Allahabad pillar probably came from
  Kauśāmbī. The records anchor them where they stand today as a
  `display_anchor`, not an asserted position, and say so; v0 has no way to give
  a place a different position at the time of the event.
* *Regions as points.* Kalinga and Lanka are regions anchored at a point.
  They want territories, which are still only proposed for contract v1.
* *Shared places.* Pāṭaliputra, Bodh Gayā, Sārnāth, Kauśāmbī, Taxila,
  Tāmralipti, Kapilavastu, Kuśinagara, Śrāvastī, Mathurā and Kashmir are the 玄奘
  thread's records and are reused, not minted again.

== Legend

The Ceylon chronicles and the northern Aśokāvadāna supply the council, the
mission to Lanka, Mahinda and Saṅghamittā, and the pilgrimage with Upagupta.
Smith relates the northern stories "simply as legends". Thirteen events rest
on them in whole or in part; each says so in its summary and cites Smith's
retelling, never the chronicle itself, which the chapter has not read. Where a chronicle's own year
disagrees with Smith's table, the record keeps the chronicle's year and notes
the disagreement.

== Engine findings

What this chapter asks of the engine, for the follow-up work:

. *Regnal calendars.* A regnal year should display as the source gives it,
  with the conversion as a secondary, cited reading.
. *A legend basis.* Legend is marked only in prose. A claim basis or event flag
  for tradition and legend would let the atlas style it.
. *Positions at a time.* Moved monuments need a position for the event's
  time and one for today.
. *Coincident points.* Barābar and Nāgārjunī share one Wikidata coordinate, and
  several edicts share a site, so labels and markers need to separate.
. *A monument place type.* Most of the chapter's places are `other`.
. *Relationships in the story.* The manifest puts relationships (the Kalinga
  annexation, the diplomacy of Rock Edict XIII) between events as steps, which
  is worth checking in the step UI.
. *Prologue nodes.* The chapter first opened at 261 BCE, with the succession
  and consecration as scene-setting steps before it. The atlas projection
  accepts an event outside the window only as a step captioned "Coda —", so
  the preview build failed, and the window now opens at the succession in
  273 BCE. A prologue label, and running the projection in `pnpm test` for
  every chapter, would catch this before a preview build does.

== What would raise quality before approval

* Re-check every quotation against Hultzsch's 1925 edition.
* Add the inscriptions found after 1920 (Erragudi, Kandahar, Laghman, Gujarra,
  Ahraura, Sannati and others) from a source with clear commercial terms.
* Cite a second chronology for the consecration, so the epoch can be recorded
  as a dispute.
* Locate Suvarṇagiri, and give the Mahāvihāra and Anurādhapura their own
  places (the mission events now sit at Lanka, a region point, and at
  Mihintale).
