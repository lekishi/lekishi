= A conventional basis for standard date conversions
:state: decided; the owner chose a basis and accepted the shape and rule below on 2026-10-22, and it is built in contract v0
:toc:
:toclevels: 2

On 2026-10-22 the owner chose to add a claim basis for dates that are standard
conversions rather than one scholar's argument. The question came from the
Greece and Persia chapters (link:GREECE_PERSIA_FEASIBILITY.adoc[feasibility
note]). This document records the contract change and the rule for when to use
it. The owner accepted both on 2026-10-22 ("conventional looks fine to me, go
ahead"). They are built in contract v0, and the three chapters' year
conversions are re-cited.

== The problem

A claim has one of two bases (link:CONTRACTS.adoc[contract v0]):

* `attested`: what a source reports.
* `cited_interpretation`: an interpretation advanced in cited scholarship, which
  must name who advances it.

Herodotus, Xenophon and Arrian give no years BCE. Every year in the three Greek
chapters is a modern conversion of a season, a halt, an archon or a month. Today
each is cited as the interpretation of the scholar whose book states it: 59 date
claims attributed to Grote and 37 to Chinnock. That has three costs:

* *Routine dates read as contested.* That Thermopylae was fought in 480 BCE is not
  Grote's view; every reference work gives it. The few real disputes, such as the
  Gordian knot, lose their weight among a hundred routine "interpretations".
* *The same step is cited two ways.* The Qin and Tang chapters convert regnal
  years (始皇帝十四年 to 233 BCE) and cite them as `attested`, with the source's
  form in `as_written`. Arrian's "in the month Maimacterion, when Nicostratus was
  archon" is the same kind of date, but it is cited as Chinnock's interpretation.
* *Review cannot see the difference.* The publication checklist asks about
  "attestation versus interpretation". A reviewer has no way to tell a standard
  conversion from a scholar's reconstruction without reading every claim.

The atlas does not show a claim's basis to readers today. The distinction matters
first for review and for the evidence display that later RFDs will add.

== Decided: three cases, one test

The test for a date claim is: *would a standard reference work give the same
year, without argument?*

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Basis |When |Example

|`attested`
|The source states the date in its own calendar (a regnal year, an archon year,
an Olympiad, an era year), and the conversion to a year BCE is not in dispute.
The record keeps the source's form in `as_written`. Mapping a stated calendar
date to a civil year is normalization, which v0 already defines; it is not
interpretation.
|Arrian: "in the month Maimacterion, when Nicostratus was archon" (Issus,
333 BCE). 始皇帝十四年 (233 BCE).

|`conventional` (new)
|The source gives no calendar date, but places the event in a sequence that ties
it to a dated anchor ("in the following spring", a count of stages or days), and
standard reference chronologies agree on the year. The same applies to a window
whose two ends are such anchors.
|Herodotus: the fighting at Thermopylae falls on the days of the sea fights at
Artemision (480 BCE). Xenophon: the army reaches Trapezus (400 BCE).

|`interpretation`
|Reference works disagree about the year, one scholar argues for it, or the year
is an editorial reckoning that no reference states.
|"About 483 BCE" for the start of the Athos canal, counted back from Herodotus's
"about three years". Any year in a disputed chronology.
|===

When in doubt between two rows, take the lower row. A date that is wrongly
labelled `interpretation` costs nothing but noise. A date wrongly labelled
`conventional` hides a dispute.

== Decided: the contract change

`conventional` joins the claim-basis vocabulary in contract *v0*. The atlas is
not live, so the owner amended v0's rule on 2026-10-22: until launch, extending
a vocabulary amends v0 in place instead of opening a new version
(link:CONTRACTS.adoc[contract v0]). The claim schema change goes in
`historical/v0/schema`, and the vectors go in `historical/v0/vectors/claims.json`.

A `conventional` claim:

. names its conversion in a new field, `convention`. The field is free text of
  up to 200 characters, such as "Herodotus's campaign seasons on the standard
  chronology of the Persian Wars";
. cites at least two sources: the passage that places the event (the input) and
  a reference work that states the year (the output);
. asserts only a time pointer (`/time` or a dated sub-field). Place
  identifications are out of scope (see <<open>>);
. has role `main` or `supporting`, never `alternative` or `conflicting`. A
  record that disputes the same pointer may not carry a `conventional` claim on
  it: if the year is disputed, it is not conventional.

Each rule becomes a vector, with these codes:

* `conventional_missing_convention`
* `conventional_single_citation`
* `conventional_outside_time`
* `conventional_in_dispute`

*Authoring.* Authors write `basis: conventional` and `convention:`. The compiler
maps the basis through unchanged and rejects a missing `convention`.

[source,yaml]
----
# historical/events/xerxes/thermopylae-480.yaml, as re-cited
date:
  value: 480 BCE
  mode: window
  calendar: julian
  as_written: these battles at sea and the battles on land at Thermopylai took place on the same days
  cite:
    - id: thermopylae-480-time
      says:
        Grote dates the occupation of the pass to about the latter part of June 480 BCE; the battle
        followed after Xerxes waited four days (VII.210).
      basis: conventional
      convention: Herodotus's campaign seasons on the standard chronology of the Persian Wars
      sources:
        - source: herodotus
          at: VIII.15
        - source: grote-history-of-greece
          at: vol. V, ch. XL
          quote:
            the land-force under the Spartan king Leonidas, the naval force under the Spartan commander
            Eurybiadês, apparently about the latter part of the month of June.
    - id: thermopylae-480-text
      says: Herodotus synchronizes the fighting at Thermopylae with the sea fights at Artemision.
      source: herodotus
      at: VIII.15
      quote:
        Now it so happened that these battles at sea and the battles on land at Thermopylai took place
        on the same days
      role: supporting
----

*Display.* Nothing changes now. When the atlas shows evidence on a card:

* an attested or conventional date reads as a plain date;
* an interpretation names its scholar ("dated by Grote");
* a dispute keeps its own marker.

== What it cost and what moved

* Contract v0 gains:
** the basis;
** the `convention` field in the claim schema;
** eight vectors for the four new codes;
** the matching checks in the reference semantics (`documents.ts`).
* The authoring compiler gains one basis and one check.
* The three Greek chapters' date claims are re-cited:
** the six events Arrian dates by archon and month (Tyre, Issus, Thapsacus,
   Gaugamela, Darius's death and the Hydaspes) are `attested`, with the archon
   and month in `as_written` and Chinnock's finer conversion as a supporting
   interpretation;
** Alexander's death is `conventional`, not attested: Arrian dates it by
   Olympiad and archon only, and the archon year of Hegesias spans 324 and
   323 BCE, so the year needs the standard chronology;
** the other 87 Grote and Chinnock conversions are `conventional`, citing the
   passage and the reference;
** the editorial count for the Athos canal stays `interpretation`.
* The Qin and Tang chapters do not change, so no approval reopens.
* The other pre-100 CE chapter threads can use the basis as soon as it merges.

Pros: a reader and a reviewer can tell a routine date from a real dispute, and a
regnal year and an archon year are treated alike.

Cons:

* A contract change, with eight vectors to maintain.
* A judgement call at the edge between `conventional` and `interpretation`. The
  test and the rule to take the lower row are meant to keep that edge
  conservative.

== Decided with it, and still open

* *`convention` or `attributed_to`.* Decided as proposed: a new field says what
  the conversion is. Reusing `attributed_to` would name a person, and a
  convention has no single author.
* *Calendars for archon and Olympiad years.* v0's calendar vocabulary (`julian`,
  `gregorian`, `islamic`, `chinese_lunisolar`, `regnal`, `unknown`) has no value
  for eponymous years (Athenian archons, Roman consuls, Assyrian limmu) or for
  Olympiads. The Arrian records now say `julian`, which describes the conversion
  rather than the source. Still open: the proposal is to add `eponymous` and
  `olympiad` to v0's calendar vocabulary, which is not built yet because the
  Long Count calendar is being added to the same list.
* *Place identifications.* Identifying an ancient name with a site by a standard
  gazetteer (Pleiades, Barrington) is the same kind of step. Left out as
  proposed, until a chapter needs it, so the first use of the basis stays
  narrow.
