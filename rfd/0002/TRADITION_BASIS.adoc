= A tradition basis for received stories
:state: decided; the owner chose a per-claim basis on 2026-10-22, and it is built in contract v0
:toc:
:toclevels: 2

On 2026-10-22 the owner chose to add a claim basis for what a source passes on
as tradition: the received story of a time the writer did not witness and that
no record of the time confirms. The question came from the kings of Rome
(chapter `rome-kings-753-509-bce`). Three options were put to the owner: a basis
per claim, a flag on the whole event, or no contract change. The owner chose
the basis per claim. This record gives the rule and the contract change.

== The problem

A claim has one of three bases (link:CONTRACTS.adoc[contract v0]):

* `attested`: what a source reports.
* `cited_interpretation`: an interpretation advanced in cited scholarship.
* `conventional`: a date that standard reference chronologies agree on
  (link:CONVENTIONAL_DATES.adoc[a conventional basis for standard date
  conversions]).

Livy and Dionysius wrote under Augustus about kings they dated seven centuries
earlier. "Romulus fortified the Palatine" is what Livy reports, so today it is
`attested`. Caesar's account of his own campaign in Gaul is `attested` in the
same way. A reader and a reviewer cannot tell an eyewitness from a legend
without reading every record's notes. Until now the kings chapter said
"tradition" only in its titles and summaries, and the founding was marked
disputed so that the date would not read as settled.

The places are not the problem. The Palatine, Alba Longa and Ostia are real
sites, located by gazetteers. The legend is in the acts and their dates.

== Decided: the rule

The test for a claim is: *does the source pass this on as what was told of a
time before any record it could check?*

* `tradition` (new): the source reports the claim as received story. Examples
  are the founding of a city by its eponymous hero, the acts and reigns of kings
  known only from much later writers, and birth legends. The claim cites the
  passage that tells it, as an attested claim would.
* `attested`: the source reports it as record. That covers a witness, a
  contemporary document, or a later writer working from records of the time.
  It is still not proof.

Tradition is about who could have known, not about whether the story is true.
A tradition can preserve a real event, and the basis does not deny it.

When in doubt, take `tradition`. A record wrongly labelled tradition invites
the reader to look at its sources. A record wrongly labelled attested presents
a legend as record.

Places keep their own evidence. A place record's location and names are cited
from gazetteers and are not tradition. Within an event, the link to the place
("Romulus fortified the Palatine") is part of the story and takes the story's
basis.

== Decided: the contract change

`tradition` joins the claim-basis vocabulary in contract *v0*, which the owner
extends in place until launch (link:CONTRACTS.adoc[contract v0]).

A `tradition` claim:

. needs no `attributed_to`, because it is what the source tells, not a
  scholar's reading. That is the same as an attested claim;
. may take any role. The competing founding dates are all traditions, so a
  tradition can be one side of a dispute;
. is not itself a dispute. An undisputed record may be told wholly as tradition;
. never shares a pointer with a `conventional` claim. A year that a tradition
  gives is not a standard conversion, even when reference works repeat it (the
  founding of Rome in 753 BCE is Varro's era, not a chronology). The code is
  `conventional_in_tradition`.

Four vectors in `historical/v0/vectors/claims.json` pin the rule:

* a tradition claim needs no attribution and no dispute;
* traditions can be the alternatives of a dispute;
* a conventional date on a pointer a tradition asserts is refused
  (`conventional_in_tradition`);
* a conventional date beside a tradition on another pointer is valid.

*Authoring.* Authors write `basis: tradition` on a cite. The compiler maps it
through unchanged.

[source,yaml]
----
# historical/events/rome-kings/rome-founding.yaml
participants:
  - actor: romulus
    role: leader
    cite:
      id: rome-founding-romulus
      says: Livy tells that Romulus alone took power and the city was named after its founder.
      basis: tradition
      source: livy
      at: "1.7.3"
      quote: "ita solus potitus imperio Romulus; condita urbs conditoris nomine appellata."
----

== Decided: what the atlas shows

An event is shown as tradition when any of its main claims is a tradition. That
is the same reading as for disputes, and it never overstates a record:

* *Card.* A `tradition` badge and one sentence: "Told as tradition: the sources
  pass this on as the received story of a time they did not witness."
* *Event list and search.* The `tradition` badge beside the disputed badge.
* *Map.* A place's name is underlined with dots when an event placed there in
  the window is tradition. The marker keeps the place's own evidence form, so a
  real site stays an attested ring. The legend shows the badge with the words
  "tradition, dotted name" when the view draws one.

== What moved

* Contract v0 gains:
** the basis;
** one code, `conventional_in_tradition`;
** four vectors (the pinned total goes from 216 to 220).
* The authoring compiler gains one basis.
* The atlas gains the tradition flag on an event, the badge, the card section,
  and the dotted place name.
* The kings chapter is re-cited. The founding, the accessions and every act of
  the kings and their families become `tradition`. The reign windows stay
  editorial interpretations, and the founding and the expulsion keep their
  disputes. Place records do not change.

Other chapters can adopt the basis when their threads re-cite. Candidates are
Sargon's birth legend and the Trojan War.
