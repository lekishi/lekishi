== Goal

Deliver RFD 1's atlas in the browser, as a static site, with historical
semantics independent of the UI framework and the renderer, and with native
delivery kept open.

== Architecture

The site is a statically built SvelteKit project. Every route is prerendered;
the artifact is plain files with no application server, database, or secret,
served from the domain root at `https://lekishi.pages.dev` on Cloudflare
Pages (the GitHub-connected project the owner authorized on 2026-09-24).

Responsibilities:

Content:: Chapter authoring files compiled at build time into the v0 contract
  documents (RFD 2), bundled into the artifact through the browser-safe
  catalog. No content is fetched from a service at runtime.
Historical logic:: Renderer-independent projection from contract documents to
  the atlas domain, time filtering, stepping, playback, search, and story
  navigation, in `src/lib/atlas` outside the renderer and the components.
Svelte and TypeScript:: Search, layers, the timeline, the selection card, the
  About sheet, responsive layout, and one owner of interaction state
  (`AtlasWorkspace.svelte`).
Renderer:: The globe, basemap, camera, picking, markers, labels, lines,
  polygons, and arcs, behind the `AtlasRenderer` boundary. Renderer objects
  never enter the domain, the scene, or the content.

=== Decision: the scene carries lines and areas

The atlas domain and the scene gain, beside placed events: journey lines
(`schematic_link`, `reconstructed_route`, `attested_itinerary`), relationship
arcs, and dated control-snapshot polygons, each with its interpretation and
its citation ids. The projection accepts the geometry interpretations RFD 2
publishes instead of rejecting them. Places become first-class scene objects
with their events attached, so the renderer draws one marker and one label
per place with a count.

=== Decision: terrain, basemap, and symbols, all shipped and provider-free

Three assets, all built offline from open data and shipped in the artifact,
with no provider account, token, or runtime tile service:

* *Terrain* for the diorama: a mesh built from open elevation (the Terrarium
  tiles on AWS Open Data, or an equivalent public-domain model) over each
  served chapter's extents, at about 130 m per sample at region scale and
  finer where a local scene needs it, with the vertical exaggeration recorded.
* *Basemap* for the globe and the 2D fallback: raster tiles painted from
  Natural Earth 1:10m tints and vectors with shaded relief, zoom 0–4 globally
  and 0–7 over chapter extents, replacing the Natural Earth II raster Cesium
  bundles.
* *Symbols*: a small library of glyph assets (conifer, broadleaf, house, hall,
  pagoda, vessel, party), instanced and scattered by terrain or placed at
  attested sites. Their terms are recorded with the build like any media.

The attribution stays visible as today. Payload is measured and recorded.

=== Decision: collision-free labels with script coverage

Labels are placed by a collision engine, one per place, with zoom priority and
fonts covering CJK and Latin. Whether that engine is the renderer's own or an
atlas-side placement over the renderer's label primitive is decided in the
renderer spike below.

=== Decision: the renderer is decided on the hero, in a bounded spike

Cesium is the current renderer. It was "confirmed" earlier because no named
requirement failed; RFD 1's hero now names one, and the hero is a tilted 3D
diorama. That rules out a 2D vector-map engine (MapLibre GL has no terrain
mesh with placed models) and leaves two candidates:

* *Cesium, stylized.* Keeps the globe, terrain streaming, camera, and picking
  the atlas already has. The work is self-hosted terrain from the open
  elevation above, painted imagery, vertical exaggeration, instanced glyph
  assets at region zoom, and custom shading or post-processing to leave the
  GIS look behind.
* *A custom Three.js scene* for region and local scale, with the globe kept
  on Cesium or a painted globe. Full control over the stylization (the mock
  was built this way in about 250 lines) at the cost of rebuilding tile
  streaming, globe-to-local continuity, and picking.

A spike of at most two days builds the hero's scene (terrain, water, scattered
symbols, a vessel, labels, shadows) on each candidate in a branch, and the
owner decides on the screenshots and on measured frame time on the desktop,
a physical iPad, and a mid-range phone. Whichever wins, the `AtlasRenderer`
boundary, the domain, and the scene are unchanged; there is no dual-renderer
framework.

=== Timeline and diagrams

The timeline strip and relationship timelines are SVG components with
selected D3 utilities, owned by the workspace, driven by the same state as
the map.

== Static-site boundaries

These stand from link:STATIC_ARCHITECTURE.adoc[the static architecture
decision] and `AGENTS.md`: no server, no server-only data loading, no module
scope access to `window` or WebGL, one workspace mount per page, Cesium (or
its successor) imported in one module, runtime assets resolved through the
base URL and checked by the build invariant, idempotent disposal, and no
provider credential anywhere in the artifact.

== Delivery

* Build: `pnpm build` from a clean checkout; output `build/`; the invariant
  script fails the build if the pages, bundles, stylesheet, or runtime
  assets are missing.
* Host: Cloudflare Pages from `main`, preview deployments per pull request.
  A custom domain is an owner decision.
* CI: format, type check, unit and contract tests, the static build, and the
  browser suite against the built artifact.
* Error visibility and a health path: a static health file and the host's
  logs; a client error reporter only if the owner authorizes a provider.
* Rollback: redeploy the previous Pages deployment; documented and exercised
  once before the public announcement.

== Devices and performance

Desktop/laptop browsers and iPad-class tablets are the primary targets and
get the 3D diorama, with the pixel look (the same scene at a quarter
resolution with a reduced palette) switchable on demand. Phones get RFD 1's
handheld tier: a top-down tile map drawn with PixiJS from a tileset and a
sprite sheet, the tiles classified at build time from the same elevation the
diorama uses, with grid movement and two buttons. The tile map is cheap to
draw and needs no WebGL beyond what PixiJS falls back from, so it is also the
fallback where the diorama cannot run. Acceptance on the tablet is on physical
hardware; the handheld tier is accepted on a mid-range phone. The
budget is reader-facing: the chapter page interactive within three seconds on
a mid-range phone over a typical mobile connection, sixty frames per second
during pan and playback on the desktop and the iPad, and a measured payload
per chapter recorded in the checklist.

== Native remains open

Versioned data formats, portable identifiers, and behaviour fixtures stay
independent of browser APIs. After the Tang chapter renders its hero and runs
on a physical iPad, assess installable web, a native shell, Flutter, and
dedicated clients against observed shortcomings. Installability alone does
not satisfy the quality bar; a native shell is acceptable if it delivers the
experience. Nothing is built for native until then.

== What this RFD stops

* The treatment comparison harness, its review route, and its measurements
  (PR #14, closed 2026-10-10).
* "Cesium confirmed" as a conclusion drawn from the absence of a named
  requirement.
* Comparing renderers through a dual-renderer abstraction rather than a
  spike.
* The MapLibre comparison: the hero is a 3D diorama, which it cannot draw.

== Open questions

* The renderer, decided by the spike between stylized Cesium and a custom
  Three.js scene.
* The elevation source and pipeline (Terrarium tiles for both the terrain and
  the basemap relief, or GEBCO/ETOPO at globe scale), recorded with its
  rights.
* The tile size and the tileset's own look for the handheld tier, decided on
  the hero at phone width.
* A custom domain and an error-reporting provider, both owner decisions.
