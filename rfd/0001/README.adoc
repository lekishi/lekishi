= RFD 1 Historical exploration and visual interactions

== Revision note

Revised on 2026-10-10 on the owner's instruction, after a status review of the
served build found the atlas correct but not worth looking at: no routes, no
areas, no images, every place at a modern city-centre anchor, labels piled on
one point, and more screen given to editorial status than to history. This
text replaces the earlier RFD 1; the earlier text stays readable in Git history
and its decisions that still stand are restated here rather than referenced.
The founding discussion's visual references remain in <<Visual references>>.

== Goal

Lekishi is a passion project for exploring what happened across the world and
how people, societies, and institutions interacted over time. It must be
engaging to explore: a reader should be able to follow a journey, a campaign,
or a transmission across the map and the timeline and come away having learned
something. A database of pins with caveats is not the product. Begin in the
browser while keeping native delivery open.

== Established direction

Start with a globe and narrow continuously into regional and local exploration.
Use a maps-application layout with Civilization-inspired readable terrain,
symbolic settlements, and visible interactions. Globe and local views are
scales of one experience, not competing modes.

War is one interaction among many. Trade, diplomacy, missions, learning,
religious transmission and division, institutional development, and local
adaptation matter equally. States, cultures, religions, and companies are not
interchangeable entities or mutually exclusive territorial categories.

The Civilization reference describes clarity, discovery, and the pleasure of
following interactions. It does not request a playable simulation, a resource
economy, alternate history, or invented certainty.

== Decision: the hero defines done

Every chapter has one target image, the *hero*: a mockup of the chapter's best
screen at desktop width, made before implementation and approved by the owner.
For the Tang chapter it is a styled relief map of the East China Sea with the
four ships' schematic return through their dated landfalls, 鑒眞's arc to
東大寺, a timeline with the 753–754 events as ticks, and one period image in
the selection card. The hero is the acceptance bar for the chapter's
visualization work: the served page is compared with it side by side, and the
owner judges the comparison. A gate, a harness measurement, or a passing test
is not a substitute for that comparison.

== Decision: show, then qualify

The atlas draws what the sources support and qualifies what is reconstructed.
Uncertainty is expressed in the visual grammar, not by leaving the map empty.
This is the reading of link:../0002/README.adoc[RFD 2] that governs the
atlas; the earlier practice of refusing to draw anything not proven to the
metre is withdrawn.

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Evidence state |Visual form |Meaning to preserve

|Attested site (an excavated palace, temple, port, or city)
|Solid marker at the site, the name in its own script, no caveat badge.
|A site coordinate from a cited gazetteer is an identification, not a survey of the event's spot.

|Identified locality or region (an island, a commandery, a bay)
|Soft-edged marker whose size follows the stated precision.
|The size is the precision. No text badge repeats it.

|Named place with no identification
|Not drawn. Named in the event's card with "location not identified".
|An absence on the map is not a claim that nothing happened there.

|Journey with dated stops
|Dashed line through the dated stops, labelled "schematic, follows the dated stops", animated along the playhead.
|The line connects attested places; it is not an itinerary between them.

|Route reconstructed in cited scholarship
|Dashed line in a distinct pattern with its citation, labelled "reconstructed".
|An interpretation attributed to its author, visibly different from attested movement.

|Political control at a date
|Dated snapshot polygon with a hatched edge and a date label.
|No interpolation between snapshots; no implication of uniform control inside.

|Relationship (contact, transmission, diplomacy, conflict, institutional change)
|Arc drawn on selection, styled by type, with the claim and its citation one click away.
|Showing a connection asserts the cited claim and nothing more; it never implies causation.

|Editorial status
|One line in the chapter's About sheet.
|Never in a map label, a row badge, or a header banner.
|===

== Proposed experience

=== Globe to region to event

At globe scale, show the chapters as sparse clusters within the selected time
window. Reveal places, relief, routes, and individual events with zoom. Keep
real geographic positions and distances. At close scales, symbolic landmarks
may appear without implying an archaeological reconstruction.

Keep the time window and selection stable across zoom changes. A selected event
opens its narrative, image, participants, sources, nearby events, and connected
events. Nearby does not mean related; connected events can be distant. Following
a link may move the camera and offers an explicit time change when the
destination is outside the window.

=== Places first

The map draws places; events attach to places. A place with many events shows
one marker, one label, and a count, and opens a list on selection. Event
titles are short names for a reader, in the chapter's own language where that
reads naturally; caveats, precision, and placement notes belong in the card.

=== Basemap and labels

The basemap is a styled relief map: hypsometric tints, shaded relief, strong
coastlines, rivers at regional scale, a muted palette that lets markers and
routes read. Labels are collision-free, one per place, prioritised by zoom,
set in fonts with the script coverage the chapter needs, with the historical
name leading and the local-script form beside its romanization following
RFD 2's naming policy. Modern names live in details and search.

=== Layout

A full-screen map, floating search and layer controls, a persistent timeline,
and a selection drawer that becomes a bottom sheet on small screens. One header
line. Editorial status, coverage, and review independence live in an About
sheet opened from the header, stated once. A flat or low-tilt view remains an
option for geographic reading.

=== Timeline and playback

The timeline is a strip, not a pair of number fields: event ticks, the window
as a draggable brush, the playhead on it, and a zoomable scale from centuries
to months so a five-week voyage is readable day by day. Offer scrubbing,
play/pause, event stepping, speed control, and optional camera following.

==== Decision: fixed exploration window with a moving playhead

Playback moves a playhead within the selected window without moving or
expanding the window. The playhead stops at the window's end; no looping and
no rolling window. Distinguish contextual events from those supported at the
playhead; an uncertain occurrence stays visibly uncertain across its span and
is never highlighted as definite activity throughout it.

==== Decision: chronological controls and separate story navigation

Time controls step through dated events chronologically. Chapters provide a
separate "Next in story" action for editorial sequences that may revisit
context or jump ahead. Story jumps are explicit, with the existing confirmation
when the destination is outside the window. Story order is presentation order,
not evidence of sequence or cause.

==== Decision: retain details when time filtering excludes the selection

Changing the window does not close the selected event's card. If the event no
longer matches, keep its content, mark it "outside selected time", and offer
"Return to event's time", which changes the window explicitly. The map marker
is not retained on that basis. A bounded uncertain occurrence that overlaps
the window is a visibly uncertain possible match, not an exclusion.

==== Decision: manual camera control stops following, not playback

A manual pan or zoom during camera-follow stops following while time keeps
playing. Following resumes only through an explicit action, at the current
playback position. Programmatic camera movement never cancels following.

=== Visual language for relationships

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Subject |Visual form |Meaning to preserve
|Campaigns and war |Directional campaign segments, battle markers, dated control snapshots |A schematic campaign is not an exact marching itinerary.
|Trade |Networks of ports, cities, and route segments |A network does not prove a particular shipment.
|Missions and learning |Selected journeys within wider networks, outbound and return stages |Travel, encounter, and later adaptation are separate claims.
|Religion |Community markers and journeys linked to a relationship timeline |Spread is not territorial ownership.
|Companies and institutions |Selectable organizations with changing roles, places, and relationships |Commerce, diplomacy, and coercion need distinct treatment.
|Diplomacy |Dated links for alliances and agreements |Connections change, overlap, and can be disputed.
|===

Use symbols, line patterns, and labels as well as colour. Reveal connections on
selection or within a story layer rather than drawing every relationship at
once. A relationship timeline supplements the map where geography alone cannot
explain institutional or religious change.

=== Decision: compare stories on a shared map and timeline

Multiple stories can be selected as layers on one map, sharing the window and
playhead, each identified by non-colour-only cues. Showing events together
establishes neither contact nor causation. An event in several stories remains
one event with visible memberships. Side-by-side maps with independent cameras
are deferred.

=== Decision: explicit coverage guidance and distinct empty states

Keep globe exploration available while identifying the chapters, regions, and
periods the content covers. Distinguish "not yet covered" from "no results with
these filters" using the declared scope. An empty map never establishes that
nothing happened. A coverage overlay is deferred.

=== Evidence and accessibility

Show dated snapshots rather than invented intermediate front lines. Label
schematic and reconstructed motion. Selecting a link exposes what is asserted
and its sources. Do not assign numerical confidence without a methodology.
Keyboard navigation, touch controls, reduced motion, readable labels, and a
non-map event list let a reader explore without animation, precise pointing,
or colour discrimination.

== Chapters

=== Decision: a chapter is served when it clears the bar

A chapter is worth serving when a reader can follow at least one journey,
campaign, or transmission end to end on the map with the sources one click
away, and when it renders its hero. The bar is content and experience, not
process. Two chapters at a few dozen events each are below it; the roadmap in
link:IMPLEMENTATION.adoc[the checklist] raises them above it.

=== Decision: widen the Tang chapter to its network

The single 752–754 embassy stays the spine, bounded as the Gate 1 decision
records. Around it the chapter adds the dated missions to Tang 630–894 as a
sparse network layer, 鑒眞's five failed crossings 743–748 as a second story,
and 圓仁's 838–847 diary as a third, all from public-domain primary sources
already in or beside the citation set. Target by the end of the first
quarter: 200 or more events, 60 or more places with site or locality
coordinates, 5 or more routes, and 20 or more cleared images.

=== Decision: give the Qin chapter a campaign shape

The end of Han 233–226 BCE is extended to the fall of the six states 230–221
BCE, with dated control snapshots cited to the standard historical atlases as
interpretations, so the chapter has a campaign to follow and the atlas has a
territory snapshot to draw.

=== Candidate stories

Alexander's campaigns, the Silk Road networks and the British East India
Company, Christianity's spread and divisions, and World War II as a later
stress case remain candidates. The next chapter after Tang and Qin is chosen
from what readers ask for once the Tang chapter is public.

== What this RFD stops

* The geographic-versus-illustrated treatment comparison. The hero decides the
  treatment; the comparison harness and its review route are retired.
* Per-row and per-banner editorial caveats on reader surfaces.
* Caveat text in event titles and map labels.
* Number inputs as the time control.

== Alternatives and trade-offs

A pin-only atlas is simpler and is what was built; it misses the product. A
game simulation would invent outcomes. A photorealistic globe reads modern; a
heavily illustrated local world risks false scale. Prefer geographic
continuity with restrained symbolic detail, routes and arcs for interaction,
and dated snapshots for control.
