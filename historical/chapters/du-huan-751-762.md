# 杜環 and the 經行記, 751–762

> **Draft.** The manifest [`du-huan-751-762.yaml`](du-huan-751-762.yaml) carries no
> approval. Development and pull-request previews serve it under the draft
> banner; production does not. Only the owner approves it, with `pnpm approvals`.

This note is the chapter's feasibility record: what the sources can carry, what
the chapter publishes, and what it leaves out and why. It was written on
2026-10-21, when the chapter was drafted as candidate 5 of the chapter-selection
pass, to give the atlas a second Tang chapter that reaches west of the Pamirs
and a test bed for journeys whose stops cannot be drawn.

## Feasibility in one paragraph

Feasible as a chapter about **a war, a lost book and a decade of contact**, not
as a drawn journey. 杜環's 經行記 is lost; about 1,500 characters survive as
quotations in 杜佑's 通典 (卷一九一–一九三), with one sentence of biography. That
sentence dates his departure (天寶十載, 751, "to 西海") and his return (寶應初, 762,
by merchant ship through 廣州), and nothing in between. The fragments describe
lands, not a route, and 通典 quotes them in its own order. Around that core, the
Tang histories give a dense, dated frame: the 750 石國 raid, the 751 defeat at
怛邏斯, and 大食's envoys, troops and raid on 廣州 in 756–758. So the chapter can
carry 23 events with real dates and disputes, but 杜環 has only two stops of his
own, both dated, and the far one (西海) cannot be placed.

## Sources and rights

All four new sources are public-domain works read in Wikisource transcriptions,
under the register's Wikisource row: quote the work, cite the edition, note the
transcription, re-check against a critical edition before release. 通典 has its
own register row because it is the only witness of 經行記.

The new 資治通鑑, 舊唐書 and 新唐書 sources are **separate ids** from the
`zizhi-tongjian`, `jiu-tangshu` and `xin-tangshu` sources of the approved Qin and
Tang chapters. Widening those sources' titles to the juan this chapter reads
would change the digests of both approved chapters. Whether to consolidate to
one source per work (and re-approve) is an owner question; see the end.

Wikidata items were read on 2026-10-21 through `Special:EntityData`, and each
coordinate cites the item revision current that day. The Wikidata and Wikipedia
APIs were rate-limited from the cloud environment.

## How the chapter handles what the sources do not say

- **杜環 is never placed at the battle.** 杜佑 says he "followed 高仙芝's western
  campaign and reached 西海 in 天寶十載". That he was captured at 怛邏斯 is the
  usual modern reading, recorded in notes and not asserted.
- **經行記's observations are undated windows** (751..762) on the places they
  describe: the Chinese craftsmen and the weekly sermon at 亞俱羅, and the 東道使 at
  末祿. A fragment that only describes a land (拔汗那, 康國, 拂菻, 苫國, 摩鄰,
  師子國, 波斯) is not an event; those lands are context places on the
  event of writing the book.
- **Identifications are not cited, so anchors assert none.** 亞俱羅 = Kufa,
  末祿 = Merv, 拂菻's royal city = Constantinople and 拔汗那 = the Ferghana valley
  are the usual readings; their places carry `display_anchor` positions whose
  notes say so. 苫國 (al-Shām), 摩鄰, 西海, 白石嶺 and 相州 carry `unknown`
  geometry. No public-domain or open scholarship on these identifications was
  reachable on 2026-10-21 (Internet Archive refused connections).
- **Chinese accounts of 大食 are kept as Chinese accounts.** 白衣 and 黑衣 大食
  are separate dynasty actors beside the state 大食, and 阿蒲恭拂 keeps the
  sources' name, with al-Manṣūr only as a Wikidata external id. The change of
  house is an undated succession: both histories tell it as an alternative
  account (一云, 或曰) without a year, so the chapter neither dates it nor borrows
  the Arabic histories' 750.
- **Not asserted:** that the 亞俱羅 craftsmen were prisoners of 751, that
  paper-making came west with them, that 杜環 stopped at 師子國, or that the 751
  war caused the later alliance.

## Journeys

The owner decided on 2026-10-21 (PR #26) that a journey is a record kind of its
own, `historical/journeys/<id>.yaml`; the record's shape and drawing rules are
still proposed in [the authoring format record](../../rfd/0002/AUTHORING_FORMAT.adoc),
and nothing of it is built. The chapter writes no journey record until it is.
Each 杜環 stop is an event with exactly one `start`, `end` or `site` place, and
everything else is `context`, so the proposed shape can use them unchanged. The
journey the sources support is:

```yaml
# historical/journeys/du-huan-west-751-762.yaml (not written: the kind is not built)
travellers:
  - actor: du-huan
    role: participant
stops:
  - du-huan-reaches-xihai-751      # 西海: unknown geometry
  - event: du-huan-return-762      # 廣州: display anchor
    by: sea                        # 因賈商船舶
```

摩鄰 is not a stop. 通典 introduces that fragment with 又云 ("it also says"), so
it describes a land and does not say 杜環 went there.

Under the proposed drawing rules this journey draws **nothing**: 西海 is unknown
and 廣州 a display anchor, so its one leg is not drawn. It is a case for the open
question "is a journey whose stops are all unlocated worth publishing for its
list and card alone?", which the proposal answers yes. A second journey, 李嗣業's
retreat (`talas` start, `baishi-ling` site), would test a leg from a located to
an unlocated place.

## Steps outside the window

Three story steps fall wholly outside the 750–762 window: the 744 marriage of
和義公主, the 748 destruction of 碎葉 and the 801 presentation of 通典. The atlas
projection requires every such step's caption to start "Coda — ", so all three
carry it, including the two that come before the window. The engine has no
label for a prelude, so a reader sees 744 and 748 badged as "coda". This
chapter is a test case for adding one.

## Exclusions

The Arabic accounts of 751 (Ibn al-Athīr and later) and of the Abbasid
revolution; the founding of Baghdad (762) and the move from Kufa; paper-making at
Samarkand; the 747 小勃律 campaign; the 安祿山 rebellion except notices naming
高仙芝, 李嗣業 or 大食; the captive 突騎施 khagan, 吐蕃 chief and 朅師 king of 751 as
records; 經行記's purely descriptive passages as events; 涼州, 鄯州, 鳳翔, 靈武 and
淮南 as places.
