This note answers the "half-day feasibility note before the owner picks" step
of the chapter-selection method of 2026-10-21, for the first-ranked candidate:
Pope Stephen II's journey from Rome to the Frankish king Pepin, 753–754. The
owner asked on 2026-10-21 for as many chapters and events as possible, to test
both the data model and the engine, so the pass also authored the chapter as a
draft: `historical/chapters/stephen-ii-journey-753-754.yaml` and the records it
reaches. Nothing in it is approved; the owner approves it, or not, with `pnpm
approvals`.

Where the note states a historical fact, a record cites a source and a locator
for it. Where it describes an identification or a reading, it says so.

== Verdict

Feasible, and a good second journey for the engine. The journey has a
followable shape with dated stops at both ends (14 October 753 from Rome,
15 November from Pavia, 6 January 754 at Ponthion), named stops between them,
and a return with the army through named places. All four primary texts are
public domain and reachable as transcriptions, and every place has a continuous
modern identity, so the stops can carry asserted coordinates rather than
display anchors. The sources disagree in three places that the contract's
dispute and window mechanisms already express.

The weak points are the editions. Every passage was read in an unproofread
transcription, the life of Stephen in a reading copy with no paragraph numbers,
and none was checked against a critical edition, because the scans refused the
container. That check is the first item before release.

== What the chapter holds

* *56 events* in four parts: the prelude in Rome (752–753, 10 events), the
  journey to Saint-Denis and the winter in Francia (753–754, 18), the first
  campaign and the return (754 or 755, 13), and a coda (756–757, 15).
* *22 places*, all with asserted points (`approximate_point`): five at site
  precision (St Peter's, Santa Maria Maggiore, Saint-Maurice, Saint-Denis,
  Monte Cassino), fifteen at locality precision, and two valleys at region
  precision (the Maurienne, the Susa valley).
* *23 actors*: 19 people, the Lombard and Frankish kingdoms, the empire, and
  the Roman church.
* *8 relationships*: conflict, alliance, travel (out and back), diplomacy
  (Pepin to Aistulf, the empire to Pepin), the 756 donation as a change of
  boundaries, and the pope's support for Desiderius.
* *5 sources*, and 3 disputes kept as disputes or windows.

== Disputes and uncertainty

[cols="1,3",options="header"]
|===
|Question |How the chapter keeps it

|Where the pope met the king
|The life has Ponthion and the continuations have Charles bring him there; the revised annals (after 814) have Quierzy. `ponthion-meeting-754` is disputed, with the revised annals as a conflicting claim.

|Who sent Carloman
|The life says Aistulf, the annals his abbot, the revised annals the abbot at the Lombard king's order. `carloman-arrival-754` is disputed and keeps all three.

|The year of the first campaign and the return
|The annals put them in 755; the life tells them straight after the 754 anointing and assembly. Every campaign event carries the window `754..755` instead of a year.

|Janua in 756
|Usually Genoa, but on the road from Chalon to the Maurienne it is read as Geneva. Editorial, said on the place and the event.

|The year of the Berny assembly
|"Evoluto anno … ad Kalendas Martias": dated 1 March 754 by the life's order; the reading is stated on the event.

|The imperial envoys "in this city of Rome"
|The life has the pope receive them in Rome while it also has him with the army. Recorded as the life gives it, not resolved.
|===

Dates are written in the Julian calendar. The life dates by indiction: the
seventh indiction began on 1 September 753, which fixes 14 October and
15 November to 753 and the following 6 January to 754.

== Fit with journeys as their own kind (PR #26)

The stops are modelled for the proposed journey record: each stop is an event
with exactly one `site`, `start`, `end` or `waypoint` place, and everything
else is `context`. Two events are deliberately not stops because their place is
not stated (`charles-meets-stephen-753`, and `george-meets-pepin-754`, "not far
from Pavia"). Once the kind exists, these journeys follow from the records
without new events:

[source,yaml]
----
# historical/journeys/stephen-to-francia-753.yaml (proposed, not written)
travellers: [{ actor: stephen-ii, role: leader }]
stops:
  - rome-departure-753
  - pavia-arrival-753
  - pavia-departure-753
  - great-st-bernard-crossing-753
  - saint-maurice-arrival-753
  - ponthion-meeting-754
  - saint-denis-winter-754
cite: [{ source: liber-pontificalis, at: XIIII die octobrii … ad exhibernandum }]

# stephen-return-754: saint-denis-winter-754, lyon-march-754, vienne-march-754,
#   maurienne-camp-754, pavia-siege-754, stephen-return-rome-754
# imperial-envoys-754: imperial-envoys-rome-754, imperial-envoys-marseille-754
#   (by: sea — "marino itinere" is stated), george-meets-pepin-754 (context: breaks)
# pepin-campaign-756: chalon-march-756, geneva-march-756, maurienne-march-756,
#   mont-cenis-crossing-756, susa-clusae-756, pavia-siege-756
# carloman-754: carloman-arrival-754, carloman-vienne-754, carloman-burial-755
#   (place: vienne, then place: monte-cassino)
----

What it exercises that the Tang voyage does not: a land journey whose every
stop has an asserted point, so legs are drawn end to end; two journeys sharing
stops (the pope's return and the first campaign); a sea leg stated by the
source on another party's journey; a journey ending at a stop that is the
start of another (Pavia); and a party whose order is attested but whose dates
are windows that overlap (Pavia to Ponthion), so the playhead has to show order
without false sub-year precision. The one open question in PR #26 this chapter
answers by example is `by`: no leg of the pope's journey states its mode, and
none can be inferred from an island, so a strict rule would leave every leg
unstated.

== What else it tests in the engine

* The first chapter in Latin and in the Julian calendar, with Latin name forms
  and no romanization.
* Dense places: Rome carries nine events and Pavia seven, and Rome and St
  Peter's are under 3 km apart, so labels and markers have to cluster.
* Windows of very different widths beside exact days, including several
  `754..755` windows overlapping the day-precise 754 events.
* Region-precision points (two valleys) beside site-precision ones.
* A chapter whose map runs from Marseille to the Moselle and from the Seine to
  Monte Cassino, so the home view is a European region rather than a sea.
