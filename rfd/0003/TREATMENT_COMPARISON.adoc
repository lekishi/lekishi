= RFD 3 treatment comparison: geographic/symbolic and illustrated-local

== Provenance and scope

This record answers the treatment comparison RFD 4 places between its Gate 2 and
Gate 3, and it is the dated decision record for the work:

* link:../0001/README.adoc[RFD 1] decided to *compare local-detail approaches
  through later implementation* — geographic detail with symbolic landmarks
  against an illustrated local historical scene — over the Tang mission and a
  bounded Qin episode, keeping scenario content, controls, selection, and time
  behavior comparable so the visual trade-off is what differs.
* link:../0003/README.adoc[RFD 3] owns the rendering evaluation: compare labels,
  geographic scale, local playback, selection, and measured rendering behavior
  across zoom levels for those two treatments and two scenarios, with Cesium
  evaluated first and MapLibre compared only if Cesium fails a named requirement.
* link:../0004/README.adoc[RFD 4] keeps the comparison behind Gate 2 and leaves
  the treatment choice and the renderer resolution to Gate 3, which is the
  owner's decision. link:../0004/GATE2_EVIDENCE.adoc[The Gate 2 evidence record]
  precedes this one and is what allowed the comparison to start.

It is internal product work. Nothing here changes a reader surface: the root page
and the chapter pages carry no comparison vocabulary, no treatment control, and
no illustrated treatment, and `scripts/check-static-build.mjs` fails the build if
they ever do. No historical content, publication set, or ledger row changed.

== What is compared

=== Treatment A — geographic/symbolic (shipped, unchanged)

The map as the site ships it: the Cesium globe with Natural Earth II imagery, the
ellipsoid terrain, the chapter's places as symbolic points with their published
label forms, the selected event outlined, playback states by point size and
colour, and the details panel's place, placement note, occurrence, dispute, and
citations. No city reconstruction, no route geometry, no illustrated scenery.

=== Treatment B — illustrated local (new, comparison only)

A restrained, code-drawn vignette of the selected event's locality, shown as a
card at the top of the selected event's details: the place the record names, the
record's own published `event_types`, a small set of silhouette shapes, and a
sentence that states what the drawing is and is not.

Its content rules are the treatment's own honesty contract, and they are the part
of it that is tested rather than eyeballed:

* the card prints the record's published `event_types` verbatim — the contract's
  own vocabulary — and classifies nothing itself. An early draft narrowed the
  published type into an atlas word ("voyage" for `journey`, "court act" for
  `mission`) and drew a vessel for any journey; that was wrong twice over: the
  card asserted a classification the record does not publish, and it decided how
  a journey was made when the record does not say. The card now shows the
  record's own words, and the `journey` composition is land-neutral — no vessel
  and no shoreline exist anywhere in the drawing vocabulary
  (`src/lib/atlas/scene-illustration.ts`);
* the scene is a pictogram of a *published type*, not a portrait of a *place*:
  every event that publishes the same types draws the same shapes, so the drawing
  claims nothing about this place's buildings, coastline, distances, or bearing;
* a composition is drawn only where the published type supports it: `founding`
  draws a foundation, `campaign` a campaign, `mission`/`council` an assembly,
  `exchange`/`process` an exchange, `journey` a land travel scene, and
  `occurrence`/`other` the plain scene rather than a wrong one;
* a record that names no place draws nothing at all, and a record the atlas does
  not place keeps the record's own statement of why beside the drawing;
* the drawing carries no text, no coordinate, and no shape derived from one, and
  every card states in words that no position, extent, or distance in it is
  sourced.

=== Boundary of this comparison, stated up front

The illustrated treatment is a surface *beside* the map, not a replacement of the
globe view at local scale. A treatment that replaced the map at local scale would
be a second rendering path, which RFD 3 forbids building speculatively, and it
would make the two treatments differ in far more than local detail. This is the
smallest representative implementation of RFD 1's illustrated option that can be
compared fairly; what it cannot measure — whether an illustrated *map* reads
better than a geographic one at local scale — is recorded as a limitation below
and is not claimed either way.

== Criteria, defined before measuring

These were fixed before any screenshot or number was taken, and the measurements
below report against them in this order.

. *Readability at globe and local scales*: what a reader can read at the default
  view and at a local view, with and without the treatment.
. *Label crowding*: how many labels the map has to draw and how close their
  anchors are, per chapter.
. *Selection and time-state clarity*: whether the selected event, its window
  relation, and its playback state stay legible.
. *Source and uncertainty cues*: whether the place's placement kind, the
  record's uncertainty, and the published dispute stay visible and unaltered.
. *Mobile and tablet usability*: the treatment at 1440x900, 820x634, 390x844, and
  320x640, including whether it overlaps anything or crowds the details out.
. *Payload and performance*: what each page loads, and the measured cost of the
  treatment on one machine.
. *Implementation risk*: what the treatment costs to build and keep.
. *Unsupported-detail risk*: what the drawing implies that the sources do not
  support, and what stops it reading as evidence.

== Concepts developed before implementation

Three concepts were drawn as design references before any code was written, and
inspected (`.amp/in/artifacts/concepts/`; no image-generation tool is available
in this environment, so the references are hand-authored SVG and their rendered
PNGs, not generated art):

* *Concept 1 — scene card in the exploration column*: a bordered vignette card
  beside the selected event's details.
* *Concept 2 — illustrated local band across the map*: a wide, short scene strip
  pinned above the time controls, with the map above it unchanged.
* *Concept 3 — illustrated vignette anchored on the map*: a framed scene over the
  map, tied to the selected marker by a leader line.

Chosen: *Concept 1*, because it is the smallest representative implementation. It
needs no new renderer API (Concept 3 would need a screen-position channel from the
renderer into the workspace, which the renderer-independent boundary deliberately
does not have), it cannot cover the map (Concept 2 takes map height exactly where
the phone layouts have least), and it is testable without a graphics engine. The
chosen concept is preserved with the other two under `.amp/in/artifacts/concepts/`
so the owner can see what was rejected and why.

== The comparison surface

The treatments are compared on one internal page,
`/review/local-treatment/<chapter-id>`, which renders a served chapter through
the same catalog, the same projection, and the same workspace as the chapter
route, with one extra page-level choice: which treatment to show. The page
prerenders for each served chapter, states `Internal comparison surface · not a
reader page`, carries `noindex, nofollow`, and holds the only treatment control in
the product.

Isolation is enforced, not asserted: `scripts/check-static-build.mjs` requires the
review pages to exist with one mount point, the internal marker, the chapter's own
name and recorded decision, and both treatment labels; and it fails the build if
the root page or a chapter page links the review surface or carries any of its
framing, its control ids, or the illustrated treatment's own markers. Because the
workspace mounts only in the browser, the HTML sentinels are backed by executed
checks: `e2e/treatment-comparison.spec.ts` mounts each chapter, selects an event,
and asserts the card and the control are absent, and `mount.test.ts` does the same
over every in-window event of both chapters.

== Findings, criterion by criterion

*Readability at globe and local scales.* Both treatments draw the same map, so
the treatment changes nothing about what the globe shows; what it adds is a
statement about the selected event's locality. At the default view both
chapters' labels overlap on the map, and the card is the clearest local
statement on screen for the selected event — but it is a statement the details
panel already makes in the record's own words ("the return voyage, 753",
"display anchor only"). At a local view the map's labels separate and the card
adds least.

*Label crowding.* Measured above. The treatment does not touch it: the map draws
the same labels in both treatments, and the Tang chapter's seven-labels-on-one-
anchor crowding is unchanged. A treatment that addressed crowding would have to
change what the map draws, which is a different treatment than the one compared
here.

*Selection and time-state clarity.* The card appears with a selection and only
with one, names the selected record's place, and disappears with the treatment
switch. It carries no playback state and no window relation, so it neither helps
nor hinders the outside-selected-time case: the details panel's notice stays where
it was, below the card, and the card's decorative marker cannot be confused with
the map's selection outline.

*Source and uncertainty cues.* Unchanged and unweakened: the card repeats the
place label the record publishes and, for a record the atlas does not place,
carries the record's own statement of why (the same sentence the details panel
shows). The placement note, the date precision and occurrence mode, the published
dispute with its accounts, and the citations are all still the details panel's
own. The card adds one more statement that no position, extent, or distance in
the drawing is sourced.

*Mobile and tablet usability.* At 1440x900 and 820x634 the card sits above the
details inside the same panel and both are readable. At 390x844 it takes roughly
a third of the panel's visible height. At 320x640 it takes most of it, so the
record's own details need a scroll that the shipped treatment does not need. The
executed geometry check passes at all four sizes: the card is inside the details
panel's own bound, the panel stays inside the viewport, and nothing crosses the
time controls.

*Payload and performance.* The illustrated treatment's code is compiled into the
chapter chunk both routes already load, so a reader page gains no request and no
measurable load time; the comparison page pays 895 bytes more than a chapter page
for its own route chunk. The measured selection-to-details times are within noise
of each other (see the table), and the container's software renderer is the
dominant cost in both.

*Implementation risk.* Contained and small: one new pure module (the scene model),
one new Svelte surface, one optional prop through the existing mount boundary
(defaulting to the shipped treatment), one review route, one build-invariant
addition, and 17 unit tests plus 4 browser checks. The reader surface is pinned
unchanged by `mount.test.ts` and by the artifact invariant. The risk that remains
is not technical: it is that the comparison surface and the vignette code keep
shipping until the owner decides.

*Unsupported-detail risk.* This is where the two treatments differ most. The
geographic/symbolic treatment claims only what the records publish: a labelled
point, its placement kind, and the record's own note. The illustrated treatment
adds a drawing of a place, and a drawing of a place is the kind of surface a
reader reads as a depiction. The mitigations are real and tested — the card
prints the record's own published event types, the same shapes are drawn for
every event that publishes them, no shape is derived from a coordinate, a record
with no place draws nothing, the `journey` composition is land-neutral, and every
card says in words that it is not a reconstruction — but they reduce the risk
rather than remove it: a reader can still take the vignette as an impression of
what the place looked like, and nothing in the sources supports that impression.
The review that produced the corrections above is the evidence that this risk is
not hypothetical: the first draft of the treatment asserted a classification the
records do not publish.

== Recommendation and trade-offs

This record does not decide Gate 3. It recommends, and the trade-offs are the
owner's to weigh.

*Renderer.* Cesium is confirmed for the experiment. The comparison produced no
named visual, device, or delivery requirement that Cesium failed: globe and local
scale both render, labels, selection, and playback states behave, and the
container's software renderer is a test-environment cost rather than a Cesium
one. No MapLibre comparison was started, because RFD 3 asks for one only when
Cesium fails a named requirement.

*Treatment.* The recommendation is to keep *geographic/symbolic* as the reader
default, and to keep the illustrated treatment as a review artifact rather than a
shipped surface. The reasons are the findings above, in the order they matter:

. the illustrated treatment's information is generic by construction: it restates
   the record's own published event types and adds a pictogram chosen from them,
   but it adds no place-specific detail the record's own text does not carry;
. it carries the larger unsupported-detail risk of the two, which the labelling
   mitigates but does not remove;
. it costs details-panel area exactly where the phone layout is tightest;
. it does not address the map's real readability problem — label crowding at the
   default view — which a treatment would have to change the map to fix.

The trade-off on the other side is real and worth stating plainly: the
illustrated treatment is the only one of the two that gives the atlas a distinct
visual identity and a calm local anchor, and the founding concepts in RFD 1 point
that way. If the owner wants illustration, the finding of this comparison is that
it has to be *sourced* illustration — rights-cleared historical imagery for the
specific place, labelled as a later illustrated copy and never as evidence, with
its own rights review under RFD 4 AC7 — because a generic generated scene buys
the visual language without buying any historical content.

*What the owner is being asked to decide (Gate 3).*

. Select the local-detail treatment: keep `geographic-symbolic` as the default
   with the illustrated treatment retained as a review artifact (the
   recommendation), or adopt `illustrated-local` as the reader default.
. If neither is settled yet, authorize a bounded follow-up instead: a
   *sourced*-illustration study for one place (rights-cleared imagery, labelled as
   a design reference), and the same comparison run again on its result.
. Confirm the renderer half of Gate 3: Cesium confirmed, with the MapLibre
   comparison remaining unstarted unless the owner names a requirement Cesium
   fails.
. Decide whether the internal comparison surface (`/review/local-treatment/*`)
   stays in the artifact for the remainder of the experiment or is dropped before
   the public launch; the public deployment remains a separate owner-authorized
   action.

== Review findings and corrections

An independent review of the change set (a stronger reasoning model reading the
diff, not the author) raised three findings before this record was finalized.
Two were real defects in the work and are corrected here; the third was a
verification gap.

. *The illustrated treatment asserted classifications the records do not
  publish.* The first draft mapped a published `journey` to an atlas word
  ("voyage") and drew a vessel, and mapped `mission` to "court act"; the card
  printed that word, so a record that was an overland installation at 東大寺 was
  labelled a voyage. Corrected: the card prints the record's published
  `event_types` verbatim, the composition names are internal and never printed,
  the vessel and shoreline are gone from the drawing vocabulary, and the
  `journey` composition is land-neutral. Two regressions over the real records
  named in the finding now pin it.
. *The build invariant did not enforce all of the isolation it claimed.* It
  banned the review route and two phrases on reader pages, but not the
  treatment's control ids or its surface names, and the browser isolation check
  never selected an event (so a reader page that handed the workspace the
  illustrated treatment would still have passed both). Corrected: the invariant
  bans the labels, the control id prefix, and the card's own test id on every
  reader page, and the browser check mounts each chapter, selects an event, and
  asserts no card and no control, while a unit test does the same over every
  in-window event of both chapters.
. *A Gate 2 check selected a record its own search had filtered out.* The
  baseline spec searched for a participant name and then selected a record that
  does not match that query; the selection would have timed out on a correctly
  working implementation. Corrected before the run recorded in
  link:../0004/GATE2_EVIDENCE.adoc[the Gate 2 evidence record]: the query is
  cleared, the window's list is asserted back, and the control-parity assertion
  now requires every expected control rather than only equality between the two
  chapters.

== Limitations recorded

* The illustrated treatment is a card beside the map, not an illustrated map. It
  therefore cannot answer whether an illustrated *local view* reads better than a
  geographic one; that question would need a second rendering path and is not
  measured here.
* The vignette is deliberately generic. It gives the kind of thing that happened,
  not what a place looked like, and the same shapes are drawn for every event of
  a kind. A place-specific illustration would need sourced, rights-cleared
  historical imagery and its own rights review under RFD 4 AC7; nothing here
  authorizes one.
* The timings come from the container's software renderer. They are comparable
  with each other and are not device evidence; RFD 4 AC5's physical-device
  acceptance remains a separate checkpoint.
* The review surface ships in the artifact because the owner reviews the built
  site. It is unlinked, `noindex`, and labelled, but it is reachable by URL until
  the owner decides otherwise; the public launch is a later owner-authorized
  action, and dropping the surface is one commit if the owner prefers.
* The comparison exercises two chapters. A chapter with dense, closely spaced
  labels (Tang) and a sparse one (Qin) are both covered, but a future chapter
  with a different label profile could behave differently.
