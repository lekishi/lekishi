== Scope and method

Evaluated here, per RFD 4 checklist item D and RFD 3 Phase 0:

* Phoenix hosting and the PostgreSQL runtime the current scaffold requires.
* Public HTTPS, health checks, log and error visibility, repeatable deploys,
  rollback and recovery, regions, operational burden, and plausible PoC costs.
* Cesium-compatible imagery and terrain that require no unsupported caching or
  offline promise, including attribution, accounts, pricing and limits, payload
  implications, and a no-paid-account development path.
* Domain and DNS implications without selecting or purchasing a domain.
* Representative desktop browsers, physical iPad-class testing, phone responsive
  checks, and a measurable performance budget.

Read as inputs: `AGENTS.md`, RFD 3 and its checklist, RFD 4 and its checklist,
RFD 1 and RFD 2 where they constrain delivery, `mix.exs`, `config/config.exs`,
`config/dev.exs`, `config/prod.exs`, `config/runtime.exs`,
`lib/lekishi_web/router.ex`, `.github/workflows/ci.yml`, `Makefile`,
`.amp/services.yaml`, `flake.nix`, and `assets/`.

Not in scope: the Svelte/Vite/Cesium build integration (Wave 1C), historical
content, PostGIS, database-backed content migration, native delivery, and any
deployment action. No account was created, no purchase was made, no secret was
configured, and no external state was changed for this evaluation.

== Delivery requirements distilled from RFD 4

The PoC needs: a stable public HTTPS URL with a valid certificate and no
mixed content (AC1); a documented health endpoint (AC1); CI passing on the
released commit including a production asset build (AC8); application errors
recorded where the owner can review them (AC8); documented and repeatable
deploys plus an exercised rollback or recovery path (AC8); production-built
assets served without the Vite development server (AC4); provider terms and
attribution satisfied with no offline promise (AC7); and measured desktop,
physical-iPad, and phone-layout results against agreed thresholds (AC5, AC6).

Historical content remains file-based, so no database content, spatial query, or
PostGIS requirement enters this evaluation.

== Hosting and PostgreSQL runtime

=== Shortlist and criteria

Shortlisted because each supports Elixir/Phoenix releases, managed PostgreSQL,
automatic TLS, health checks, and a documented rollback path: Fly.io, Render,

self-managed Kubernetes (high operational burden for a PoC), serverless
function platforms without long-lived WebSocket/Phoenix support, and providers
that require a paid plan before any evaluation is possible.

=== Recommendation: default and fallback `[recommendation]`

Default: Render — one web service on the Starter plan plus a Basic-256mb
PostgreSQL database, in the region closest to the owner and reviewers.

* No Dockerfile is required (native Elixir build), which keeps Wave 2 release
  preparation small.
* Health checks gate a new deploy on readiness, and instant rollback retains
  five builds on Hobby, which maps directly onto AC8's rollback requirement.
* Managed PostgreSQL at $6/month, with point-in-time recovery on paid
  instances, is the cheapest *supported* managed database of the shortlist;
  Fly's managed Postgres starts at $38/month and its cheaper unmanaged Postgres
  is documented as unsupported.
* HTTPS, two custom domains, 7-day log retention, and region choice are
  included on the Hobby workspace without extra metering.
* Expected cost for the PoC: about $13/month `[estimate]` (Starter $7 +
  Basic-256mb $6), plus bandwidth only above 5 GB/month.

Fallback: Fly.io — one `shared-cpu-1x` Machine with 512 MB or 1 GB in the region
closest to reviewers, with Fly Managed Postgres if the owner wants a managed
database from the same vendor, or an external managed PostgreSQL otherwise.

* Lower compute cost (about $3.19–$4.18/month for 512 MB `[verified]`) and
  fine-grained regional placement.
* Requires a Dockerfile and more machine, volume, IP, and certificate
  management; every organization needs a card on file; rollback redeploys an
  image but does not restore configuration or secrets.
* The managed database is the expensive part; the cheap path is explicitly
  unsupported for production, so the owner must accept that trade-off.

Also acceptable for a zero-spend start: Gigalixir's free tier (one 0.5 GB
replica, free PostgreSQL limited to 10,000 rows and 2 connections, custom
domains with TLS, zero-downtime deploys, `releases:rollback`) `[verified]`. Its
database limits make it a development or demo host rather than the public PoC
host unless the owner accepts them; the Standard tier costs $25/month for
0.5 GB.

Railway and Heroku remain viable (Railway Hobby $5/month including $5 usage;
Heroku Eco $5/month plus Essential-0 Postgres $5/month) but add no capability
this PoC needs over Render or Fly.

=== Required PostgreSQL runtime

* PostgreSQL 18 is what CI and local development use `[verified]`; a managed
  PostgreSQL 16–18 service is acceptable `[recommendation]`.
* The PoC stores no historical content, so a 256 MB–1 GB instance with 1 GB
  storage is enough `[recommendation]`.
* Set `POOL_SIZE` deliberately: the default pool of 10 fits Render's
  Basic-256mb, but must be lowered to 2 on Gigalixir's free database and kept
  below the chosen plan's connection limit otherwise `[recommendation]`.
* Run `mix ecto.migrate` as a release step (Render pre-deploy, Fly
  `release_command`, or the platform's release phase) so a failed migration
  stops the deploy `[recommendation]`.
* Required environment: `DATABASE_URL`, `SECRET_KEY_BASE`, `PHX_HOST`, and
  `PHX_SERVER=true`; `PORT` is supplied by the platform `[verified]` against
  `config/runtime.exs`.

=== Error visibility

* Sentry's free Developer plan (one user, 5,000 errors per month, 30-day
  lookback, email alerts) `[verified]`, with the official Elixir SDK
  (`sentry` 13.5.1, published 2026-09-02, with Phoenix and LiveView
  integrations) `[verified]`, is the recommended error-visibility mechanism for
  AC8 `[recommendation]`.
* Platform logs alone are a fallback: Render retains 7 days on Hobby `[verified]`;
  Fly provides live tail plus 7-day searchable logs in Grafana `[verified]`.
* LiveDashboard is not a production option here: it is gated behind
  `dev_routes` and would need authentication before production use
  `[verified]`.

== Imagery, terrain, and provider rights

=== Constraints

* Cesium-compatible sources, with the renderer's resources served from the
  release artifact (AC4).
* Public reviewers need no personal token or per-reviewer setup (AC1), so any
  provider key must be held by the application and protected, never entered by
  the reviewer.
* No offline promise and no caching that provider terms forbid (AC7, RFD 4
  non-goals).
* Attribution, license, and reuse terms must be recordable in the release
  (AC7).
* Historical content must not depend on renderer or provider objects (RFD 2,
  RFD 3).

=== Development path with no paid account `[recommendation]`

Use MapTiler Cloud's free plan as the development provider: an account is
required but no billing information is, and the same key serves OSM-based
imagery plus quantized-mesh terrain, so the Cesium integration developed during
Wave 1C and Wave 2 carries over to the release. Accept the free-plan conditions
knowingly: non-commercial use, the MapTiler logo on the map, quota pausing, and
key protection.

Zero-account alternative, if the owner prefers no provider account at all:
NASA GIBS imagery plus AWS Open Data terrain tiles. This path costs no money and
needs no account, but requires a custom heightmap decoder and offers only
coarse-resolution imagery for local detail `[recommendation]`.

OpenStreetMap's own tile service is not categorically prohibited for a
low-traffic public PoC: the policy explicitly permits normal interactive human
viewing that requests only the tiles for the current viewport, provided the
requirements above are met. It is still not recommended as the release source,
because availability is best-effort with no SLA, access may be blocked without
notice, and the policy warns commercial services that access may be withdrawn at
any point `[verified]`. The scaffold's default
`referrer-policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin` still sends a Referer header
on cross-origin tile requests, so it does not violate the policy; the release
must not switch to `no-referrer` or strip Referer through a proxy `[verified]`.
The strict User-Agent requirements target applications and SDKs; browsers send
their own default User-Agent, which the policy accepts for web pages
`[verified]`.

  typically tens of kilobytes each, 512×512 tiles count as four MapTiler
  requests, and quantized-mesh or Terrarium terrain tiles are comparable per
  tile `[estimate]`.

  application and renderer assets plus about 5–20 MB of imagery and terrain,
  depending on how deep the local exploration goes `[estimate]`. Hosting egress
  at 100 sessions/month is about 2.5 GB (inside Render's included 5 GB) and
  about 25 GB at 1,000 sessions (about $3.75 at Render's $0.15 per GB, or about
  $0.50 at Fly's $0.02 per GB) `[estimate]`.
* Bound client caches explicitly: Cesium exposes memory limits such as
  `maximumMemoryUsage` (256 MB default for point clouds) and
  `Scene.requestRenderMode` for on-demand rendering `[verified]`; the
  performance budget requires the measured heap to stay inside a fixed ceiling.

== Domain and DNS implications

* AC1 does not require a custom domain: a platform hostname
  (`+*.onrender.com+` or `+*.fly.dev+`) provides stable public HTTPS with a
  valid certificate and no reviewer setup `[verified]`. Using it keeps the
  domain decision open past Gate 1 at no cost.
* If the owner later adds a custom domain, no application change is needed
  beyond setting `PHX_HOST` and re-checking `force_ssl` and
  `check_origin` `[recommendation]`.
* Record types differ by platform `[verified]`:
  ** Render: an A record for the apex (Render uses IPv4; remove AAAA records),
     a CNAME for subdomains, automatic TLS via Let's Encrypt and Google Trust
     Services, and CAA records that include both authorities if CAA is defined.
     Adding `www` makes Render add the apex and redirect it to `www`.
  ** Fly.io: A and AAAA records are recommended for the apex, a CNAME for
     subdomains, plus `+_fly-ownership+` TXT or `+_acme-challenge+` CNAME for
     verification and wildcard certificates; Let's Encrypt certificates cost
     $0.10/month per single hostname after the first 10 free.
* Apex CNAME records are problematic without provider CNAME flattening; use
  A/AAAA or ALIAS/ANAME where available `[verified]`.
* Choose one canonical host (apex or `www`) and redirect the other; Render's
  behavior is automatic, Fly requires both certificates and an explicit
  redirect `[recommendation]`.
* Domain registration is estimated at $10–20 per year for a typical `.com`
  `[estimate]`. No domain is selected, checked for availability, or purchased
  here, and no registrar or DNS provider is recommended.
* Nothing in the PoC requires email, so MX/DKIM/SPF setup is out of scope
  `[recommendation]`.

== Performance budget

These thresholds are proposed for Gate 1 approval and must be frozen before the
comparative measurements (AC5). They are deliberately stated as budgets, not
observations: no measurement has been taken yet, and no threshold here may be
adjusted to match a later result. A change requires a dated decision recorded in
this document (or its successor) and referenced from the owning checklist.

Measurement protocol `[recommendation]`: cold cache for every load measurement;
five runs per device, reporting the median and the worst run; record device,
OS, browser version, and network profile; desktop measured on an unthrottled
connection of at least 50 Mbps, tablet measured on Wi-Fi and additionally on a
throttled 4G profile; use Chrome DevTools Performance, Memory, and Network
panels for desktop, and Safari Web Inspector on a Mac attached to the iPad
(Timelines, Network, and the memory or JavaScript-allocation instruments
available in the installed Safari version) for iPadOS. Record which
instruments were used. Safari's Memory and JavaScript Allocations timelines are
disabled by default and must be enabled in the Timelines tab, and enabling them
affects the inspected page's performance, so memory runs are recorded separately
from the frame-rate and latency runs. `performance.measureUserAgentSpecificMemory`
is Chromium-only, is listed by MDN as experimental and not Baseline, and requires
a cross-origin-isolated document, so it is unavailable on Safari/iPadOS and,
without COOP/COEP headers, in the PoC's Chrome sessions; it must not be assumed
available `[verified]`. Record payload bytes from the Network panel rather than
estimating them.
