= RFD 5 Roadmap reset: from a correct atlas to one worth looking at

== Why this RFD exists

The owner's assessment on 2026-10-10, after inspecting the served build: not
happy with what has been accomplished so far, both in data quality and in
visualization. This record states where the project actually is, why the result
disappoints despite everything being "done", and a roadmap that puts the
experience and the content first. It proposes; it decides nothing until the
owner does.

Nothing here rewrites RFD 1–4. Their requirements stand. What changes is the
order of work, the bar a chapter must clear before it is worth serving, and the
reading of RFD 2's evidence rules, which the implementation has applied as a
prohibition on drawing rather than as a duty to qualify what is drawn.

The site is live at `https://lekishi.pages.dev`. The build, the tests, the
static invariant, and the publication gate all pass. By every criterion the
checklists track, the project is on schedule. That is the problem: the
checklists measure correctness and process, and neither measures whether a
reader learns or enjoys anything.

== What the screenshots show

Captured from the built artifact on 2026-10-10 (`.amp/in/artifacts/`, desktop
1440×900 and phone 390×844):

* *The map is a blur with a pile of labels.* At the default height Natural
  Earth II is a low-resolution raster with no relief, no coastline detail, and
  no sense of place. The event labels are drawn unconditionally and collide:
  "The 節刀 grant and promotions, 752" lies over six other labels at 平城宮.
  Zooming in to follow a selection shows a green-brown smear with one dot.
* *Nothing moves and nothing connects.* No route between 蘇州黃泗浦 and the
  landfalls. No arc between 揚州 and 東大寺 for 鑒眞's transmission. No
  territory for Han or Qin. The 13 relationships exist only as JSON.
* *Governance is louder than history.* One screen says "Owner-approved for
  release, 2026-09-25" three times (header badge, sub-header, notice panel),
  then "Not independent or specialist review; those remain unperformed", then
  "2 of 24 in-window events and 1 of 15 places without a sourced map
  position". Every event row says "display anchor only". The reader is being
  told what the atlas refuses to claim before being told anything it does.
* *Titles are caveats.* "The return voyage, 753: documented landfalls only, no
  route drawn" is an editorial note, not a title; it is also the map label.
* *Time is a number field.* The window is two `<input type=number>` fields and
  the playhead is a bare range slider with no ticks, no event marks, and no
  scale. Playing a 750–754 window takes four steps and shows nothing happening.
* *The phone view is the same chrome at a third of the width.*

== Diagnosis

=== 1. Rigour was applied as refusal, not as annotation

RFD 2 says: interpolate movement only when "clearly labelled illustrative", use
"dated political-control snapshots rather than invented continuously moving
boundaries", and label "schematic route geometry separately from the historical
claim". Every one of those sentences permits drawing with a label. The
implementation read them as "draw nothing you cannot prove", so the chapter
asserts no route, no area, no site, and the domain layer rejects route geometry
outright (`projection.ts` lists `reconstructed_route` and `schematic_link`
among the interpretations it refuses). The result is an atlas whose honesty is
expressed as absence. A reader cannot see the honesty; they see an empty map.

=== 2. The data is thinner than the free sources it cites

The chapter places 黃泗浦 at the centre of modern Suzhou (31.30 N, 120.62 E)
and labels it "display anchor only". Wikidata, the same CC0 source the record
cites, has a site-precision item for the Huangsipu archaeological site at
31.853 N, 120.612 E, about 60 km away. The palace, the temples, 大宰府, and
the Daming Palace's 含元殿 are all excavated sites with published coordinates;
the chapter anchors them at modern city centres. The CHGIS gazetteer, Pleiades,
and the World Historical Gazetteer were deferred at Gate 1 and never revisited.
The feasibility report's own candidate images (東征伝絵巻, public domain) were
never cleared. The content is not wrong; it is far less than what was
available for free, because the process rewarded caution over coverage.

=== 3. The content format is built for a newsroom and used by one person

A place with one point is ~100 lines of JSON with a revision envelope, claim
ids, a revision hash, and a publication pin, and any change needs a new
revision, a new set, a ledger row, and a recomputed digest, which the build
then enforces. RFD 2 already decided the owner may approve his own sourced
work. The machinery for multi-editor publication exists before a second editor
does, and it makes every correction a governance event. Thirty-four events
took three weeks; a chapter that is interesting needs a few hundred.

=== 4. The roadmap measured gates, not the experience

RFD 4's waves and gates are all about verification: Gate 1 freeze, Gate 2
baseline, Gate 3 treatment, AC1–AC8. None asks "would a reader stay on this
page for five minutes?" The treatment comparison in PR #14 spent a sprint
comparing two ways of showing nothing local, and recommended keeping the
current one. The "Civilization-inspired readable terrain, symbolic settlements,
and visible interactions" that RFD 1 established as direction has not been
started.

== Principle change: show, then qualify

Replace "refuse unless proven" with "draw what the sources support, qualify
what is reconstructed, and make the difference visible in the visual grammar":

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Evidence state |Today |Proposed

|Attested site (excavated palace, temple, port)
|Modern city-centre anchor, "display anchor only"
|Solid marker at the site, name in its own script, no caveat badge.

|Identified locality or region (an island, a commandery)
|Point with "approximate location (region)"
|Soft-edged marker sized to the precision; the precision is the marker's size, not a text badge.

|Named place with no identification (驩州)
|Omitted from the map, present in the list
|Keep it off the map; show it in the event's story card with "location not identified".

|Journey with dated landfalls
|No geometry; the route is refused
|Dashed schematic line through the dated landfalls, labelled "schematic, follows the dated landfalls", animated along the playhead. The contract's `schematic_link` already exists for this.

|Reconstructed route from scholarship
|Refused
|Dashed line with a citation, `reconstructed_route`, visibly distinct from attested movement.

|Political control at a date
|Nothing
|Dated snapshot polygon with a hatched edge, labelled with its date and source; no interpolation between snapshots.

|Relationship (contact, transmission, diplomacy)
|JSON only
|Arc drawn on selection, styled by type, with the claim and its citation one click away.

|Editorial status
|Three banners plus per-row badges
|One line in the chapter's "About" sheet. Never in a map label or a row.
|===

Every one of these remains within RFD 2. What changes is that uncertainty
becomes something a reader can see rather than something that was left out.

== Roadmap

Three tracks, run in parallel, each with an exit condition that is a thing a
reader sees. Estimates assume one person plus agent assistance at the current
pace.

=== Track 0 — Decide and unblock (one week)

. *Pick the hero.* One screenshot that defines what "done" looks like for the
  Tang chapter: a styled relief map of the East China Sea, the four ships'
  schematic return, 鑒眞's arc to 東大寺, a timeline with the 753–754 events
  as ticks, one image. Make it (as a mockup, not code), and put it at the top
  of RFD 1's implementation checklist as the bar.
. *Close PR #14 by decision.* Accept its recommendation (geographic default,
  Cesium stays for now), merge the Gate 2 evidence, and drop the
  `/review/local-treatment/*` route from the artifact. The treatment question
  is answered by the hero, not by a harness.
. *Lift the content ceremony for single-editor work.* Keep claims, citations,
  precision, and the attested/interpretation distinction. Replace revision
  envelopes, per-record hashes, publication sets, and the digest ledger with:
  one compact authoring file per chapter (YAML, one entry per place, actor,
  event, relationship, with citations inline), a build-time compiler to the
  v0 contract shape the atlas already consumes, Git history as the revision
  history, and a `published:` flag per chapter the owner flips. The
  publication-approval test keeps its purpose (nothing unapproved is served)
  with one row per chapter instead of one digest per set.
. *Decide the gazetteer rights.* CHGIS is CC BY-NC 4.0: usable for a
  non-commercial passion project with attribution, and the single largest
  improvement available to Chinese place data. Pleiades is CC BY. The owner
  decides; the recommendation is to adopt both with attribution.

=== Track 1 — Make the map worth looking at (four to six weeks)

Each item is independently shippable and visible.

. *Basemap.* Replace Natural Earth II at zoom 0–2 with a self-built, shipped
  raster from Natural Earth 1:10m hypsometric tints plus shaded relief
  (GEBCO/ETOPO or SRTM-derived), rendered offline at zoom 0–7 for the chapters'
  extents and zoom 0–4 globally. No provider, no token, still static. Muted
  palette, strong coastlines, rivers at zoom ≥ 5. This alone changes the
  product more than anything since the Cesium mount.
. *Labels.* Collision-free labels: one label per place, event counts as a
  badge, label priority by zoom, script-appropriate fonts (Noto Sans CJK for
  CJK labels, a serif for Latin titles). Event titles become short names;
  the caveat text moves to the card.
. *Places first.* The map draws places; events attach to places. A place with
  seven events shows a "7" and opens a list, instead of seven overprinted
  labels.
. *Movement and connection.* Schematic journey lines through dated landfalls,
  animated with the playhead; relationship arcs on selection; a dated-snapshot
  polygon type for territory, used first by the Qin chapter.
. *Timeline.* A real timeline strip replaces the number inputs: event ticks,
  the window as a draggable brush, the playhead on it, and zoomable scale
  (centuries → years → months) so a 753 voyage is readable day by day.
. *Chrome diet.* One header line. The notice panel becomes an "About this
  chapter" sheet. Editorial state appears once, there. The selection card
  leads with the narrative and the image, then the sources.
. *Renderer check, bounded to two days.* Build the basemap and label items on
  MapLibre GL JS globe in a spike branch and compare: label collision is
  built in, vector styling is far easier, terrain-RGB is supported, the
  payload is a third of Cesium's. RFD 3 already names MapLibre as the
  comparison when Cesium fails a visual requirement; the hero mockup is that
  requirement. Decide on the spike's screenshots, not on a harness.

Exit: the Tang chapter renders the hero. Measured by a side-by-side of the
hero mockup and the served page, reviewed by the owner.

=== Track 2 — Make the data worth exploring (continuous, starts week one)

. *Fix the places we have.* Replace every display anchor with the best
  available site or locality coordinate from Wikidata, CHGIS, or Pleiades,
  with the dataset cited. Target: 0 of 19 city-centre anchors where a site
  exists.
. *Draw the voyage.* Publish the 753 return as a `schematic_link` through the
  dated landfalls, and 鑒眞's 753–754 overland leg 大宰府 → 難波 → 平城京 the
  same way. Cite the biography for the landfalls; the line is labelled
  schematic.
. *Images.* Clear and add the public-domain 東征伝絵巻 scenes and Wikimedia
  Commons photographs of 唐招提寺, the Daming Palace site, and 平城宮跡, each
  with its licence and a "later depiction, not evidence" label where it
  applies.
. *Widen the Tang chapter to its network.* The single embassy is a thin
  story; the embassy system is a rich one. Add the dated missions 630–894 as
  a sparse network layer (each mission one event at the Japanese and Chinese
  ends), 鑒眞's five failed attempts 743–748 as a second story, and 圓仁's
  diary 838–847 as a third. All are in public-domain primary sources already
  in the citation set or their siblings. Target by the end of the quarter:
  200+ events, 60+ places with site or locality coordinates, 5+ routes, 20+
  images.
. *Give Qin a shape.* Add the dated control snapshots for Han's territory
  (233, 230 BCE) from the standard historical atlases' readings, cited as
  interpretations, and extend to the fall of the six states 230–221 BCE so
  the chapter has a campaign to follow.
. *Batch the philology.* The critical-edition locator re-check is one pass
  before a release, not a gate on every edit.

Exit: a reader can follow one journey end to end on the map with the sources
one click away, and a second chapter has a visible shape.

=== Track 3 — Ship and learn (after Track 1's exit)

. Replace RFD 4's AC list for the first public release with four reader-facing
  criteria: the hero renders on desktop and iPad; a journey can be followed
  end to end; every drawn thing can be traced to a citation in two clicks;
  nothing takes more than three seconds to appear on a mid-range phone.
  Keep AC7 (rights) and AC8 (CI, rollback) as they are.
. Announce the Tang chapter publicly, collect reactions, and let the next
  chapter be chosen from what readers ask for.

== What to stop doing

* Stop adding gates, ledgers, invariants, and decision records ahead of
  content. A new decision record is warranted when a product decision changes,
  not for each presentation tweak.
* Stop labelling the reader's view with the editor's doubts. Doubt belongs in
  the visual grammar and in the card, not in titles, badges, and banners.
* Stop measuring the treatment comparison, the payload deltas, and the gate
  criteria until the map has something to compare.
* Stop treating "no coordinate asserted" as the safe default when a free,
  citable site coordinate exists.

== Owner decisions requested

. Approve the hero mockup as the bar for Track 1.
. Decide CHGIS (CC BY-NC) and Pleiades (CC BY) adoption.
