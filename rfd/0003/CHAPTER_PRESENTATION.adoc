= RFD 3 chapter presentation decision

== Provenance and scope

Two owner review comments on the served static build, both on 2026-09-27, drove
this record. The first asked why a reader would care about the Qin chapter at all:

*"you're not getting it. I'm not asking if that's the appropriate copy (obviously
not in my eyes), but why would a user look at this and even care about it? it's
just another chapter. comparing it or not is our problem not a user looking at
this page. also I don't think it needs to show reviewed chapter anymore, and
provide a name for each chapter instead"*

The second asked what the previous framing had meant in the first place: *"what
does a comparison review mean? isn't it just another chapter?"* The framing it
replaced is recorded in link:COMPARISON_REVIEW_ROUTE.adoc[the comparison review
route decision]'s update of the same day, which this record supersedes for the
user-facing surface; that record stays readable and its route, status, and ledger
decisions still stand.

The change is presentation only. No historical content, no publication set, and
no ledger row changed: the Tang set is still the owner-approved `lks:set:tang-mission-750-754-v1`
and the Qin set is still a `preparation snapshot` with no owner decision.

== Decision

* **The root page presents chapters, not review categories.** Each chapter is a
  peer card: the chapter's name, its coverage window in the atlas's year text, a
  story summary, and an ordinary `Open the chapter` entry. The card list comes
  from `src/lib/atlas/chapters.ts`; `chapters.test.ts` pins it against the
  content — one card per chapter tree, each card's window equal to the window the
  projection gives that chapter, and each card's target pinned to the route that
  serves it — so the page cannot describe a chapter the atlas does not carry.
* **Chapter names are presentation text.** The published story summary opens with
  the chapter's own construction ("from appointment through the return voyage ...
  with the included coda nodes ... presented after it"), which describes how the
  chapter was assembled rather than what a reader follows. The display names are
  therefore product copy — *The embassy to Tang China* and *The end of the state
  of Han* — grounded in the chapter's published scope, and they are the names the
  chapter's own page uses too (page title, workspace notice, globe label). The
  workspace takes the display name as a mount parameter, so it stays
  chapter-agnostic and falls back to the chapter's published title for a chapter
  that has no card.
* **Editorial state is secondary metadata, never the pitch.** The Tang card
  states the recorded owner approval and the specialist-review caveat in small
  print beside its entry, and `release.test.ts` still reads the RFD 2 ledger and
  the set: the date the card states is the date the Tang row records, and the line
  cannot describe the approved set as independent or specialist review. The Qin
  card states `Unapproved preparation · self-review only`, so discoverability
  still cannot read as release. Neither line is a chapter's identity or its
  reason to exist.
* **The review-category label leaves the user surface.** `Reviewed chapter` is
  gone from the root page, from the atlas page's header badge, and from the
  workspace notice: the notice now states the review independence the chapter's
  publication records (`Self-review only` for Tang) above the chapter's name, and
  the Qin chapter's page keeps its `Unapproved preparation` badge and its review
  note, which is where that status has to stay prominent. The Qin page is titled
  with the chapter's name instead of `Comparison fixture`, and its navigation
  offers the atlas, not a "reviewed" variant of it.
* **The Qin chapter's page still exists, is still unapproved, and is still not a
  launch chapter.** `/compare/qin` keeps its path: the owner is reviewing it by
  URL, and the path is the least user-visible place the harness vocabulary
  survives. Renaming it into a chapter namespace is an open presentation decision
  for the owner, not something this record takes on its own.
* **The artifact invariant follows the presentation.** `scripts/check-static-build.mjs`
  requires the root page to link both entries, to state the Qin chapter's
  unapproved preparation, and to state the Tang chapter's recorded owner approval,
  and it fails the build if `index.html` carries the exact framings the owner
  rejected — a review category, the comparison harness, the harness's `map
  treatments` phrase, or the contract id. `atlas.html` must still name the chapter
  it renders (now by display name) and must not carry a review category or the
  unapproved label; the Qin page must still carry its window and its unapproved
  status and must not label itself a comparison fixture. The bans are the rejected
  framings, not ordinary historical words: a paraphrase is a copy-review question,
  and the card test says the same.
* **The page still leads with the atlas.** The hero's `Open the atlas` call to
  action is unchanged and remains the first link in `main`; the chapter cards
  follow it with equal weight, so no chapter is set apart as the project's "real"
  one.

== Alternatives considered

* **Rename `/compare/qin` to a chapter path.** Deferred, not rejected: the owner
  is reviewing the current URL, the path is not shown as chapter identity, and the
  static file server that serves the review artifact does not read
  `static/_redirects`, so a rename would break the link the owner is using. This
  is recorded as an open presentation decision below.
* **Curate the names in the chapter content.** Rejected: the Tang set's canonical
  digest carries the owner's approval and the Qin set's digest is what its ledger
  row records, so editing either set to rename a chapter would invalidate state
  the owner has not re-decided. A display name is presentation, and presentation
  belongs on the browser side of the content boundary.
* **Keep the published story summary verbatim on the cards.** Rejected for the
  root page: it is written for the record's readers and describes the chapter's
  construction. The Tang chapter's own page metadata still carries it, where that
  provenance belongs.
* **Keep a `Reviewed chapter` badge on the atlas page.** Rejected: it is the same
  internal category the owner asked to drop, and the notice's review-independence
  line states what the publication records.
* **Keep the Qin card as the privileged review entry and add a second, equal
  card.** Rejected: the owner asked for peers, and a review card beside a chapter
  card is the framing the review rejected.

== Consequences

* A reader arriving at the site sees two chapters with names and stories, and the
  page's own categories are gone; the project's review vocabulary lives in the
  records and on the review page, where the status has to be stated.
* The Tang chapter keeps its recorded owner approval visible without the page
  reciting the frozen set's preparation notes; the previously quoted
  preparation-time notes are no longer shown, so their "approval pending" wording
  cannot stand as the current state anywhere a reader sees it.
* The Qin chapter is more discoverable than before — it is a peer card, not a
  subordinate review card — which raises the cost of a mistake in its status line.
  The tests and the artifact invariant hold that line to the ledger's words.
* Display names are code, not content: renaming a chapter does not require a new
  publication set or a new ledger row, and a chapter tree that has no card fails
  `chapters.test.ts` rather than shipping unnamed.

== Limitations recorded

* `/compare/qin` keeps the harness word in its path. The page presents as a
  chapter, but the URL does not; renaming it is an open owner decision.
* The Qin chapter is still unapproved preparation. Nothing here advances its
  approval, and RFD 4's AC2 still does not apply to it.
* The details panel still states the chapter publication's review independence
  beside a selected record (`Self-reviewed by ...: not owner review, not
  independent review, not specialist review`). That is the same kind of process
  prose the owner asked to remove from the cards, but it belongs to the production
  surface's approved presentation, so changing it is an open owner decision rather
  than a consequence of this record.
* The treatment comparison, the shared baseline, Wave 2, Gate 2, and the PoC
  remain open; this record changes presentation only.
* The cards' summaries are reviewed copy, not derived text: the tests check that a
  summary names a year from the chapter's own window and carries none of the
  rejected framings, and correctness of the prose itself stays a review question.
