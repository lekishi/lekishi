= RFD 2 Historical data, time, and evidence

== Revision note

Revised on 2026-10-10 on the owner's instruction, after the status review
found the content thinner than the free sources it cites and the authoring
workflow heavier than a single editor can sustain: 0 of 19 places with an
asserted historical coordinate, 13 of them anchored at modern city centres
while cited gazetteers hold the sites, no route or area anywhere, and every
correction requiring a new revision, a new publication set, a ledger row, and
a digest. This text replaces the earlier RFD 2. The dated records under this
directory, link:CONTRACTS.adoc[the v0 contract],
link:TANG_MISSION_FEASIBILITY.adoc[the feasibility report], and
link:PUBLICATION_APPROVAL.adoc[the publication approval gate], stay readable
as the evidence they are; where this text changes their effect it says so.

== Goal

Represent historical events and relationships with enough temporal,
geographic, and evidentiary context to support link:../0001/README.adoc[RFD 1]
without turning visual convenience into historical certainty, and at a volume
and pace that produces chapters worth exploring. Support one owner-editor
first and eventual contributions from researchers and other editors.

== Domain model

Places:: Stable identities with historical names, cited locations, geometry
  validity periods, and spatial precision. A changing name must not silently
  create a new place.
Actors:: People, states, communities, companies, religious bodies, and other
  institutions, with roles that vary over time. Cultures do not occupy
  exclusive, sharply bounded polygons.
Events:: Occurrences or processes with temporal bounds, participants and
  roles, locations, and membership in a campaign, mission, or sequence.
Relationships:: Typed connections among actors, places, and events: trade,
  travel, conflict, diplomacy, transmission, institutional change, or proposed
  influence, with explicit direction and validity where meaningful.
Claims and sources:: Assertions attached to citations with locators,
  provenance, review status, uncertainty, and supporting or conflicting
  interpretations.
Stories:: Curated sequences over the records, not copies of them.
Media:: Images and other assets with per-file rights, attribution, and a
  depiction label (contemporary depiction, later depiction, modern
  photograph of a site, modern reconstruction).

Portable identifiers, retained external identifiers, GeoJSON geometry with
temporal and evidentiary metadata, and no renderer objects in the source of
truth all stand as decided in link:CONTRACTS.adoc[the v0 contract].

== Decision: historical names with local-script forms

The period-appropriate historical name is the primary label, paired with its
local-script form where sources support it, with the romanization or
translation marked as editorial. Multiple attested forms may coexist. Modern
equivalents live in details and search. This decision stands unchanged.

== Time

Historical dates keep the source's expression, calendar, precision, and
uncertainty separately from the normalized values used to sort and filter.
Duration is distinct from an uncertain occurrence inside a range. BCE/CE,
year zero, interval boundaries, and ordering are specified in
link:CONTRACTS.adoc[the v0 contract] and pinned by the vectors under
`historical/v0/vectors`; they stand.

=== Decision: possible temporal matches are included by default

An event whose supported range overlaps the window is a visibly uncertain
possible match. Playback never invents an exact date or animates continuous
activity across an uncertainty range. Stands.

=== Decision: undated events remain discoverable outside temporal results

Undated events are reachable through search, connections, and a "Date
unknown" list; they are absent from time-filtered map results, stepping, and
playback, and are labelled unknown rather than outside the window. Stands.

== Location

=== Decision: cite the best available coordinate, and only then anchor

A place is placed at the best coordinate a citable source supports, in this
order of preference:

. A site coordinate for an excavated or standing site, from a cited gazetteer
  or dataset (Wikidata CC0, Pleiades CC BY, the World Historical Gazetteer
  under its dataset terms, CHGIS under its terms once the owner decides).
  The record names the dataset, the identifier, and the precision it states.
. A locality or region identification from cited scholarship or a historical
  geography, with the precision stated (`locality`, `region`).
. A display anchor at a modern namesake, used only when neither of the above
  exists, and stated as such in the record.

A display anchor is a last resort, not a default. The earlier practice of
anchoring palaces, temples, and ports at modern city centres while a cited
site exists is withdrawn: 黃泗浦 anchored at central Suzhou when Wikidata
holds the excavated Huangsipu site 60 km away is the case that decided this.
A place with no citable placement keeps `unknown` geometry and stays out of
the map.

=== Decision: routes and areas are published with their interpretation

Movement and control are drawn, with the interpretation recorded on the
geometry and shown in the visual grammar RFD 1 defines:

* `schematic_link`: a line through the dated places a record attests, asserting
  no path between them. A journey with dated stops publishes one.
* `reconstructed_route`: a route proposed in cited scholarship, attributed to
  its author.
* `attested_itinerary`: a path the source itself describes stage by stage.
* A dated control snapshot: a polygon with its date and source, drawn as a
  snapshot and never interpolated. Political control, religious presence,
  and cultural influence remain different relationships and never share one
  territorial meaning.

The atlas domain accepts all four. The earlier projection rule that rejected
every route interpretation as an error is withdrawn. A chapter that draws
nothing it could draw with a label is below RFD 1's bar.

=== Decision: readable, explicitly approximate locations

Approximate points and source-supported areas appear on the map with
non-colour cues and an explanation in details. No invented radius or
boundary. Stands, with the precision now carried by the marker's size rather
than by a text badge.

== Claims drive visualization

Documented contact does not establish influence; a network does not prove a
voyage; a council does not cause a uniform division. Competing accounts are
stored without averaging. Where location or date is unknown the record stays
in lists and cards. Stands.

=== Decision: first-release interpretation policy

The release includes directly attested claims and interpretations advanced in
cited scholarship, distinguished and attributed. Every relationship, route,
and snapshot carries a cited basis. Original contributor hypotheses stay out
of public content. Stands.

=== Decision: an attributed main account with cited alternatives

One main account, visibly marked when disputed, with the alternatives and
their citations in the card. Stands.

== Acquisition

=== Decision: default to sources whose terms allow commercial use

Decided by the owner on 2026-10-10. A dataset, text transcription, or media
source is adopted by default only when its terms allow commercial use with
attribution: public domain, CC0, CC BY, ODC-By, or government standard terms
compatible with CC BY. A source with a non-commercial or share-alike term, or
with terms the project has not read, is not adopted by default; it needs a
separate owner decision per source, and anything taken from it must stay
removable, which is why every coordinate, identification, quotation, and
image records its dataset of origin. The rule keeps the project free to
decide its own future rather than inheriting a constraint from a dataset.

Quoting a public-domain text remains quoting the work, whatever the terms of
the transcription it was read in: the record cites the work and the edition,
notes the transcription, and re-checks the locator against a critical edition
before release, as the existing chapters do.

=== Decision: media is content

Public-domain and openly licensed images are cleared per file and published
with attribution and a depiction label. The 東征伝絵巻 scenes and Commons
photographs of 唐招提寺, the Daming Palace site, and 平城宮跡 are the first
set. A later depiction is labelled as such and is never evidence.

Sources remain primary texts in public-domain editions, cited scholarship,
and the datasets above, each with recorded reuse terms. Owning a book does
not grant reproduction rights; cite and summarize.

== Publication

=== Decision: a single-editor workflow until there is a second editor

The owner is the editor and may approve his own sourced work for the public
release (unchanged from the earlier decision). The workflow is sized for
that, in a form that later contributors can grow into (next section):

* *Authoring format.* Approved by the owner on 2026-10-10. A folder per
  chapter with one small file per record (`events/akimeya-landfall-753.yaml`,
  about ten lines), for places, actors, events, relationships, stories,
  sources, and media, with citations inline, in the vocabulary
  link:CONTRACTS.adoc[the v0 contract] defines. A build-time compiler
  concatenates the folder into the contract documents the atlas and the
  contract tests consume, so the contract's semantics and vectors stand
  unchanged while the authoring cost per fact falls from a hundred lines to a
  few. One file per record, with flat fields of simple types, is what a web
  form, a scholar, and two concurrent contributors can all write; a record's
  history is its file's history.
* *Revision history.* Git history, with the commit as the change explanation.
  Readers get a correction history from it when the atlas grows a history
  surface; nothing requires per-record revision envelopes or hashes for one
  editor.
* *Approval.* One `published` decision per chapter, recorded by the owner
  with the commit it approves, enforced by the build: a served chapter must
  carry the owner's approval of its current content.
* *Review.* The owner's review checklist (source support, attribution,
  uncertainty, attestation versus interpretation, reuse rights) is run once
  per chapter release and once per batch of corrections, not per edit. The
  critical-edition re-check of locators cited from unproofread transcriptions
  is one pass before a release.

This changes the effect of link:PUBLICATION_APPROVAL.adoc[the publication
approval gate]: the per-set digest ledger governs the chapters until the
authoring format lands, and is then replaced by the per-chapter approval
above. The ledger and the sets stay in the tree as the record of how the
first two chapters were approved; `publication-approval.test.ts` is retired
with them.

=== Decision: stable records with reviewed revisions

Records keep stable identities across corrections. The current published
content is what the chapter's approved commit contains. Corrections are
distinct from scholarly disagreement, which stays in the current content under
the main-account policy. Stands in substance; the mechanism is the one above.

Contributors propose changes to the record files; publication requires the
owner's approval. Broader delegation is defined when a second editor exists.
AI may assist extraction and checking; its output is a candidate with a
source, never an approval.

=== Decision: standardize the content model, grow the contribution surface

Decided by the owner on 2026-10-10, for contributors who may be scholars and
experts rather than engineers. What is standardized is the content model and
the approval contract, because they survive every stage; the way a
contribution arrives is expected to change, and Git stays the storage and
history layer underneath for the foreseeable future without contributors
having to see it.

The standard:

* The record shape: stable ids; claims with citations; attested versus
  interpretation; disputes as sibling claims in one record, each with its
  citation, the main account marked as an editorial choice; name forms with
  language and script; sources with reuse terms; `contributed_by` and
  `reviewed_by` on each claim, shown as credit on the record's card.
* The approval contract: nothing reaches the site without the editor's
  approval of a specific version, and a pending contribution never changes
  what is published.
* The roles: contributor, specialist reviewer, editor. A specialist's review
  is recorded as the reviewer on the chapter's approval line, which is what
  the earlier `specialist_review` flag meant.
* The checks CI runs on every proposed change, so review is about history and
  not syntax: schema validity, every id resolving, every drawn geometry with
  a cited basis, every name form with language and script, every source with
  terms, and no change to an approved chapter reaching the build without a
  new approval.

The contribution surface, in stages, each entered when the previous one is
felt to be short:

. *Now.* The owner edits record files, with AI assistance drafting entries
  from sources. A scholar contributes as to any editor, in a document or a
  message, and is transcribed with credit.
. *First regular contributors.* A structured proposal form (a GitHub issue
  form with fields for the claim, the source, and the locator) that needs a
  GitHub account and shows no file; each proposal becomes a record through
  review.
. *Several contributors.* A Git-backed editing site: a web form per record
  kind that saves to the repository, with draft, review, and publish states
  mapped to branches and the editor's approval, and a review queue per
  chapter for its specialist. This needs one small piece of infrastructure,
  an authentication proxy, which is a separate owner decision under the
  no-provider rule.
. *Many editors or real query needs.* The database-backed editorial
  application link:../0003/README.adoc[RFD 3] reserves, importing from the
  files, with Git kept as the publication record. Only when demonstrated.

== Alternatives and trade-offs

Keeping the per-record revision and set machinery would preserve a
multi-editor publication model that has no second editor and that made
thirty-four events a three-week job. A database now would add migration work
without a demonstrated query need. The compact format with Git history keeps
every distinction the contract makes and removes the ceremony.
