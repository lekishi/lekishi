the Internet Archive were not reachable from this orb, so works hosted there
were read through Europe PMC, OpenAlex, Crossref or RePEc.

== What the sources support

The Americas before 100 CE are well covered by archaeology and almost not at
all by text. The only dated writing is the Long Count of the last century BCE.
So every date in the chapter comes from radiocarbon or luminescence ages, from
stylistic dating, or from a Long Count reading with its correlation, and every
place is a site.

=== Rights

The commercial-use rule leaves few quotable sources for this period. National
Park Service pages are public domain. Three journal articles are CC BY 4.0
(Dillehay 2015 and Sugiyama 2021 in PLOS ONE, Hansen 2022 in _Ancient
Mesoamerica_). Everything else is cited only: _Science_, _Nature_ and _PNAS_
articles under publisher copyright, four articles under CC BY-NC or CC BY-NC-ND,
conference proceedings and society papers without a stated licence, the Poverty
Point nomination, and an INAH museum guide whose site terms were not read.

Being cite-only costs the chapter little, because a date and a place are
facts, which the claim states in its own words with a locator.

=== Weak points

* *One secondary source carries nine events.* The 2013 Poverty Point World
  Heritage nomination dates Poverty Point itself and, in its comparative
  analysis, Watson Brake, Indian Knoll, Horr's Island, Real Alto, Caral, San
  Lorenzo, Grave Creek and Nasca. Each of those should be checked against a
  primary site report before release.
* *Most journal sources were read as abstracts.* The claims say only what the
  abstract says.
* *Long Count readings depend on a correlation.* Chiapa de Corzo Stela 2 (36 BCE)
  and Tres Zapotes Stela C (32 BCE) follow the correlation their sources use.
  The INAH guide gives Stela C as 32 CE.

== Disputes

Four events carry a dispute with a conflicting claim: the Poverty Point
earthworks (nomination against the NPS Spanish page), the Cascajal block (date
and authenticity), Serpent Mound (Adena against Fort Ancient) and Tres Zapotes
Stela C (32 BCE against 32 CE). The chapter notes give each one's sources.

Serpent Mound is an editorial choice the owner should check. The draft takes
the Adena date as the main claim, which puts the mound inside the chapter; the
fuller source read (Lepper 2020) favors the Fort Ancient date, after 100 CE.

== What the format cannot yet say

Writing these records found seven gaps. Each record's notes say how it worked
around the gap.

[[gap-approximate]]
=== 1. "About" dates, centuries, and open bounds (notation only)

Nearly every date in the chapter is approximate: "about 14,500 cal BP", "ca.
1000 BC", "the first century BCE", "after about 3400 BP". Contract v0 can store
this: `endpoint_certainty` is `exact`, `approximate` or `bounded`, `precision`
can be `decade` or `century`, and `bounds` can be `lower_only` or `upper_only`
(link:CONTRACTS.adoc[CONTRACTS], "Explicit endpoint semantics"). The
link:AUTHORING_FORMAT.adoc[date notation] cannot write any of them. The draft
writes exact converted years and says in the notes that they are approximate.

Proposal (decision card 1): extend the notation, with no contract change. For
example `~1000 BCE` for an approximate endpoint, `1st c. BCE` or `100 BCE..1 BCE
precision: century` for a century, and `after 1451 BCE` for a lower bound.

[[gap-bp]]
=== 2. Years before present

Most early dates are written by their sources as BP (before 1950) or as "years
ago". The draft converts BP to a civil year by hand: N BP is N − 1949 BCE. "Years
ago" names no epoch and does not say whether an age is calibrated, so the draft
does not convert it on its own: it is read as cal BP only where the source shows
that (Huaca Prieta), and otherwise the date is taken from a calendar or cal BP age
that a cited source gives (Hopewell, Serpent Mound). The BP
conversion is easy to get wrong by a year, and the authored year hides the
source's precision. A notation such as `3400 BP` that the compiler converts
would remove the arithmetic, at the cost of one more form to learn. This is
offered as an option on decision card 1.

[[gap-long-count]]
=== 3. No Mesoamerican calendar (contract v1)

The `calendar` enum has no Long Count. The draft records Chiapa de Corzo Stela 2
and Tres Zapotes Stela C as `julian` years with the Long Count and Calendar
Round in `as_written` (`7.16.3.2.13 6 Ben 16 Xul`, `7.16.6.16.18 6 Etznab 1 Uo`).
That loses the fact that the year depends on a correlation constant. Neither
source for Stela C names the constant it uses, and the reason they differ (32 BCE
against 32 CE) is not established; recording the correlation would at least say
which one a date assumes.

Proposal (decision card 2): add `mesoamerican_long_count` to the calendar enum
in contract v1, with a required `correlation` field (for example GMT 584283 or
584285) whenever the date is converted to a Julian year.

[[gap-radiocarbon]]
=== 4. Uncalibrated radiocarbon years

Two sources give uncalibrated radiocarbon years (Paso de la Amada's ball court,
"about 1400 BC"; the Fort Ancient charcoal dates for Serpent Mound, 920 ± 70 BP).
They are not calendar years, and marking them `calendar: unknown` does not keep
them off the timeline: the projection plots a date's year whatever its calendar.
The draft therefore never dates an event from an uncalibrated age. The ball court
takes the calibrated 1650 BCE that Blomster and Salazar Chávez (2020) give, with
the uncalibrated figure as a second citation, and the Fort Ancient dates appear
only in the Serpent Mound dispute. A radiocarbon calendar would need calibration,
which the atlas should not do. Recorded here, not proposed.

[[gap-dispute-date]]
=== 5. A dispute's alternative date is prose only

A dispute holds a summary and a conflicting claim, but the conflicting date is
only in words. The timeline cannot show the other reading of Poverty Point,
Serpent Mound or Stela C. A machine-readable date on a conflicting claim would
let the reader see both. This touches the dispute model for every chapter, so
it belongs with contract v1's other proposals.

[[gap-place-type]]
=== 6. No archaeological site or earthwork place type

Eleven places are typed `other` with a note, because the place vocabulary has no
type for an archaeological site, a mound or an earthwork, or a rock shelter. A
`site` or `archaeological_site` type would cover all of them, and would serve the
other chapters before 100 CE too.

[[gap-scale]]
=== 7. Deep time and placeless events (engine)

These are not format gaps, but the chapter is built to test them:

* The story spans 21051 BCE to 550 CE, about 21,600 years, while the densest part
  is 2700 BCE to 100 CE. The timeline needs a scale that shows both.
* The chapter window must hold every step that is not labelled a coda, so the
  window opens on all 21,000 years. There is no way to open on the dense stretch
  and label the earliest sites as a prelude, the counterpart of a coda.
* The contract allows a relationship between two events, but the atlas projects
  only relationships between actors and places. The San Lorenzo
  `proposed_influence` record is therefore kept out of the story until the atlas
  can show it.
* Spans run from three months (Mound A, inside a 300-year window) to 4,000 years
  (Monte Verde's early horizons).
* The Clovis event has no place, and two places (Xihuatoxtla, Horr's Island) have
  no position.
* Six events overlap 300–200 BCE, and several long spans (El Mirador, Nasca,
  Teotihuacan) overlap most of the later steps, so the reader's step list and map
  must show many overlapping spans at once.
